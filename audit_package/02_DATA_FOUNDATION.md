# Data foundation for the Nile Delta water allocation model

Compiled 14 November 2026 from three parallel research passes covering Egyptian agricultural statistics, prices and costs, and Nile hydrology with crop water requirements. Access date for every source cited below: 14 November 2026, unless the entry itself states an earlier pull date.

The rule of this document: every parameter of the model is either backed by a real, named, dated source recorded here, or it is listed in the gap section at the end in plain words. Nothing in between. Where a number is my arithmetic on sourced values rather than a published value, the text says so and shows the operation.

Conversions used throughout, stated once here:
* 1 feddan equals 0.42 hectares. A value per feddan becomes a value per hectare when divided by 0.42. An area in feddan becomes an area in hectares when multiplied by 0.42.
* 1 millimetre of water over 1 hectare equals 10 cubic metres, so millimetres become cubic metres per hectare when multiplied by 10.
* 1 ardab of wheat equals 150 kilograms, so a price per ardab becomes a price per tonne when divided by 0.15.
* 1 cubic kilometre equals 1 billion cubic metres.

Web addresses in the source lines are identifiers copied exactly as published, not prose. Full research detail, including every route tried and every dead end, sits in the three notes files in the data/external folder: research_notes_capmas.md, research_notes_prices.md and research_notes_hydrology.md. Every dataset sits beside them as a CSV in the same folder.

## Section 1. National crop yields and harvested areas

Status: real before this rebuild, unchanged by it.

* Value used by the model: Egypt mean yields and harvested areas for wheat, rice, maize, sugar beet and potatoes, averaged over 2014 to 2023, which also set the national crop mix of the model baseline pattern.
* Source: FAOSTAT, Crops and Livestock Products domain, as republished by the Our World in Data grapher mirror. Publisher of the underlying data: Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations.
* Route and date: pulled 12 November 2026 through the mirror, because the FAOSTAT site itself failed that day with server error 521 on the API and 403 on the bulk download host.
* Unit: yields in tonnes per hectare, areas in hectares, production in tonnes, exactly as served in the mirror files held in data/raw.
* Outstanding: a direct FAOSTAT pull of the crops domain is still owed to replace the mirror. Section 2 proves the bulk download route now works for the prices domain from the same host that returned 403 in November. After this rebuild the mirror is the weakest provenance left in the foundation, and it carries the model baseline yields, areas and national crop mix, so closing it is the highest value single pull that remains.

## Section 2. Crop producer prices

Status: now real, 1991 to 2023, with stated holes. Before this rebuild, four of the five model prices were invented values.

* Source: FAOSTAT, Prices domain, producer prices. Publisher: Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations.
* Route that carried it: the bulk download file Prices_E_All_Data_(Normalized).zip from bulks-faostat.fao.org, file dated 9 October 2026 inside the archive, archive size 11,684,078 bytes as downloaded, downloaded in full and filtered to Egypt. The FAOSTAT API at fenixservices.fao.org was tried first on 14 November 2026 and returned server error 521 on both its definitions and data endpoints. The Humanitarian Data Exchange mirror of the same FAOSTAT file was downloaded and compared value by value against the bulk original for all 318 delivered annual rows: zero differences. The mirror file itself was not retained in the delivered folder, so that comparison is recorded here and cannot be replayed from the delivered files. The bulk original is the cited route.
* Years covered: 1991 to 2023 for every crop. Rice and sugar beet have no annual value for 2019, 2020 or 2021 in this vintage. No annual value for 2024 or 2025 exists for any of the five crops in this vintage. Monthly values in pounds exist in the same file for January to December 2024 for wheat, rice, maize and potatoes and were deliberately not averaged, because an average built by the researcher would be a constructed number.
* Units: Egyptian pounds per tonne and US dollars per tonne, both delivered. Item codes in the source: wheat 15, rice 27, maize 56, sugar beet 157, potatoes 116.
* Rice basis: the source item named Rice, code 27, is paddy rice. The FAO commodity definition for code 0027, titled Rice Paddy, describes grain after threshing and winnowing, rice in the husk. The series therefore matches the paddy price the model needs. It is not a milled rice price.
* Latest published values, 2023, pounds per tonne, with 2022 in brackets: wheat 10,000 (5,900); rice, paddy, 18,761 (14,955); maize 14,400; sugar beet 795 (795); potatoes 8,160. The full annual series for both currency elements sits in faostat_producer_prices_egypt.csv.
* Cross validation: the CAPMAS income bulletin described in Section 4 prints farm prices sourced from the Ministry of Agriculture Economic Affairs Sector, and its values match this FAOSTAT series exactly at every overlapping point checked, for example wheat 3,770 pounds per tonne in 2017 in both.
* Against the invented model values the model carried into this rebuild: rice 16,000 invented against 18,761 real for 2023; maize 9,500 invented against 14,400 real; potatoes 7,000 invented against 8,160 real. The invented values were all low, and maize was low by about a third.
* International cross check, sanity only: World Bank Commodities Price Data, the Pink Sheet, annual averages, editions dated 3 November 2026 and 3 September 2025, in pink_sheet_crosscheck.csv. These are international reference prices in dollars per tonne, not Egyptian farm gate prices. Converted at the 2023 rate implied by the FAOSTAT Egypt pair, 30.63 pounds per dollar, the international wheat level sits about 4.3 percent above the Egypt farm gate level, the Egypt paddy rice level sits about 10.6 percent above the converted world milled benchmark, and the Egypt maize level sits about 86 percent above the converted international level. The rice and maize gaps are flagged, not adjusted.

## Section 3. Procurement prices, the 2025 anchors

Status: real, and the only honest price anchors at 2025 levels.

* Wheat: CONFIRMED. The model figure of 2,200 pounds per ardab of 150 kilograms, equal to 14,667 pounds per tonne, is correct for the 2025 harvest season. The price was set by Cabinet decision of 2 October 2024 and announced by the Ministry of Supply and Internal Trade before the season opened; Ministerial Decree 46 of 2025 formalised it in Al Waqaia Al Masriya, the official gazette, issue 88, on 19 April 2025, four days after the season start, and the ministry announcement on its own site, April 2025, gives 2,200 pounds per ardab at cleanliness grade 23.5 carats, 2,150 at grade 23 and 2,100 at grade 22.5, for the season 15 April to 15 August 2025. FAO GIEWS country brief of 6 June 2025 and USDA Foreign Agricultural Service Cairo report EG2025 to 0009 of 27 March 2025 agree.
* Wheat, neighbouring seasons, same source family: the 2024 harvest price was 2,000 pounds per ardab, set 14 March 2024, equal to 13,333 pounds per tonne. For the 2026 crop, FAO GIEWS of 1 December 2025 records an announced 2,350 pounds per ardab from late October 2025, and FAO forecast reporting republished in July 2026 records a later figure of 2,500 pounds per ardab. Both 2026 figures are filed with their dates in procurement_prices_egypt.csv. They are announcements at different points of a season, not a resolved contradiction.
* Sugar beet, factory procurement: base price 2,400 pounds per tonne of clean beet at 16 percent sugar content, before bonuses, for the 2024 to 2025 season, announced by the Sugar and Integrated Industries Company for its Abu Qurqas factories with deliveries from 4 March 2025, with Cabinet price reporting for the same season in agreement. For the 2025 to 2026 season the base price was cut to 2,000 pounds per tonne by Cabinet decision.
* Sugar beet, the trap this rebuild exposes: the FAOSTAT farm gate series of Section 2 gives 795 pounds per tonne for sugar beet in 2022 and 2023, while the announced factory procurement price for the 2025 season is 2,400 pounds per tonne. The two series measure different things and differ by a factor of three. The invented model value of 2,500 sat near the procurement concept by luck. The model must state which beet price concept it uses and why; the data cannot make that choice for it.
* Supporting company evidence, labelled arithmetic: the Delta Sugar Company board report for the financial year ending 31 December 2025 states 2,653,108 tonnes of beet supplied in the 2025 season and 8,242 million pounds paid, which divides to an average outlay of about 3,107 pounds per supplied tonne including bonuses. That figure is division performed on company totals, not a price the company states.

## Section 4. Production costs

Status: real for 2015 to 2016 and 2016 to 2017. A gap at recent levels for every crop.

* Source: Central Agency for Public Mobilization and Statistics, Annual Bulletin of Estimates of Income from the Agricultural Sector. The bulletin names its data source as the Ministry of Agriculture and Land Reclamation, Economic Affairs Sector, which is the ministry cost study system the model design asked for, reached through its CAPMAS republication. Tables 15, 16 and 17 of each edition give total revenue, cost and net return per feddan by crop. Footnote 1 to those tables states that the cost includes rent. The arithmetic of every delivered row was checked during research: revenue minus cost equals the printed net return, exactly. The revenue and net return columns were not carried into the delivered CSV, so that check is recorded here and cannot be replayed from the delivered files.
* Editions read in full: 2015 to 2016 and 2016 to 2017. Later editions exist, including a 2023 to 2024 edition issued 30 November 2025, but its public release carries only national totals and its per crop cost tables were not publicly retrievable. Provenance caution added after the skeptic pass: the censusinfo download address for the 2016 to 2017 edition could not be reopened during that pass, the fetch failing without a returned status, so the provenance of this section rests on the research pass record until the address is reopened in a full browser.
* Unit as published: pounds per feddan, one rent inclusive total per crop, no component breakdown. Converted unit: pounds per hectare, by division by 0.42. Both figures sit in production_costs_egypt.csv.
* Values for 2016 to 2017, pounds per feddan as published, then pounds per hectare converted: wheat 9,019, then 21,473.81; sugar beet 7,395, then 17,607.14; rice 8,359, then 19,902.38; maize, summer, 7,952, then 18,933.33; maize, Nili, 5,897, then 14,040.48; potatoes, summer, 20,804, then 49,533.33; potatoes, Nili, 11,827, then 28,159.52. The 2015 to 2016 values sit in the same CSV.
* One later wheat anchor: a government report republished 30 May 2024 states wheat cost at about 17,000 pounds per feddan in 2022 to 2023, equal to 40,476.19 pounds per hectare, with land rent of about 4,700 pounds per feddan, about 27.7 percent of the total, and labour, machinery services and chemical fertiliser together at about 54 percent; and states an expected cost of about 22,000 pounds per feddan for 2023 to 2024, equal to 52,380.95 pounds per hectare. The second figure is a forecast inside that report and is flagged as a forecast in the CSV.
* Against the invented model costs per hectare (wheat 30,000; rice 42,000; maize 32,000; sugar beet 36,000; potatoes 95,000): the official 2016 to 2017 levels are far below every invented value, while the 2022 to 2023 wheat anchor of 40,476 sits above the invented wheat figure. Egyptian farm costs rose steeply across the devaluation years between these two observations, and no public official series found in this rebuild bridges them for rice, maize, sugar beet or potatoes.

## Section 5. District land base

Status: now real for 2020 to 2021 and partly for 2019 to 2020, from the Ministry of Agriculture bulletins that supply the CAPMAS series; no CAPMAS branded governorate table was obtained for any year from 2019 to 2024. Before this rebuild, all eight district land figures were invented. One naming warning: the four governorate CSV files in the external folder carry capmas at the start of their file names, kept for continuity with the research notes, but the data inside them is Ministry bulletin data, exactly as the provenance paragraph below states. A reader who opens only the files should read this warning first.

* Provenance, stated exactly: no CAPMAS branded governorate table for any year from 2019 to 2024 could be obtained as an open CAPMAS file. What was obtained in full is the underlying official series: the Agricultural Statistics Bulletins of the Economic Affairs Sector of the Ministry of Agriculture and Land Reclamation, openly posted at agri.gov.eg. CAPMAS states in its own bulletin methodology that its crop area and plant production data are collected from the Ministry of Agriculture, and the Ministry tables carry the same governorate structure of old land, new land and total that CAPMAS republishes. The panel below is therefore first hand official data, and it is not CAPMAS branded output. Both halves of that sentence matter.
* Source for the land base: Ministry Agricultural Statistics Bulletin Part 2, agricultural year 2020 to 2021, Table 19 for total cropped area by governorate and Table 17 for cultivated area by governorate; and Part 2 of 2019 to 2020, Table 19, for total cropped area in that year.
* Units as published: feddan. Converted: hectares, by multiplication by 0.42. Both sit in capmas_governorate_total_cropped_area.csv.
* The two measures are different concepts and must not be mixed. Total cropped area counts every crop season in the year, so one physical feddan planted twice counts twice. Cultivated area counts the physical land inside the agricultural boundary once.
* Values for 2020 to 2021, hectares converted, total cropped area first, then cultivated area: Beheira 720,839 and 387,541; Sharkia 679,538 and 375,274; Dakahlia 533,501 and 271,077; Kafr El Sheikh 450,195 and 228,118; Gharbia 308,142 and 159,439; Menoufia 269,172 and 141,087; Qalyubia 119,480 and 66,784; Damietta 95,608 and 47,260. Totals across the eight governorates: total cropped area 3,176,474 hectares; cultivated area 1,676,580 hectares.
* Against the invented model land base, which totals 1,900,000 hectares: the invented total sits between the two real concepts, and the invented distribution is wrong in a way the total hides. The invented figures for Qalyubia, 150,000 hectares, and Damietta, 120,000 hectares, exceed even the total cropped area of those governorates, 119,480 and 95,608 hectares, so the invented base is impossible for those two governorates under either land concept. At the other end, the invented Beheira figure of 380,000 hectares matches its cultivated area of 387,541 almost exactly while its total cropped area is 720,839.
* Modelling note the foundation cannot settle: the model land constraint binds the sum of winter and summer plantings in one pool, which matches neither published concept exactly, because a physical hectare can carry a winter crop and then a summer crop while the model as built makes them compete. Which concept the rebuilt model adopts is a modelling decision. Both series are delivered so the decision can be made openly.
* Rejected candidate, recorded so it is not rediscovered as a shortcut: a 2026 conference paper by a CAPMAS author, labelled Source: CAPMAS, 2024, prints total cropped area and rice area by governorate, but states no agricultural year, and its printed national total cropped area of 12,418,324 feddan contradicts the CAPMAS national figure of 16.8 million feddan for 2022 to 2023. It is saved in isolation in capmas_secondhand_isi_cropped_and_rice_area.csv with its warning, and it is not used anywhere in this foundation.

## Section 6. District cropping pattern

Status: now real for agricultural year 2020 to 2021 as a complete panel, with partial panels for 2019 to 2020 and 2023 to 2024. Before this rebuild, the model applied the national crop mix to every district.

* Sources, all Ministry of Agriculture Agricultural Statistics Bulletins, Economic Affairs Sector, first hand, published in feddan and converted at 0.42: Part 1, Winter Crops, 2020 to 2021, Table 2 for wheat and Table 12 for sugar beet; Part 2, Summer and Nili Crops, 2020 to 2021, Table 20 for rice, Table 22 for summer maize with white and yellow combined, Table 78 for potatoes across all seasons with the season split in Tables 79 to 81; Part 1 of 2023 to 2024 for wheat, sugar beet and winter potatoes; Part 2 of 2019 to 2020 for rice, summer maize, potatoes across all seasons and total cropped area.
* Files: capmas_governorate_area.csv, capmas_governorate_production.csv and capmas_governorate_yield.csv, 176 rows each, every row carrying its source identifier, table number, evidence grade and data status.
* The pattern in words, shares within the five model crops for 2020 to 2021, computed by me from the delivered areas and labelled as arithmetic: the Delta is not one pattern. Wheat takes 44.0 percent of the five crop area in Beheira and 41.9 percent in Sharkia, but rice takes 49.0 percent in Damietta and 37.2 percent in Dakahlia, maize takes 55.1 percent in Qalyubia and 51.6 percent in Menoufia, and sugar beet takes 21.0 percent in Kafr El Sheikh against 0.3 percent in Qalyubia. A single national mix applied to all eight districts misstates every one of them.
* Holes inside the complete panel, recorded in the CSVs as not reported in source table, never as zero: the 2020 to 2021 rice table carries no row for Menoufia at all; Nili potatoes are reported for only three of the eight governorates; and Nili maize by governorate sits in Tables 27 to 31 of the same Part 2 bulletin, located but not extracted in this pass, with national Nili maize area of 196,372 feddan equal to about 9 percent on top of the national summer maize area, so every saved maize figure is summer maize only and understates the full year.
* Partial years: for 2023 to 2024 only the winter season exists here, so rice, maize, summer potatoes and total cropped area by governorate for that year are missing. For 2019 to 2020 only the summer season exists here, so wheat, sugar beet and the seasonal potato split for that year are missing. For 2021 to 2022 and 2022 to 2023 the only governorate figures obtained are second hand, for Menoufia wheat and maize only, from a 2025 Human and City Studies paper quoting the CAPMAS bulletins, with its yields computed by the researcher and flagged as computed. For 2024 to 2025 nothing at governorate level was obtained from any source reached. One caution on the research notes layer beneath this section: the national summer maize production totals printed in research_notes_capmas.md, 7,447,935 tons for 2020 to 2021 and 7,102,273 tons for 2019 to 2020, each sit one ton away from the sum of their white and yellow components as rendered from the bulletin PDFs, whose text layers garble digits in places. Neither total is used anywhere in this foundation, and the flag is recorded so the notes are not quoted on those two numbers without a recheck against the printed pages.

Wheat: 6.84, 7.34, 6.59, 6.31, 6.88, 7.04, 6.79, 6.57. Rice: 8.87, 8.38, 9.56, 9.69, 8.77, not reported, 7.59, 8.61. Maize, summer: 8.80, 8.47, 9.43, 9.20, 8.64, 8.94, 8.29, 8.51. Sugar beet: 44.34, 51.45, 52.60, 45.74, 60.24, 50.76, 48.81, 59.75. Potatoes, all seasons: 27.08, 31.14, 29.04, 36.95, 30.32, 25.77, 42.70, 23.86.
* Implied multipliers, my arithmetic, defined as each governorate yield divided by the production weighted mean yield of the eight governorates for the same crop and year: wheat spans 0.92 to 1.07 around a weighted mean of 6.87 tonnes per hectare; rice spans 0.83 to 1.06 around 9.14; maize spans 0.94 to 1.07 around 8.79; sugar beet spans 0.90 to 1.22 around 49.37; potatoes span 0.84 to 1.50 around 28.39. The full multiplier table is reproducible from the CSVs.
* Two findings the invented multipliers got wrong. First, multipliers are crop specific in reality, often in opposite directions inside one governorate: Kafr El Sheikh carries the lowest wheat multiplier of the eight at 0.92 and the highest rice multiplier at 1.06, so the invented single value of 0.90 for that governorate, justified in the old code by northern Delta soil salinity, is contradicted for rice by the official table itself. Second, the real spread for potatoes, 0.84 to 1.50, is far wider than the invented spread of 0.90 to 1.05 applied to every crop, so the old water value ranking across districts, which the audit showed restated the invented multipliers rank for rank, has no surviving basis.
* Winter yields for 2023 to 2024 are also delivered in the same CSV: wheat spans 6.29 tonnes per hectare in Kafr El Sheikh to 7.84 in Menoufia, with sugar beet and winter potatoes by governorate alongside.

## Section 8. Crop water requirements

Status: real values from four named sources, on two different bases that must not be blended. Before this rebuild, all five model values were invented midpoints. None of the five invented values matches any single named source below.

* Source one, the Delta standard on the net basis: Gabr, Mohamed El Sayed (2022), Management of irrigation requirements using FAO CROPWAT 8.0 model, a case study of Egypt, Modeling Earth Systems and Environment, volume 8, pages 3127 to 3142. Table 6, Delta territory, weather from 31 stations over 1985 to 2018. Values are net irrigation requirement, defined as crop evapotranspiration minus effective rain. Net requirement is not applied water. Delta values in cubic metres per hectare, net first, then evapotranspiration: wheat 1,272 and 1,782; rice 11,441 and 11,441; maize 5,762 and 5,762; sugar beet 3,927 and 4,192; potatoes 2,448 and 3,184.
* Flag on the Gabr wheat row, unsoftened: the Delta wheat evapotranspiration of 1,782 cubic metres per hectare is internally inconsistent with the same paper territory tables for the Canal area, Alexandria and Greater Cairo, which print wheat evapotranspiration of 2,217 to 2,572, and with the west Delta CROPWAT value of 4,904 below. No erratum was found. The row is delivered as published and flagged, and it should not be used for Delta wheat until the inconsistency is resolved.
* Source two, basis unstated in the source and treated as applied in this foundation: Abdelwahab and coauthors (2024), Acta Scientiarum Polonorum Formatio Circumiectus, volume 23, issue 4, Table 3, printing water requirements sourced from CAPMAS (2017) for the Ismailia Canal area of the East Nile Delta. The paper labels the figures water requirement without stating net or gross; they behave as applied requirements and are recorded here exactly as labelled in the paper. Values as published in cubic metres per feddan, then converted per hectare: wheat, winter, 2,153, then 5,126; rice, summer, 6,972, then 16,600; maize, summer, 3,908, then 9,305, with the Nili season at 3,122, then 7,433; sugar beet, winter, 3,959, then 9,426; potatoes, winter, 1,713, then 4,079, with the summer listing at 2,581, then 6,145. These are a second hand printing of an official table that could not be read in its original form in this pass.
* Source three, official duty tables: Sallam and Abd El Nasser (2006), Second International Conference on Water Resources and Arid Environment, Table 1, printing the crop water duty tables of the Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation (2003) by region. The region used here is Lower Egypt, an administrative duty region broader than the eight model governorates, so its values are the closest regional duties to the Delta and not measurements for the eight governorates themselves. Values per feddan, then per hectare: wheat 1,464, then 3,486; rice 5,521, then 13,145. The table covers wheat, rice and cotton only, so maize, sugar beet and potatoes have no value from this source. Also a second hand printing of an official table.
* Source four, a check on evapotranspiration: a soil mapping study of newly reclaimed areas west of the Nile Delta, Australian Journal of Basic and Applied Sciences, volume 2, issue 1 (2008), pages 165 to 173, CROPWAT 4.2 with the Penman Monteith method, in cubic metres per hectare: wheat 4,904; sugar beet about 5,567; maize 6,685. Sandy reclaimed land west of the Delta, not old Delta clay, so these are a check and not Delta values. No rice or potatoes values were printed in the parts read.
* Verdicts against the invented model values, crop by crop. Wheat, invented 4,200: inside the published span of 1,272 net to 5,126 applied, matching none; the two applied basis figures bracket it at 3,486 and 5,126. Rice, invented 12,000: between the net figure of 11,441 and the applied figures of 13,145 and 16,600, matching none. Maize, invented 5,800: almost exactly the Gabr net figure of 5,762, which means the model has been treating a net requirement as applied water for this crop, understating applied water by roughly the efficiency gap below. Sugar beet, invented 5,200: between evapotranspiration of 4,192 and the applied figure of 9,426, matching neither, and the applied to net disagreement for this crop is the widest of the five and unresolved. Potatoes, invented 4,800: between the winter applied figure of 4,079 and the summer applied figure of 6,145, matching neither, and for this crop the season must be named before any single value is defensible.
* The efficiency anchor that governs any net to applied conversion: Gabr (2022) prints a water balance, sourced inside that paper to MWRI (2017) and CAPMAS (2018), of measured gross irrigation Nile freshwater of 42 billion cubic metres per year against a computed total net irrigation requirement of about 21 billion cubic metres per year, an irrigation efficiency of 50 percent. It is the only Egypt wide efficiency anchor found. Dividing a net figure by 0.5 to estimate applied water is an assumption built on this single anchor, and wherever the rebuilt model does it, it must be labelled as an assumption, never as a measurement. All rows sit in crop_water_requirements_egypt.csv.

## Section 9. Yield response factors

Status: verified for three crops, corrected for one, a gap for one. Source family: FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper 33, Doorenbos and Kassam (1979), Yield Response to Water, as tabulated in two citable FAO secondary sources that agree on every crop they both list: FAO Paper 56 (Allen and coauthors, 1998), Table 24, and FAO Paper 66 (Steduto and coauthors, 2012), Table 1. The 1979 original table itself was not read in a publicly extractable copy.

* Maize: 1.25 in both tables. The model value of 1.25 is verified.
* Sugar beet: 1.0 in both tables. The model value of 1.00 is verified, for beet yield; a FAO deficit irrigation collation prints 0.80 and 0.90 under different yield definitions, beet against sugar, which matters only if the model ever models sugar yield.
* Potatoes: 1.1 in both tables. The model value of 1.10 is verified.
* Wheat: Paper 33 splits the crop. Winter wheat is 1.05 and spring wheat is 1.15 in both tables. Egypt grows winter wheat, so the relevant value is 1.05, and the model value of 1.00 is low by 0.05 against these two sources. A third FAO source, the manual on irrigation with wastewater, Table 19, prints winter wheat as 1.0, so the discrepancy between FAO printings is recorded here rather than hidden.
* Rice: no seasonal value appears in Table 24 of Paper 56 or in Table 1 of Paper 66, and Paper 56 states that Paper 33 gives values for only about 23 crops. The model value of 1.20 is therefore unverified, and a weakly hosted paper attributes stage values of 1.2 for the vegetative period and 2.4 for flowering to Paper 33, which raises the possibility that 1.20 is a stage value mistaken for a seasonal one. It must be checked against the 1979 original before it is used again. All values sit in fao33_ky_values.csv.

## Section 10. Nile inflow record, dry year frequency, and scenario probabilities

Status: the frequency analysis is now computed from a real published series. Before this rebuild, the scenario probabilities of 0.25, 0.50 and 0.25 and the budget multipliers of 0.85 and about 1.08 were declared judgment.

* The extracted series: annual flow of the Nile at Aswan, 1871 to 1970, one hundred annual values with no missing years.
* Source of the values: Cobb, G. W. (1978), The problem of the Nile, Biometrika, volume 65, pages 243 to 251, Table 1 on page 249, as republished in Durbin and Koopman (2001), Time Series Analysis by State Space Methods, Oxford University Press, and distributed as the Nile data set in the R datasets package. The file copy read was the University of Auckland Rdatasets text file with its documentation page; full addresses sit in research_notes_hydrology.md and in the CSV.
* Unit as published: ten to the power eight cubic metres. Conversion: flow in billion cubic metres equals the published value divided by ten. Example: the published value for 1913 is 456, giving 45.6 billion cubic metres.
* What the series is and is not: the source flags an apparent change point near 1898. Its documentation does not state whether each annual value is a calendar year total or a water year total, and it publishes no naturalisation method. It is therefore recorded here as gauged annual flow at Aswan as published, and it has not been blended with any naturalised series. Verification note from the skeptic pass: the exact Auckland file copy could not be reopened during that pass, but the canonical R series values match the delivered CSV at every spot checked position, including the 1913 value of 456 in published units, and every statistic below was recomputed from the CSV exactly during that pass.
* Longer records exist only as figures or summary statistics, reported in full in research_notes_hydrology.md and never mixed into the CSV: a naturalised Aswan record from 1871 to 2015 with mean 91 billion cubic metres per year, minimum 42 in 1913 and maximum 145 in 1897 (Shapland, Wheeler and Cascão, 2025, Chatham House); a naturalised reconstruction from 1900 to 2018 ranging from 45.6 to 120 with mean 86.5 (Wheeler and coauthors, 2020, Nature Communications, volume 11, article 5222); the MWRI (2000) summary as reported by Conway and coauthors (2005) for 1871 to 1872 through 1998 to 1999, mean 88.350, standard deviation 15.705, maximum about 142.378 in 1878 to 1879 and minimum about 45.879 in 1913 to 1914; and the Sutcliffe and Parks (1999) split of mean 110.4 before 1899 against 84.1 after, with a whole period mean of 93.2. Published long term means therefore range from 84.1 to 93.2 billion cubic metres by source, period and method, and no source found reconciles them.
* Frequency analysis, computed from the delivered CSV, with every figure independently recomputed during the skeptic pass: the sum of the one hundred flows is 9,193.5 billion cubic metres; the mean is 91.94; the sample standard deviation is 16.92; the median is 89.35; the lowest single year is 1913 at 45.6; the highest is 1879 at 137.0.
* Dry year definition and count: a dry year is a year below 0.85 times the long term mean, a threshold of 78.14 billion cubic metres. Twenty three of the one hundred years fall below it, a dry share of 23 percent. Fifty years fall below the median, which is true by construction and carries no extra information.
* Dry years by decade: the share is 0 percent in the partial 1870s and in the 1880s, 10 percent in the 1890s, then 30 percent in the 1900s, 40 percent in the 1910s, 30 percent in the 1920s, 30 percent in the 1930s, 30 percent in the 1940s, 20 percent in the 1950s and 30 percent in the 1960s, with the single year 1970 also dry. Every dry year in this record falls in 1899 or later, which is the change point the source itself flags.
* The change point split: years 1871 to 1898, twenty eight years, mean 109.77 and standard deviation 13.50. Years 1899 to 1970, seventy two years, mean 85.00 and standard deviation 12.48. The later regime mean agrees with the independent published means for similar later periods above, 84.1 and 86.5, while the full record mean of 91.94 is pulled up by the early high regime.
* Worst runs inside the extracted record: the longest runs of consecutive dry years are three years each, 1939 to 1941 with flows 77.1, 67.6 and 64.9 and a mean of 69.9, and 1968 to 1970 with flows 71.8, 71.4 and 74.0, the second run cut off by the end of the record, so its true length in the real river is unknown from this series. The lowest three year window is 1913 to 1915, mean 66.07, which is 0.719 times the long term mean. The lowest five year window is 1911 to 1915, mean 70.78. The lowest ten year window is 1919 to 1928, mean 80.07, which is 0.871 times the long term mean. The lowest twenty year window is 1912 to 1931, mean 81.73.
* The late 1970s to 1980s drought is not inside the extracted series, which ends in 1970, and nothing about its year by year shape is claimed here. Published period means only: the ten years 1978 to 1987 averaged 72.0 billion cubic metres per year in the naturalised record built from Deltares work and the Dongola gauge (El Sheikh and coauthors, 2024, Communications Earth and Environment), with Wheeler and coauthors (2020), Supplementary Table 3, giving 72.3 for the same decade, and a reported worst figure of 62 billion cubic metres for 1982 to 1984 (Hassan, in the NATO ASI volume on Third Millennium BC Climate Change and Old World Collapse). The neighbouring decades in the same record averaged 88.4 for 1968 to 1977 and 92.2 for 1988 to 2002.
* Derivation A, the model own band definitions on the full record: low means below 0.85 times the mean, high means above 1.08 times the mean, band edges 78.14 and 99.29. Low band: 23 years, probability 0.23, mean flow inside the band 71.77, typical multiplier 0.781. Expected band: 45 years, probability 0.45, mean flow 87.77, typical multiplier 0.955. High band: 32 years, probability 0.32, mean flow 112.28, typical multiplier 1.221. The lesson: 0.85 and 1.08 are band edges, not typical values. A typical low year delivers about 0.78 of the mean and a typical high year about 1.22.
* Derivation B, terciles of the historical distribution: cut points 83.10 and 98.40 billion cubic metres. Low tercile: probability 0.33, mean flow 74.57, multiplier 0.811. Middle tercile: probability 0.34, mean flow 89.45, multiplier 0.973. High tercile: probability 0.33, mean flow 111.86, multiplier 1.217.
* Sensitivity on the later regime only, years 1899 to 1970 with mean 85.00 and band edges at 0.85 and 1.08 of that mean: low band 10 years, probability 0.139, typical multiplier 0.788; expected band 43 years, probability 0.597, typical multiplier 0.966; high band 19 years, probability 0.264, typical multiplier 1.188. The typical multipliers barely move against the full record values, but the probabilities move a great deal, so a probability statement for this river is a statement about a chosen reference period, and the model must name its period when it names its probabilities.
* What this means for the current model numbers, stated plainly: the current probabilities of 0.25, 0.50 and 0.25 sit between Derivation A and the later regime sensitivity and are produced exactly by neither, so they remain judgment, now bounded judgment. The current budget multipliers of 0.85 and about 1.08 are band edges under Derivation A. If the three budgets are meant to be typical outcomes, the record supports multipliers near 0.78 to 0.81 for low and near 1.19 to 1.22 for high. Applied to the current expected budget of 11.00 billion cubic metres purely as an illustration of scale, a typical low year is 8.59 under Derivation A or 8.92 under Derivation B, and a typical high year is 13.43 or 13.39, against the current 9.35 and 11.90, both too close to the centre.
* Storage caution the record forces: annual flow at Aswan is not the same quantity as water released to the Delta in a single year, because Lake Nasser stores about two years of mean flow and releases have in practice been held near the treaty share in most years. Single year flow probabilities therefore overstate single year supply risk and understate the real risk, which is a multi year run like 1978 to 1987 arriving on a reservoir already drawn down. A defensible scenario set should be driven by multi year mean flows as well as single years. For reference, the lowest ten year mean in the extracted record is 0.871 of its long term mean, while the published 1978 to 1987 mean of about 72 against the naturalised 1900 to 2018 mean of 86.5 is a ratio of about 0.83.

## Section 11. Egypt water budget anchors

Status: real as published, with two official totals that disagree and are recorded unreconciled. Full rows, with addresses, sit in egypt_water_budget_anchors.csv.

* Nile share of Egypt under the 1959 Nile Waters Agreement: 55.5 billion cubic metres per year. Source: Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation, Strategy for the Development and Management of Water Resources until 2050, December 2016, verified against the live strategy document during the skeptic pass.
* The full 1959 accounting as printed in the Nile Basin Initiative (2025), Reporting the Nile Basin, a sourcebook for journalists: mean annual flow agreed as 84 billion cubic metres measured at Aswan, of which 55.5 allocated to Egypt, 18.5 to Sudan and 10 counted as losses. The components sum to 84. The 84 is an agreement assumption from the record available in 1959, not a current measured mean; Section 10 shows later published means from 84.1 to 93.2.
* Total fresh water resources, first official version: 59.68 billion cubic metres per year, built as Nile 55.50 plus deep groundwater 2.50 plus rains and floods 1.30 plus desalination 0.38. Source: CAPMAS, Egypt in Figures 2024, water balance table, itself naming the Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation as its source; the same total and components are printed by the OECD (2025), Policies for the Future of Farming and Food in Egypt.
* Total water resources, second official version: about 59.25 billion cubic metres per year, built as Nile 55.5 plus deep groundwater 2.1 plus rainwater and flash floods 1.3 plus desalinated water 0.35. Source: the MWRI Strategy until 2050, December 2016, verified against the live document during the skeptic pass. The 0.43 difference between the two versions sits entirely in the groundwater and desalination components. No official publication found reconciles them, and a model must cite which version it uses. A related flag: the Egypt third Nationally Determined Contribution passage prints components of 55.5, 2.5, 1.3 and 0.4 beside a total of 59.25, and those components sum to 59.7, not 59.25. The passage is recorded as published, with the arithmetic flagged, not corrected.
* Total water resources including reuse: 81.63 billion cubic metres for the most recent year printed in the CAPMAS Egypt in Figures 2024 table, against 85.50 to 86.66 in the earlier years of the same table. This total counts some water more than once as it is reused and must never be compared directly with the 55.5 treaty share as if they were the same kind of quantity.
* Agricultural use: agriculture accounts for 76 percent of total water use in Egypt. Source: CAPMAS (2025) as cited by the OECD (2025) for the 2023 to 2024 year. On a volume basis, the water balance inside Gabr (2022), sourced there to MWRI (2017) and CAPMAS (2018), gives measured gross irrigation Nile freshwater of 42 billion cubic metres per year against a computed total net irrigation requirement of about 21 billion cubic metres per year, the 50 percent efficiency anchor of Section 8.
* Press reported official figures, recorded as press and not as primary publication: a Ministry presentation to the Egyptian Senate, reported in 2026, puts total annual water demand at 88.55 billion cubic metres with agriculture at 68.1, against renewable resources printed in the same report as 65.35, a total that matches neither official version above because it counts deep groundwater at 7.9. It must not be used alongside 59.68 in one budget.
* The model area budget itself: no official figure exists for a water budget of the eight governorate model area, and none is claimed here. The current expected budget of 11.00 billion cubic metres remains a calibration set just under the water demand of the current cropping pattern, exactly as the model code declares. What this foundation changes is the scaling and weighting around that calibration: Section 10 now supplies record derived multipliers and probabilities. The budget level stays labelled a calibration until an official Delta allocation series is obtained, and it appears in the gap list in those words.

## Section 12. The gap list, complete

Every remaining gap this rebuild knows about, in plain words, unsoftened. Items are grouped by the parameter they block.

Costs:
1. Production costs at recent year levels are a gap for all five crops. The complete official per crop tables retrieved end at 2016 to 2017. Only wheat has later official anchors: the 2022 to 2023 figure in the republished government report, and a 2023 to 2024 forecast inside the same report, which is a forecast and not an outturn.
2. No public official per crop cost component breakdown exists in the tables found, except the wheat 2022 to 2023 rent and input shares in that same republished report. The CAPMAS tables give one rent inclusive total per crop.
3. The Ministry Economic Affairs Sector standalone cost studies were not found as public documents. Ministry data was reached through its CAPMAS republication, and the censusinfo download address for the 2016 to 2017 edition could not be reopened during the skeptic pass, so that provenance is asserted from the research record pending a recheck in a full browser.

Prices:
4. FAOSTAT annual producer prices stop at 2023 in the current vintage. No published FAOSTAT annual price exists for 2024 or 2025 for any of the five crops. The only honest 2025 price anchors are procurement prices: wheat at 14,667 pounds per tonne and sugar beet at 2,400 pounds per tonne.
5. FAOSTAT annual values for rice and for sugar beet are missing for 2019, 2020 and 2021. The hole is unfilled by any named source.
6. The sugar beet price concept is a decision the data forces, not a data gap: the FAOSTAT farm gate series gives 795 pounds per tonne for 2023 while the announced factory procurement price for the 2025 season is 2,400 pounds per tonne. The model must state which concept it uses and why.

National series:
7. National yields and harvested areas still ride on the Our World in Data mirror of FAOSTAT, pulled 12 November 2026. A direct FAOSTAT crops domain pull is outstanding, and since the bulk route now demonstrably works, this mirror is the weakest provenance in the foundation while carrying the model baseline.

Governorate statistics:
8. No CAPMAS branded governorate table for any year from 2019 to 2024 was obtained as a CAPMAS file. The governorate panel is first hand Ministry of Agriculture bulletin data, the series CAPMAS republishes. The four governorate CSV file names carry capmas for continuity with the research notes; the warning in Section 5 governs their reading.
9. Governorate coverage by year is partial: 2021 to 2022 and 2022 to 2023 exist only as second hand Menoufia wheat and maize figures; 2023 to 2024 is winter season only; 2019 to 2020 is summer season only; 2024 to 2025 has nothing at governorate level from any source reached.
10. Menoufia rice for 2020 to 2021 is not reported in the source table. It is recorded as not reported, never as zero, and the cell remains unverified.
11. Nili maize by governorate sits in Tables 27 to 31 of the 2020 to 2021 summer bulletin, located but not extracted. All saved maize figures are summer only and understate the full year; nationally Nili maize adds about 9 percent on top of summer maize area.
12. The CAPMAS bulletin for 2010 to 2011 was located in full text but its governorate tables were not extracted. It would add a CAPMAS direct historical anchor year outside the target window.
13. No source reached goes below governorate level. Any within governorate allocation in the model remains an assumption, not data.

Hydrology:
14. No publicly extractable year by year Nile flow table exists for any year after 1970. The records that run to 2015 and to 2018 exist as figures and as model inputs, not as published tables. Extending the series requires the Deltares reconstruction or MWRI yearbooks, neither public in extractable form.
15. The late 1970s to 1980s drought, the most decision relevant event in the record for a storage based system, is known here only through published period means of about 72 billion cubic metres per year for 1978 to 1987 and one reported worst figure of 62 for 1982 to 1984. Its year by year shape is unavailable.
16. The Cobb series documentation does not state its year definition or its naturalisation status. It is gauged flow at Aswan as published and must not be relabelled natural flow. The exact Auckland file copy cited as read could not be reopened during the skeptic pass; the canonical R series matches the delivered CSV at every spot checked position, and every derived statistic replays exactly from the CSV.
17. Published long term means for Aswan flow range from 84.1 to 93.2 billion cubic metres by source, period and method, with no reconciling official series. Any single expected flow is a named choice, not a fact.
18. Official Egyptian water totals disagree with each other: 59.68 against 59.25 billion cubic metres for total fresh resources, the difference sitting in the groundwater and desalination components, and one Nationally Determined Contribution passage whose printed components sum to 59.7 beside a printed total of 59.25. No official reconciliation was found.

Crop water:
19. No single consistent source gives applied water requirements for all five crops under Delta conditions. The applied figures are second hand printings of official tables, CAPMAS (2017) via a 2024 journal paper and MWRI (2003) duty tables via a 2006 conference paper, neither read in its original form in this pass. The MWRI duties are for the Lower Egypt region, broader than the eight model governorates.
20. The Gabr (2022) Delta wheat evapotranspiration of 1,782 cubic metres per hectare and net requirement of 1,272 are internally inconsistent with the same paper tables for its other territories, which print 2,217 to 2,572, and with the west Delta CROPWAT wheat value of 4,904. No erratum exists. Delta wheat has no clean CROPWAT number from this work.
21. The sugar beet applied requirement from CAPMAS, 9,426 cubic metres per hectare, is more than double the Gabr Delta net figure of 3,927, and no third source was found to arbitrate. Delta sugar beet applied water is unresolved within a very wide range.
22. Crop specific and Delta specific irrigation efficiency does not exist in anything found. The only anchor is one Egypt wide pair, gross 42 against net about 21 billion cubic metres, giving 50 percent, as cited inside Gabr (2022) from MWRI (2017) and CAPMAS (2018). Every net to applied conversion, crop by crop, is a labelled assumption resting on that single anchor.

Model structure gaps the data exposed:
23. FAO Paper 33 seasonal yield response factor for rice could not be verified in any citable source: both authoritative FAO secondary tables omit rice. The model value of 1.20 stands unverified and may be a vegetative stage value mistaken for a seasonal one. It needs checking against the 1979 original before reuse.
24. No official water budget exists for the eight governorate model area. The expected budget of 11.00 billion cubic metres remains a calibration, now surrounded by record derived multipliers and probabilities, and no source reached publishes Delta releases by year that would let it be replaced by a measured series.
25. Potatoes are three crops in the sources, winter, summer and Nili, with materially different areas, yields, costs and water requirements, while the model carries one potatoes parameter set. A season must be named, or the parameter set split, before any single potatoes number is defensible.
