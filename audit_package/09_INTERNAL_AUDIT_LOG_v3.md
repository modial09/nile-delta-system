## Iteration 2: the infeasibility verdicts, and the highest floor that can be held

The logs report that the 95 and 100 percent floors cannot be held in either variant under Derivation A. This iteration attacks that verdict as a possible modelling artefact.

Which scenario binds. Budgets rise district by district from low to expected to high, because every district budget is the scenario total times the same land share. Staple capacity rises with water. The low scenario therefore binds for any planting: a planting that can deliver a floor level in the low scenario can deliver at least as much in the other two, and a planting that cannot reach the floor in the low scenario fails the guarantee whatever it does elsewhere. Feasibility of a floor share is a physical property of land, crop caps, yields, water requirements and the low budget. Prices play no part in it, so the highest feasible share is the same for both beet variants by construction.

Independent test. An independent linear program was written for this audit and solved with a different solver from the model solver. It chooses hectares and low scenario water to maximise staple production, under the district land pools, the crop cap shares, the district low budgets, the requirement ceilings on water per planting, and the production floor at zero on every planting, using the same printed parameter values. Result for Derivation A: maximum staple production in the low scenario is 10,763,331 tons. Divided by baseline staple production at full water of 11,493,640 tons, the highest floor share that is feasible in all scenarios under Derivation A is 0.9365, that is 93.65 percent, for variant beet795 and, identically, for variant beet2400.

What that settles. The 95 percent floor needs 10,918,958 tons. The maximum attainable is 155,627 tons short of it. The 100 percent floor needs 11,493,640 tons and stands 730,309 tons above the maximum. The 90 percent floor needs 10,344,276 tons and stands 419,055 tons below the maximum, which is why the 90 percent runs solve. The infeasibility at 95 and 100 percent is economics and data, not a solver artefact and not a formulation artefact: an optimiser built independently for this audit, with no stake in the model, hits the same wall at the same place.

Derivation B cross check. The same independent program at the Derivation B low budget of 8.921 billion cubic metres gives a maximum of 11,026,897 tons, a share of 0.9594, that is 95.94 percent. That predicts exactly the pattern the beet795 log prints for Derivation B: the 95 percent floor is held, with headroom of 107,939 tons, and the 100 percent floor is not held. A modelling artefact would have no reason to reproduce that shift when only the scenario budgets and probabilities change. The log pattern matches the independent computation in both derivations.

What drives it, in numbers. Baseline water demand at full watering is 16.724 billion cubic metres. The low budget of 8.591 billion cubic metres is 51.37 percent of that demand. The expected budget of 10.505 billion cubic metres is 62.82 percent of it. Staples alone, at the baseline pattern, demand 14.630 billion cubic metres at full watering, which exceeds the low budget by 6.039 billion cubic metres and exceeds even the expected budget by 4.125 billion cubic metres. District example: Dakahlia baseline demand is 3.460 billion cubic metres against a low scenario district budget of 1.443 billion cubic metres and an expected scenario district budget of 1.764 billion cubic metres. No replanting inside the caps escapes the arithmetic, because the independent program above already chooses the best replanting for staples and still reaches only 93.65 percent.

The version 2 analogue. The version 2 audit found a highest feasible share of about 1.039, so the version 2 floors were feasible at every level and the version 2 result at full protection was a loss, not an impossibility. That world is gone. On the version 3 data the guarantee dial now runs from 90 percent, which is held, to a ceiling of 93.65 percent, beyond which no plan exists under Derivation A.

Verdict for Iteration 2: the infeasibility verdicts SURVIVE the attack in full. The highest feasible floor share under Derivation A is 0.9365 for each variant.

Of the growth in the gain, the baseline fall contributes 54.8 percent and the optimised rise contributes 45.2 percent. This variant also changes the beet price concept against version 2, so its split is the less clean of the two, and the beet795 split is the one to quote.

The fairness reference, restated for version 3. Baseline expected staple output as a share of baseline staple production at full water: for beet795, 7,988,507 tons divided by 11,493,640 tons is 69.50 percent. For beet2400, 7,386,025 tons divided by 11,493,640 tons is 64.26 percent. A gain that falls off a cliff when budgets move five percent in one direction, and barely moves when they move five percent in the other, is a gain that lives on the feasibility margin described in Iteration 2, not a gain that rests mainly on clever reallocation.

The sentence this iteration is required to state plainly, stated plainly: the gain is mainly the baseline being water starved under applied requirements.

Verdict for Iteration 3: the headline gain figures SURVIVE as model arithmetic, and their meaning SHRINKS. Any presentation of the 47.74 percent or the 52.92 percent that does not carry the decomposition with it, and the 69.50 percent staple delivery share beside it, misleads by omission.

## Iteration 5: the evaluation path change, verified outside the model code

The claim under attack. RERUN_CHOICES.md states that plan evaluation in version 3 holds the planting through a fixed planting path, because the version 2 approach of bounding the stage one variables misreported cap binding plans as infeasible on the real data, the average year plan scoring as infeasible in all three scenarios under the old approach and correctly under the new one. If the new path is wrong, the baseline margin is wrong, and every gain figure built on it dies. This iteration therefore recomputes the baseline expected margin without using the model code at all.

Independent method. The baseline planting is the printed real district pattern. Planting cost is sunk once the planting is fixed, so in each district and scenario the water allocation that maximises margin is found by plain reasoning that fits the linear structure exactly. First, each planting receives the minimum water that keeps its production at zero or above: for a crop with response factor above one, that minimum share of the requirement is the factor minus one, divided by the factor. Second, the remaining district budget goes to the plantings in falling order of marginal revenue per cubic metre, where marginal revenue is price times district yield times the response factor divided by the requirement, up to the full requirement of each planting. District budgets are separate in this model, so the calculation is exact district by district, with no solver and no model code. Parameters were rebuilt by this audit from the raw yield files and the delivered CSV files, following the definitions printed in RERUN_CHOICES.md and the foundation.

Results, variant beet795, Derivation A. Baseline expected margin by this audit: EGP 95,108,497,248. Printed in the log: EGP 95,107,983,182. Difference: EGP 514,066, which is 0.0005 percent of the margin, five parts in a million at the level of the printed billions. Baseline expected staple output by this audit: 7,988,546 tons, against 7,988,507 tons printed, a difference of 39 tons. Baseline expected water use by this audit: 10.936711 billion cubic metres, against 10.937 printed.

Results, variant beet2400, Derivation A. Baseline expected margin by this audit: EGP 96,780,067,296. Printed: EGP 96,779,569,341. Difference: EGP 497,955, again 0.0005 percent. Expected staple output agrees to within 37 tons.

Results, variant beet795, Derivation B. Baseline expected margin by this audit, under the Derivation B budgets and probabilities: EGP 95,120,069,975. Printed: EGP 95,119,555,100. Difference: EGP 514,875, the same size and the same sign as in the primary runs. Expected staple output by this audit: 7,988,247 tons, against 7,988,208 tons printed.

Reading of the residual. The residual is systematic: the same direction and the same relative size in three independent evaluations, on two budget sets and two price sets. That pattern points at a rounding level difference in parameter reconstruction, namely the yield per hectare column of the delivered CSV files, which is printed to three decimals, against the per feddan values from which the model builds its multipliers. It does not point at the evaluation path, because a path error on cap binding plans produces infeasibility verdicts or margin errors of a wholly different size, and because the beet795 optimised plan itself sits exactly at the wheat cap and the potatoes cap in every district, which is the population of plans the old path was said to misreport. The baseline pattern that this iteration evaluates is not the cap binding plan, but the average year plan evaluation inside the information values uses the same fixed planting path, and the value of the stochastic solution it produces is graded in Iteration 7.

Scope note. This audit did not rerun the version 2 bounding path, so the historical claim about the old path is not independently confirmed here. It does not need to be: no version 3 number rests on the old path, and every version 3 number that rests on the new path is verified above.

Verdict for Iteration 5: PASS. The fixed planting evaluation path is verified independently. The baseline margins stand, and with them every gain figure that subtracts them.

## Iteration 6: the beet contrast, and one identity that fails

Planting and coverage, from the printed plans and coverage tables.

* Variant beet795. The optimised plan plants zero hectares of sugar beet in every one of the eight districts. The coverage table prints the sugar beet row as not a number in every scenario, because there is no planting to deliver water to. The version 2 write off is gone in this variant, completely: nothing is planted, nothing is sunk, nothing is written off.
* Variant beet2400. The optimised plan plants sugar beet in all eight districts, totalling 321,075 hectares by addition of the printed plan. Coverage of the beet requirement is 0.000 in the low scenario, 0.033 in the expected scenario and 1.000 in the high scenario. At a response factor of exactly 1.00, production is the delivered share of full production, so beet production in the low scenario is zero on 321,075 planted hectares: a write off, with planting cost sunk. The sunk beet planting cost in the low scenario, at the printed cost of EGP 17,607.14 per hectare, is about EGP 5.65 billion. The write off pattern therefore returns under the procurement price, at a similar area to version 2 and at about half the version 2 money, because the version 3 cost is the real CAPMAS level and the version 2 cost was invented.

The identity that fails. The identity put to this audit was that the baseline margin difference between the two variants equals baseline beet production times the price difference of EGP 1,605 per tonne. It does not hold, in any reading of the words baseline beet production that this audit could construct.

* The baseline margin difference, by subtraction of the printed margins, is EGP 1,671,586,159. Divided by EGP 1,605 per tonne, it implies a beet production of 1,041,487 tons.
* Baseline beet production at full water, recomputed by this audit from the printed baseline pattern, national yield and district multipliers, is 8,994,929 tons. Times the price difference, that is about EGP 14.44 billion, which is not the margin difference.
* Expected baseline beet production under the water allocation that the baseline evaluation actually chooses, recomputed by this audit in Iteration 5, is 291,300 tons in the beet795 evaluation and 5,159,334 tons in the beet2400 evaluation. Neither figure, times the price difference, gives the margin difference, and the two figures differ from each other by a factor of about eighteen, because the price change itself moves the water.
* Decomposition of the difference, from the Iteration 5 replay, in round terms. Beet revenue rises by about EGP 12.151 billion when the beet price moves from 795 to 2,400, because beet is watered and harvested in the second evaluation and largely not in the first. Rice revenue falls by about EGP 7.759 billion and maize revenue falls by about EGP 2.721 billion over the same move, because the water that grows that beet is taken from rice and maize in the baseline districts. The net of those three movements is about EGP 1.672 billion, which is the margin difference. The margin difference is a reallocation effect with a price trigger, not a tonnage times a price difference.

Verdict for Iteration 6: the planting and coverage claims PASS in both variants, exactly as the results note states them. The price identity FAILS and is killed in the kill list. No sentence may ship that describes the gap between the two variant baseline margins as baseline beet production times EGP 1,605 per tonne. The results note itself makes no such claim, and this kill is recorded so that the claim is never introduced downstream of this audit.

## Auditor verdict

The arithmetic of the version 3 data run is sound. Every headline figure in paper/Results_Note_v3_DataRun.md replays from the two run logs, the baseline margins replay from raw parameters by arithmetic that owes nothing to the model code, the dual tables replay in both units, the information values replay exactly, and the infeasibility verdicts replay under an independent solver.
