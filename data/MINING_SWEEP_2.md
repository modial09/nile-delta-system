# Mining sweep 2 for the Nile Delta water allocation model

Date of sweep: 14 November 2026. Purpose: to test the verdict that the first data foundation stopped at the bare minimum, above all on recent production costs. Method: five parallel investigator passes, one per target, working from primary or republished primary sources only, with Arabic language searching where the record is Arabic, every dead end logged with the route tried, and no figure accepted without a date and a stable reference. This document records what was found, what was translated, what failed and why, and closes with a map of all 25 gaps in data/DATA_FOUNDATION.md Section 12 to one of three states: closed by this sweep, closable by computation, or genuinely open.

Headline of the whole sweep: the first foundation was wrong in one place that matters. It stated that the Ministry Economic Affairs Sector standalone cost studies were not found as public documents. They exist, they are public, and they carry official component level costs for every model crop through the 2022 season. Details in Target 1.

## Target 1. Recent crop production costs and farm economics, 2019 to 2026

### What was found

1. The prize find. The Ministry of Agriculture and Land Reclamation, Economic Affairs Sector (قطاع الشئون الاقتصادية) publishes its own cost series, the Bulletin of Cost Statistics and Net Return, Arabic title نشرة إحصاءات التكاليف وصافى العائد, hosted at agri.gov.eg in two parts per year. The foundation wheat anchor, about 17,000 pounds per feddan in 2022 to 2023 with rent about 4,700, remains the strongest post 2022 official adjacent figure, and it sits plausibly on the line from the Ministry bulletin value of 15,852 for 2021 to 2022. The press range implies a much steeper jump driven mainly by rent, and no official table confirms it.

### What failed and why

1. CAPMAS per crop PDFs for 2017 to 2018 onward. Routes tried: Arabic title searches for the income bulletin 2023 and 2023 to 2024, site restricted search on capmas.gov.eg, and the censusinfo and metadata CAPMAS catalogue and history pages. Result: only the 2016 to 2017 full PDF, Egypt in Figures, and press release republications. No per crop PDF for any later edition exists in the open routes searched.
2. Cost bulletin editions after 2022. Routes tried: Arabic title searches with 2022 to 2023 and 2023 to 2024, site restricted search on agri.gov.eg, and the series catalogue page itself. Result: the catalogue ends at 2022 and searches return only editions to 2022. Either the series stopped, or later editions sit behind the library login. The evidence found supports stopped or unpublished, not hidden in the open web.
3. Summer 2021 Part 2 PDF. The edition is listed in the catalogue; two targeted searches returned only the summer 2020 and summer 2022 PDFs, and the catalogue item is login gated. Logged as a route requiring a live browser session.
4. The Ministry routes named agr.doe.gov.eg and agriculture.gov.eg. No publication was retrieved from either. All Ministry PDFs in this sweep came from agri.gov.eg uploads.
5. A named Ministry publication called نشرة الاقتصاد الزراعي (Agricultural Economics Bulletin). Searched as a standalone title; not found. The cost bulletin in item 1 above is the Sector priced publication.
6. A State Information Service repository PDF for the 16 April 2025 CAPMAS release. The fetch misresolved to an unrelated Bulgarian document and the snippet text was garbled beyond use. No values taken from it.

### Target 1 verdict

The recent cost gap is partly closed, and the closed part is the larger part. Official, national, component level costs now exist in hand for every model crop and season from winter 2019 to 2020 through summer 2022, in both rent bases, verified inside the tables. The open part is 2023 to 2026, plus the single missing summer 2021 season, whose edition exists behind a login. For 2023 onward the honest position is: no official per crop table exists in the Ministry catalogue, which ends at 2022, or in CAPMAS channels; the gap for those years can be approached by computation, carrying the 2022 official base forward with the wheat anchor for 2022 to 2023 and the press range as an outer check, and every such figure must be labelled an estimate, never an official value.


## Target 2. Egyptian university research

### What was found

Attribution note that governs everything below: most numbers published in Mansoura University journals are not Mansoura University fieldwork. They are Agricultural Research Center trials at Sakha Agricultural Research Station, Kafr El Sheikh, North Delta, clay soil, repeated across two decades, published in the Journal of Soil Sciences and Agricultural Engineering (JSSAE) on the Egyptian Knowledge Bank journals platform. Genuine university farm trials exist at Benha (Moshtohor, Qalyubia) and at the Kafrelsheikh University farm. All journal PDFs named below are public on the EKB journals platform (journals.ekb.eg and its journal subdomains), which is open even though the EKB main portal is gated.

1. Allocation modelling and cropping pattern economics.
   1. Abo El Magd, Sherif Salah Zaky (2016), Cropping Pattern Optimization in Egypt and Associated HAD Operation Rules, MSc thesis, Faculty of Engineering, Cairo University. Full text public on the Ain Shams repository: https://research.asu.edu.eg/bitstream/12345678/10622/1/V4815.pdf . National scale nonlinear GAMS optimisation, 7 High Aswan Dam release scenarios from 40 to 55.5 billion cubic metres, maximising agricultural net profit. The closest Egyptian thesis analogue to this model, but national, with no governorate disaggregation and no field measured coefficients.
   2. Soliman, Eman Sayed Ahmed (2005), Crop Pattern Assessment in Western Delta Using GIS, MSc thesis, Ain Shams University. Full text public on the same repository. Pilot district only (Abou Hummus, Beheira), GIS database and an LP decision support system. Method value, not coefficients.
   3. El Shazely, Hesham El Sayed Omar (2012), Development of a Model for Environmental Watershed Management, Mahmoudia Canal Command Area, PhD thesis, Ain Shams University. RIBASIM and SEBAL based allocation and drainage reuse study with 2008 and 2008 to 2009 cropping tables. Tail end allocation value, not coefficients.
   4. Dawoud, Seham D. Z. (2014), Economic optimal allocation of irrigation water in Egypt, Journal of Development and Agricultural Economics 6(12), pages 472 to 480, Damietta University author. Full text public. National LP on MALR and CAPMAS statistics, average 2009 to 2011, with a published per crop coefficient table: applied water per feddan of 1,760 for wheat, 6,294 for rice, 3,179 for summer maize, 2,599 for Nili maize, 2,204 for sugar beet; net return per 1,000 cubic metres of 1,766 pounds for wheat, 432 for rice, 607 for summer maize, 1,491 for sugar beet. The cleanest published Egyptian LP coefficient table found. One warning: its rice duty of 6,294 per feddan (14,986 per hectare) stands far above every field measurement found in this sweep and should be flagged as a different accounting, likely including land preparation and percolation, not averaged with field values.
   5. Morsi and Mekled (undated, EKB record), An Analytical Study for Effect of Reducing Water Voracious Crop Areas on the Cropping Pattern Structure and Its Water Needs, Annals of Agricultural Science, Ain Shams University. Old lands LP, 3 models by 2 scenarios: available water 41.483 billion cubic metres, optimised use 38.604 to 38.726, savings of 6.65 to 6.94 percent, net return of the water unit rising from 1.634 to between 1.755 and 1.767 pounds per cubic metre.
   6. Abdel Hamid and Abu El Azaim (2018), العائد الاقتصادي لوحدة المياه فى إنتاج المحاصيل المختلفة ودوره فى تحقيق أهداف السياسة الزراعية المصرية, The economic return of the water unit in the production of different crops and its role in achieving the goals of Egyptian agricultural policy, Egyptian Journal of Agricultural Economics, volume 28, number 2, pages 445 onward. Full PDF public: https://meae.journals.ekb.eg/article_111976_f7328b22f2f886f5f1e6548764eaf378.pdf . National current pattern on MALR Economic Bulletin data; states a field irrigation system efficiency of about 70 percent as an asserted national average on page 446. Not a measured series.
   7. Abu Zeid, Dalia El Sayed (2019), الكفاءة الاقتصادية للموارد الأروائية المتاحة بمنطقة القنطرة شرق – محافظة الإسماعيلية, The economic efficiency of available irrigation resources in the Qantara Sharq area, Ismailia Governorate, Egyptian Journal of Agricultural Economics, volume 29, number 4. Values are treatment ranges; the non deficit surface irrigation treatment in each paper is the duty analogue, and any use must say so.
   1. Sugar beet, the arbitration the foundation needed. Five independent Sakha trials plus two modelled sets converge: applied water 2,075 to 3,150 cubic metres per feddan for standard surface irrigation, equal to 4,940 to 7,500 per hectare; consumptive use 1,709 to 2,560 per feddan, equal to 4,069 to 6,095 per hectare.    4. Maize, Sakha and El Hamoul. JSSAE (2012), DOI 10.21608/jssae.2012.53319: surface irrigation applied 2,647 cubic metres per feddan, consumptive use 2,262; subsurface drip applied 2,018, consumptive use 1,604; water application efficiency 94.27 percent subsurface drip against 76.59 percent surface. 3. Irrigation efficiency and water productivity.
   1. Canal and mesqa level, the only Delta efficiency measurements found, both in Kafr El Sheikh. Sharaf (2009), JSSAE 34(6), pages 7481 to 7503, DOI 10.21608/jssae.2009.103878: improved mesqas on the Dakalt canal saved about 1,019 to 1,241 cubic metres per feddan for long berseem against unimproved mesqas; sugar beet applied water on improved mesqas 2,815 to 2,923 per feddan against 3,482 on the unimproved comparison; conveyance efficiency above 90 percent and an irrigation water efficiency conclusion of about 70 percent with developed canals. ### What failed and why

1. Egyptian Knowledge Bank main portal: gated by account and IP, as expected. The journals platform is open; the thesis and database side is not verifiable from public pages. Route logged as gated.
2. Egyptian Universities Libraries Consortium (EULC): gated by design. Kafrelsheikh University own postgraduate page states the public rule: a researcher outside the university sees only 10 percent of a thesis full text. No thesis records could be verified through the consortium with search and fetch tools alone. The agricultural economics theses most likely to hold duty tables (a Cairo University PhD of 1994 and an Ain Shams MSc of 1996, known only from the reference list of Abdou, 2003) sit behind that wall and in physical library holdings.
3. University repositories other than Ain Shams: no thesis repository records for Delta water allocation or crop water requirements were returned for Cairo, Mansoura, Alexandria, Benha or Tanta universities. 4. The CLAC original study, Irrigation water requirements for cultivated crops in Nile Delta and Valley: still not read in the original. ### Target 2 verdict

This literature fixes the weakest part of the foundation and cannot fix the rest. It supplies, in the original and in public full text: an arbitrated sugar beet duty; field measured applied water and consumptive use for wheat, rice and maize under North Delta clay conditions; a published Egyptian LP coefficient table for cross checking; and canal level efficiency evidence for one governorate, usable as a bounded sensitivity range (field application 60 to 77 percent surface, 88 to 94 percent drip and subsurface). It does not contain a Delta wide or governorate level measured dataset, a Delta potato duty, or an efficiency time series, in any publicly retrievable form. If those exist, they sit in EULC gated theses and in research institute internal reports. Sakha duties applied to all eight governorates remain a limitation that must be named, not hidden.


## Target 3. Satellite and remote sensing data

### What was found

1. FAO WaPOR, current version 3, launched 4 October 2023 at the Rome Water Dialogue. Method: ETLook, a Penman Monteith based two source energy balance, with a light use efficiency model for biomass. Layers include actual evapotranspiration and interception (AETI) split into evaporation, transpiration and interception; reference evapotranspiration; net primary production; total biomass production; gross and net biomass water productivity; precipitation; land cover; phenology; and relative soil moisture. Resolutions in version 3: Level 1 global at 300 metres, Level 2 national at 100 metres covering the African continent and the Middle East (so Egypt and the whole Delta carry 100 metre national data), Level 3 sub national at 20 metres for selected areas only. There is no 375 metre WaPOR product; 375 metres is the native resolution of a VIIRS input and a recurring error in secondary summaries. Aggregations: dekadal (every 10 days), monthly, seasonal and annual depending on layer; AETI is available at all three of annual, monthly and dekadal at all resolutions. Coverage of version 3 runs from January 2018 to the present, in near real time, with dekads first released near real time and later replaced by final versions. Product documentation: https://www.fao.org/in-action/remote-sensing-for-water-productivity/wapor-data/en and the Digital Earth Africa WaPOR specification, modified 19 March 2026.
2. The download route was tested, not merely documented. The FAO GISMGR 2.0 catalogue API at data.apps.fao.org answered anonymous catalogue reads with no API key on 14 November 2026:
   1. The workspace list at https://data.apps.fao.org/gismgr/api/v2/catalog/workspaces returned the WAPOR 3 and WAPOR 2 workspaces among 20 on the first page.
   2. The mapset list for WAPOR 3 returned the Level 1 and Level 2 mapsets with units and scale factors, codes in the form level, variable, frequency: for example `L2-AETI-A` is Level 2 AETI annual, unit millimetres per year, scale factor 0.1.
   3. The rasters call for `L2-AETI-A` returned exactly 8 annual rasters, 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025, each dimensioned 1 January to 31 December of its year, each with a direct download address and a cloud storage address. The 2025 raster was created 7 January 2026, after its year closed. This is direct tested evidence that complete annual 100 metre actual evapotranspiration files exist for the Delta for every year 2018 to 2025. The files are global and about 9.2 to 9.4 gigabytes each, so extraction should use a spatial query or Earth Engine, never a whole file download for a Delta study.
   4. Licence in the tested workspace record: Creative Commons Attribution NonCommercial ShareAlike 3.0 IGO, free access. One secondary source states a different Creative Commons variant, so the workspace record governs any citation.
3. Google Earth Engine carries WaPOR version 3 as dekadal collections only, loaded by project identifier (the FAO note of 6 June 2025 lists 12 identifiers under projects/UNFAO/wapor/v3, for AETI, evaporation, transpiration, interception, net primary production and reference evapotranspiration at Levels 1 and 2), plus daily reference evapotranspiration. 5. Other public products, tested or documented in the investigator pass:
   1. USGS SSEBop (MODIS version 5, VIIRS version 6): 1 kilometre, dekadal to annual, global, MODIS from 2003 and VIIRS from February 2012; actual evapotranspiration only; portal download and Earth Engine community assets. In Egypt it is the strongest cross check found: validated in the Delta against lysimeters with only 0.68 percent underestimation (cited in Saleh et al. 2025), and ranked best of three products in Egypt by Ayyad et al. (2019). It tends to underestimate where WaPOR overestimates, which is why the ensemble of the two is defensible.
   2. NASA MODIS MOD16A2 and its gap filled form: 500 metres, 8 day, global from 2000; evapotranspiration and potential evapotranspiration via Earth Engine, AppEEARS and LP DAAC, with a free NASA Earthdata login for downloads. Second to SSEBop in Egypt in Ayyad et al. (2019); gives no evapotranspiration over open water, which matters for Delta canals and lakes. Third opinion only.
   3. PML V2.2: 500 metres, 8 day, global, 2000 to 2024 on MODIS and 2012 to 2025 on VIIRS, with transpiration split and gross primary production. Best performing evapotranspiration product alongside WaPOR version 2.1 over the Nile Basin in the water balance evaluation of McNamara et al. (2021). No Delta specific validation found.

   6. EEFlux and METRIC in Egypt: overestimated actual evapotranspiration by 36 percent in Ayyad et al. (2019). Not recommended without local calibration.
   7. OpenET: United States only. Dead end for this project, checked directly.
   8. Nile Basin Initiative: publishes no evapotranspiration raster product. It appears only as a supplier of rain gauge and discharge series, ending 2011 and 2014, to other teams evaluations. No download route exists to budget time against.
6. Published Delta values, extractable now:
   1. Zankalon region (mostly Sharkia, about 5 percent of the old lands), Ayyad, Al Zayed, Ha and Ribbe, International Journal of Plant Production, 2024: five year average seasonal actual evapotranspiration from WaPOR, 2016 to 2021 seasons: rice 807, maize 660, wheat 595, berseem 767 millimetres per season; transpiration fraction at or above 0.79 for all crops in all years. The same paper yield and water productivity columns for rice and maize sit far below commonly reported Egyptian values and the authors themselves flag them; use the evapotranspiration column, not the yield columns. Cross check inside the same paper, El Agha et al. remote sensing for the central Delta 2008 to 2009: rice 779, maize 740, wheat 634, berseem 746 millimetres per season.
   2. Whole Delta irrigation performance, Saleh, Ayyad and Ribbe, Earth 6(3), 16 July 2025: eleven Delta governorates, crop mapping 2015 to 2020, WaPOR version 2 land cover as training labels for a Landsat random forest, ensembled WaPOR and SSEBop actual evapotranspiration. Adequacy (actual over potential evapotranspiration): summer 0.4 to 0.63 for rice and maize, meaning under supply; winter 1.3 to 1.7 for wheat and clover, meaning over supply. Equity coefficient of variation below 10 percent; reliability 0.02 to 0.08; all indicators improve toward the north and new lands on the fringes are worst. This summer under supply and winter over supply split is exactly the pattern a seasonal allocation model exists to optimise, and it is invisible in annual tables.
   3. Ground validation in the Delta, ICID 3rd World Irrigation Forum paper, Bali, 2019: WaPOR against SSEBop agreement r squared 0.8448 for field crops and 0.5699 for an orchard; trends and magnitudes agree, with significant differences from ground measurements; orchards show large underestimation. No per crop bias percentage is published, which is itself a gap.
   4. Error bars from continental work: Blatchford et al. (2020) direct validation RMSE 1.2 millimetres per day at point scale, with overestimation at low values; an Agricultural Systems analysis (2023) supports plus or minus 9 percent for aggregated biomass and evapotranspiration; a 2025 Zankalon preprint states WaPOR version 2 AETI uncertainty of plus or minus 15 percent or greater in arid environments. For annual governorate means, carry plus or minus 9 percent as the working bar and report the point scale figures only as context.
   5. Plausibility band from eddy correlation in the Delta (Sugita et al.): estimated annual evapotranspiration 566 to 828 millimetres under water saving irrigation and 875 to 1,225 under conventional irrigation, for a double cropped Delta year.

### Target 3 verdict

Satellite data can replace the weakest machinery in the model, and the route is tested working today. First: measured AETI can stand as the model water consumption term in place of the net requirement tables, which dissolves the net against applied contradiction, because AETI is the consumption outcome both bases try to predict. Second: if the model works in consumptive use, the single Egypt wide efficiency anchor of about 50 percent is no longer needed as a conversion at all; it survives only to convert a target consumption into a diversion requirement, where it should become a district and season parameter, not a national constant. Third: governorate means from the raster give the district level variation the project has never had, at a defensible resolution. Recommended base: 2024 as the primary year, with 2023 and 2025 as sensitivity years, or a 2023 to 2025 mean to damp product uncertainty. What satellite data cannot replace: applied water and diversions (AETI is consumption, not delivery, and says nothing about conveyance loss, drainage outflow or reuse); the split between Nile water, groundwater and drainage reuse inside consumption; crop attribution inside a season without an external crop map (the Saleh et al. Landsat random forest approach is the worked example); harvested yield and revenue (biomass is not grain; keep official yield statistics for the value side); and any counterfactual response, since observed consumption describes the current allocation, not behaviour under a new one. Its right role in the model is the measured baseline the optimisation must first reproduce.

## What computation can now close, stated once for the whole sweep

Three computations became possible in this sweep that were not possible before it. First, the cost series for 2023 onward can be forecast from an official base that now runs 2015 to 2022 in two bulletin families (CAPMAS income bulletin to 2016 to 2017, Ministry cost bulletin 2019 to 2022), with the wheat anchor of about 17,000 per feddan for 2022 to 2023 as a validation point and the press range of 35,000 to 50,000 as an outer bound to test against, using at least ten years of history where the combined families allow it and reporting backtest error before any forecast figure is used. Second, governorate actual water consumption can be computed from WaPOR Level 2 rasters for every year 2018 to 2025, giving the model a measured consumption baseline by governorate and season and a measured Delta consumption total, which no official publication provides. Third, the remaining statistical extractions (Nili maize tables in the located 2020 to 2021 bulletin, potato season tables in the 2021 to 2022 bulletin, net return sections of the cost bulletins, the CAPMAS Statistical Yearbook 2024 agriculture chapters) are defined processing jobs on documents already located, not searches.

## Closing gap map: the 25 foundation gaps, restated

Each entry keeps the foundation numbering of data/DATA_FOUNDATION.md Section 12 and gives one state, with the evidence in one or two lines.

1. Recent production costs missing for all crops. **Closed by this sweep for 2019 to 2022; the 2023 to 2026 tail is closable by computation.** Ministry cost bulletin values for every crop and season are in Target 1, verified inside the tables. No official table exists for 2023 onward; forecast from the 2022 base with the wheat anchor, labelled as estimate.
2. No public per crop cost component breakdown. **Closed by this sweep.** The Ministry cost bulletin publishes the full component list per crop, by governorate, for 2019 to 2022.
3. Ministry standalone cost studies not found as public documents. **Closed by this sweep.** The statement was wrong. The Bulletin of Cost Statistics and Net Return is public at agri.gov.eg and is the same system that feeds the CAPMAS income bulletin.
4. FAOSTAT prices stop at 2023. **Closed by this sweep for wheat, maize and potatoes; genuinely open for rice and sugar beet farm gate.** The December 2025 vintage carries 2024 values of 17,380, 19,942 and 10,446 pounds per tonne. Rice stops at 2023 in the newest vintage; sugar beet could not be verified past 2023, and its procurement record carries the post 2023 anchor instead.
5. FAOSTAT rice and sugar beet values missing for 2019, 2020 and 2021. **Genuinely open.** The rice hole is confirmed present in the newest vintage. The sugar beet hole is untested in the newest vintage and unfilled by any named source. Interpolation across a high inflation hole is possible arithmetic and is not defensible as a price.
6. Sugar beet price concept is a forced decision. **Closed by this sweep as a record.** Both concepts are now documented to their limits: farm gate to 2023, factory procurement complete to an announced 2026 to 2027 price of 2,300 per tonne. The modelling choice itself remains the model decision the foundation said it was.
7. National yields and areas ride on the Our World in Data mirror; direct FAOSTAT crops pull outstanding. **Genuinely open in this sweep, as a retrieval debt, not a data absence.** The bulk route is proven and the mirror is unchanged; the direct pull was outside the five targets and remains a defined next job.
8. No CAPMAS branded governorate table obtained for any year. **Genuinely open.** Recent CAPMAS crop bulletins exist only as press releases with national totals in every route searched. All governorate data in hand remains Ministry branded, and that provenance is now stronger, not weaker, after Target 4.
9. Governorate coverage by year is partial. **Closed by this sweep in part; remainder genuinely open.** Winter 2024 to 2025 and summer 2021 to 2022 were added first hand. Still missing: winter 2021 to 2022, both seasons of 2022 to 2023, summer 2023 to 2024 and summer 2024 to 2025, with the missing editions behind the Ministry library login.
10. Menoufia rice for 2020 to 2021 not reported. **Genuinely open.** The cell is an absence in its own source table. Nothing in this sweep fills it, and an estimate from neighbouring years would be a labelled computation, not a recovery of the figure.
11. Nili maize governorate tables located but not extracted (2020 to 2021 bulletin, Tables 27 to 31). **Closable by computation.** The tables are located, and the 2021 to 2022 edition found in this sweep carries the same Nili structure. Extraction is a defined processing job.
12. CAPMAS 2010 to 2011 bulletin governorate tables not extracted. **Closable by computation.** Same position as gap 11: a located document and a defined extraction, outside the target window in any case.
13. No source goes below governorate level. **Closable by computation.** WaPOR Level 2 rasters at 100 metres support within governorate zonal statistics today; the mixed pixel caveat for small Delta holdings governs how far below governorate the results can be trusted, and governorate and season remains the defensible resolution.
14. No extractable year by year Nile flow table after 1970. **Genuinely open.** Nothing in this sweep found one. The records that run later exist as figures and model inputs, and a year by year series would require the Deltares reconstruction or ministry yearbooks, neither public in extractable form.
15. The 1978 to 1987 drought known only as period means. **Genuinely open.** Its year by year shape remains unavailable in published form.
16. Cobb series year definition and naturalisation status undocumented. **Genuinely open.** A documentation question about a 1978 source; this sweep found nothing that settles it, and the series must keep its gauged flow label.
17. Published long term Aswan means range from 84.1 to 93.2 with no reconciling series. **Genuinely open.** The 2025 National Water Balance does not reconcile Aswan means. Any single expected flow remains a named choice.
18. Official Egyptian water totals disagree (59.68 against 59.25). **Closed by this sweep, by supersession.** The National Water Balance for 2025 is published in full inside the MWRI Allocation Framework of August 2026: 65.35 billion cubic metres fresh and 88.55 including reuse, with components. The old pair was never reconciled; it was overtaken by a newer balance on a stated wider definition, and the five official totals now on record differ almost entirely in groundwater and desalination counting.
19. No single consistent source for applied requirements for all five crops; applied figures second hand. **Closed by this sweep for four crops; genuinely open for potatoes.** Field measured applied water and consumptive use, read in the original, now exist for wheat, rice, maize and sugar beet under Delta conditions (Target 2). No Delta clay soil potato duty exists in the journals searched. The satellite route in Target 3 also offers a measured consumption alternative to applied tables altogether.
20. Gabr (2022) Delta wheat figures internally inconsistent; no clean CROPWAT wheat number. **Closed by this sweep in effect.** Measured Sakha wheat water relations (Target 2) replace the need for the Gabr figure, and a CROPWAT Delta set exists in Swelam and El Marsafawy (2019). The internal inconsistency inside Gabr itself remains unexplained and the figure should not be reused.
21. Sugar beet applied water unresolved between 9,426 and 3,927 per hectare. **Closed by this sweep.** Five Sakha trials and two modelled sets converge on applied 4,940 to 7,500 per hectare; both foundation extremes fall outside every Egyptian source found. A defensible duty is about 5,600 to 6,600 per hectare, documented against named trials.
22. No crop or Delta specific irrigation efficiency; one Egypt wide anchor of about 50 percent. **Closable by computation.** No efficiency series exists, and that part stays open. But the conversion the anchor served is no longer forced: satellite consumptive use removes it on the consumption side (Target 3), and Kafr El Sheikh canal measurements bound the diversion side (conveyance above 90 percent, field level about 70 percent claimed, field application 69 to 77 percent surface and 88 to 94 percent drip in trials).
23. FAO Paper 33 seasonal yield response factor for rice unverified. **Genuinely open.** Nothing in this sweep reached the 1979 original. The model value of 1.20 still stands unverified and still needs that check before reuse.
24. No official water budget for the eight governorate model area; the 11.00 billion cubic metre budget is a calibration. **Closable by computation on the consumption side; genuinely open on the release side.** WaPOR zonal totals can now produce a measured Delta consumption series for 2018 to 2025, and the CAPMAS irrigation bulletin adds a national three node series for 2020 to 2024. No Delta release series exists in any source found, so a measured release budget counterpart does not exist to compute from.
25. Potatoes are three crops in the sources; the model carries one parameter set. **Closable by computation.** The season split data now exists in hand: summer and Nili potato costs separately in the Ministry cost bulletin, and all season potato tables confirmed present in the 2021 to 2022 statistics bulletin. Splitting the parameter set is an extraction and modelling job on located documents.

## Closing note on method

Two findings in this sweep carry a general lesson the next pass should keep. The largest single find, the Ministry cost bulletin, sat in a public catalogue the whole time, under its Arabic title, in the ministry uploads index; it was missed because the first pass searched for the CAPMAS republication and stopped. And the two strongest closures after it, WaPOR and the field trial literature, both came from following the institutions own platforms (the FAO catalogue API and the EKB journals platform) rather than from general web search. The remaining hard core is small and specific: costs and prices for 2023 onward in official form, the missing bulletin seasons behind a login, post 1970 flows in table form, and one FAO factor from 1979. Everything else on the original list of 25 is either closed, or has a named computation or a named route standing against it.
