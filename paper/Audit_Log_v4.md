# Independent audit of the Nile Delta allocation model, version 4

Audit only, written 14 November 2026. I did not build version 4. I read the governing spec in docs/FOUNDATIONS.md, the results note in paper/Results_Note_v4.md, the model code in src/allocation_model_v4.py, and the four run logs, and I attacked the build at the standard set by docs/GPT_AUDIT_v3.md. Every number below comes from my own code under data/processed/audit_v4_work/, loading the raw CSV files in data/external/ and data/processed/forecast_prices_yields_v4.csv, with my own formulation and my own solver settings. Water in my formulation is carried in cubic metres; the builder carries thousands of cubic metres. Where a plan is fixed, my binary linkage uses the planted area of the cell itself as its bound; the builder uses the district physical area. I did not import the model file anywhere in my audit code, and no solution value from it is used as evidence anywhere below.

## 1. Verdict

The arithmetic of version 4 is the strongest part of the build and it largely survives an independent reconstruction. The reference tonnage, the frontier margins at 0, 50 and 60 percent, the wait and see value in all twelve scenarios, the true mean plan evaluation and its penalised variant, the stress pairings, the baseline margin and the ceiling all reproduce, several of them to the pound. The six version 3 defects named in the foundation are repaired in form: land binds by season, abandonment is a real decision with the three properties the foundation requires, benchmarks use true means, the frontier is priced against the unconstrained optimum and against the next lower level, and stress results pair capacity with choice.

The build is not yet sendable, for two reasons that sit below the arithmetic. First, the reserve construction fails a basic consistency test: the historical planting pattern itself does not fit inside the seasonal land limits the construction produces, in seven of the sixteen governorate pools. A land layer that excludes the observed pattern it is benchmarked against needs repair or a stated defence before any plan it produces is presented as plantable. Second, the headline ceiling of 75.45 percent is exact arithmetic for the stated model, but it is conditional on two choices the note under reports: holding the full annual unmodeled area back as a reserve, without which the ceiling is 88.47 percent, and scaling district yields to the forecast national level, without which the ceiling is 80.12 percent on the published yields the foundation named as the primary basis. The note reports neither bound. Major revision of the land layer and of the ceiling presentation is required; the rest of the note needs only the small corrections listed below.

## 2. Errors found, largest first

**Error 1. The reserve construction excludes the historical pattern it benchmarks against, and the ceiling is materially a reserve level result.**

I replayed the construction exactly as coded. Annual unmodeled area per governorate is annual cropped area minus total observed modeled area, split between the winter and summer pools in proportion to observed modeled area in each pool, and each pool limit is physical cultivated area minus its reserve share. The arithmetic of that construction reproduces to the hectare. Its consistency does not. The observed modeled area exceeds the pool limit in seven of the sixteen pools: Dakahlia summer by 16,557 hectares, Kafr El Sheikh winter by 11,084 hectares, Menoufia summer by 10,933 hectares, Qalyubia summer by 7,031 hectares, Gharbia summer by 2,597 hectares, and Damietta winter by 636 hectares and summer by 452 hectares. In those pools the construction reserves more land for unmodeled crops than the physical area leaves behind the observed modeled crops, which cannot be true of the season it describes. The baseline evaluation in the note is still computable because a fixed plan evaluation never checks the land limits, but the statement that the historical pattern holds or fails a guarantee inside this land layer is made against limits that the pattern itself violates.

On materiality, the split rule is not the problem and I say so plainly: the ceiling is not mainly a reserve split artefact. Under an equal split of the annual unmodeled area the ceiling is 9,226,677 tonnes, 75.29 percent. Under a reserve capped so that the baseline pattern fits, the ceiling is 9,266,626 tonnes, 75.62 percent. Under a three pool construction with the Nili season separate, the ceiling is unchanged at 75.45 percent. What moves the ceiling is the existence and size of the reserve itself. With no reserve at all, pool limits equal to physical area, the ceiling is 10,841,146 tonnes, 88.47 percent, identical to removing the land family in my relaxation runs. The honest presentation is a band: 75.45 percent under the stated reserve construction, up to 88.47 percent with no reserve held, with the construction and its baseline inconsistency printed beside the headline.

**Error 2. The primary yields are scaled to a national figure, the reference is not, and the unscaled ceiling is not reported.**

The note states the construction correctly in one sentence and then denies it in the next. District yields in the model are the published district yields scaled per crop so that the area weighted district mean equals the forecast national yield. My replayed scale factors are 0.9728 for wheat, 0.9993 for rice, 0.8743 for summer maize, 1.1354 for sugar beet, 1.1451 for winter potatoes, 0.9639 for summer potatoes, 0.9193 for Nili maize and 1.7393 for Nili potatoes. The reference tonnage in the denominator of every floor share is built from the unscaled published yields. The ceiling ratio therefore mixes two bases: a numerator produced at scaled yields and a denominator stated at published yields. Rebuilding the ceiling on published yields throughout, with the forecast band ratios kept, gives 9,818,690 tonnes, 80.12 percent, which is 572,425 tonnes and 4.67 points above the headline. The foundation named published district yields as the primary basis and rescaling as a sensitivity. The note reports a sensitivity in the other direction, the version 3 rescaled basis at 69.62 percent, which I verified, and never reports the unscaled basis. The sentence in Section 1 of the note saying that no rescaling to national means is used in the primary run should be withdrawn: scaling district yields so that their mean equals the forecast national yield is a rescaling to a national figure, and the paper should say which national figure and why.

**Error 3. Summer and Nili crops are pooled into one land pool, against the seasonal structure the foundation specifies.**

The foundation carries potatoes as three seasonal crops, winter, summer and Nili, and binds land by season. The code places rice, summer maize, Nili maize, summer potatoes and Nili potatoes in a single summer pool with a single limit. A field that carries a summer crop and then a Nili crop is counted twice against one season limit, so the pool is stricter than the physical season it names. Part of the baseline overflow in Error 1 comes from this pooling: under the three pool construction, the Dakahlia summer overflow of 16,557 hectares becomes slack of 15,550 hectares and the Gharbia summer overflow becomes slack of 3,539 hectares. Five pools still fail under three pools, Kafr El Sheikh winter, Menoufia summer, Qalyubia summer and both Damietta pools, so pooling is not the whole of Error 1. The ceiling effect of the pooling is nil on my test, which is why this error ranks below the first two, but every planting plan the model prints is shaped by the pooled limit and the note describes the pool without naming the Nili merge as a deviation from the foundation.

**Error 4. The baseline pattern mixes years for potatoes, and the note does not say so.**

Observed areas for wheat, sugar beet, rice and summer maize are the 2020 to 2021 panel. Potato areas in all three seasons come from the season split file, whose agricultural year is 2021 to 2022 throughout; I verified the file carries no other year. The code comment states the choice; the results note never does. The mix enters the reserve construction, the land limits, the baseline margin and the absorption denominators. It does not enter the reference tonnage, which uses staples only. A build that was criticised in version 3 for mixing economic years should not mix cropping years in silence in version 4.

**Error 5. The robustness run freezes summer planting at the normal inflow variant for every sample, and its distribution cannot be recomputed from the saved files.**

The fixed plan saved in data/processed/plan_v4_robust_plan.csv carries four summer planting variants, one per inflow state. The robustness evaluation loads the plan and evaluates every sample with the variant for the normal inflow state, because the evaluation is run as a single scenario problem for that state. My replication confirms this is what the code does: drawing the same sequence with seed 20261114, alternating single years and ten year window means, and evaluating the fixed plan my way reproduced all nine sample values printed in the robust log to the tonne, and fourteen samples in total without a miss. The choice is defensible, since a fixed plan must commit to one summer pattern, but neither the note nor the log states it, and the ceiling of the fixed plan across samples, 72.41 percent, is exactly the staple limit of that one variant at full water. Separately, the per sample results are not saved anywhere; only the plan is saved. The distribution summary, the worst decile shortfall of 663,137 tonnes, the mean shortfall of 75,371 tonnes and the 82 percent hold share therefore cannot be recomputed from the files the note points to. That is an audit limitation of the build, recorded here and again in Section 5.

**Error 6. Two water value ranges in Section 5 of the note are misstated or selective.**

The dual conversion itself is correct; see the grading list. The ranges quoted around it are not all correct. For the high inflow state at expected prices, the note quotes about 10 to 11 pounds per cubic metre where water binds at all. The log values for that scenario are 10.36, 7.86, 11.16, 11.32, zero, 10.67, zero and 10.05. Sharkia binds at 7.86 and sits outside the quoted range; the true range where water binds is 7.86 to 11.32. For the normal inflow state at expected prices, the note quotes about 38 to 54 pounds in the six governorates where water binds hardest. Those six values do span 37.61 to 53.91, but Qalyubia at 9.26 and Damietta at 11.58 also bind, and a reader is not told that two binding governorates sit far below the quoted range. The dry inflow ranges are quoted correctly: 44.19 to 56.56 at expected prices, stated as 44 to 57, and 777.73 to 920.25 at low prices, stated as 778 to 920.

**Error 7. The 70 percent plan sits exactly on the floor in its hardest scenario, and the code as written is numerically fragile in a way the note does not disclose.**

For the saved 70 percent plan, the maximum staple tonnage attainable in the dry inflow state at low prices is 8,578,085.1 tonnes against a floor reference of 8,578,085 tonnes. The plan holds its hardest scenario by about a tenth of a tonne. In my own solves, floored recourse problems for this plan returned Infeasible at a floor of exactly 0.700 and Optimal at 0.699, a tolerance artefact on a knife edge, while the max staple runs prove the floor is attainable. Any reader who replicates the evaluation with slightly different tolerances can watch the plan flip between feasible and infeasible, and the note gives no warning of this. The related fragility is in the binary linkage bound. The builder code bounds kept area with the district physical area, up to 387,541 hectares, against planted cells as small as 7.98 hectares. In my first replication pass with that same bound, CBC returned recourse solutions labelled Optimal that understated the baseline margin by 1.84 billion pounds and understated high scenario staple tonnage by up to about 291,000 tonnes; with a tight per cell bound the same problems reproduce the published figures. The published numbers match the tight solves, so no published figure is shown wrong by this, but the formulation as coded sits in the fragile zone the builder reported for water scaling, and replication will silently diverge for anyone who copies the bound.

**Error 8. The penalised value of the stochastic solution is one pound short against its own printed components.**

The recourse value 194,764,695,263 minus the penalised expected value result 187,415,904,232 equals 7,348,791,031. The note and the log print 7,348,791,030. My own penalised evaluation gives an unrounded difference of 7,348,791,030.6, so the printed figure is a truncation where the printed components round the other way. Immaterial in scale, and exactly the class of one pound inconsistency the version 3 audit flagged; it should still be made consistent.

**Error 9. The duals were computed at a floor of 75.35 percent and are described as 75 percent.**

The log computes the relaxation at the ceiling of 75.4526 percent minus 0.001, that is 75.35 percent, and prints the level as 75 percent; the note repeats 75 percent. My own relaxation at 0.753526 gives an objective of 159,182,433,648 against the log value of 159,182,779,221, a difference of 0.0002 percent, so the dual table itself is the table it claims to be. The label should carry the second decimal or say about 75 percent.

## 3. What the attacks established, attack by attack

**Land and reserve.** Covered in Errors 1 and 3. Physical cultivated area replays at 1,676,580.36 hectares and annual cropped area at 3,176,473.86 hectares, matching the note. Observed modeled totals are 905,415 hectares in the winter pool and 921,514 hectares in the pooled summer pool. The reserve construction arithmetic is exact; its consistency and its level are the findings.

**Ceiling and its binding family.** My own maximisation of staple tonnage held in every scenario, built from the raw panels, gives 9,246,264.9 tonnes in the linear form and the identical value in the binary form, 75.4526 percent of the reference. The 80 percent floor is infeasible in both forms. Relaxing one family at a time, in the linear form: removing seasonal land gives 10,841,146 tonnes, 88.47 percent; tripling dry inflow water gives 10,039,384 tonnes, 81.92 percent; tripling all water gives 15,136,676 tonnes, 123.52 percent, where land binds instead; dropping the drought state from the minimum gives no change at all, 75.45 percent; dropping the dry state gives the same value as tripling dry water, 81.92 percent; holding the minimum in the expected price layer only gives 9,551,899 tonnes, 77.95 percent; removing the minimum viable water share gives no change. Maximising expected staple tonnage instead of the every scenario minimum gives an expected 11,566,662 tonnes, 94.39 percent, whose own minimum scenario is the same 75.45 percent. The ceiling is therefore bound jointly by seasonal land as constructed and by dry scenario water, then by the low yield layer at 2.50 points; the drought state and the minimum share bind nothing at the ceiling. The ceiling solution plants no rice and no sugar beet in any inflow state, which is why the sugar beet duty across 5,600 to 6,600 and the rice response factor across 1.00 to 1.40 cannot move it, as the note claims. With the named caps the ceiling is 8,842,923.9 tonnes, 72.1612 percent, in both my forms, matching the capped log.

**Abandonment.** In my own solve at the 70 percent floor I find sixteen abandoned cells. Three examples, each verified separately for the three properties. Kafr El Sheikh winter potatoes in the dry inflow state at low prices: planted 151,345.6 hectares, kept area zero, water exactly zero, output exactly zero, planting cost of 13,115,177,714 pounds still charged in the objective. Dakahlia winter potatoes in the same scenario: planted 80,712.0 hectares, water zero, output zero, cost 6,994,275,157 pounds charged. Kafr El Sheikh rice in the dry inflow state at expected prices: planted 30,322.4 hectares, water zero, output zero, cost 1,268,991,017 pounds charged. Production is defined on kept area only in the formulation, so the yield response relation is never applied to abandoned area, and the zero output values confirm it in the solution. Across the 257 kept cells in that solve, the smallest water share is exactly 0.3000, which is the minimum viable share the code and the note state. In the unconstrained solve nothing is abandoned and every kept share is 1.0.

**Frontier costs.** Every cost in the primary frontier table recomputes to the pound from the log margins: at 50 percent, 139,422,516,002 against the unconstrained optimum and the same against the next lower level; at 60 percent, 185,917,383,785 and 46,494,867,783; at 70 percent, 234,824,085,110 and 48,906,701,325. My own solves reproduce the margins themselves to the pound at 0, 50 and 60 percent, and bracket the 70 percent margin: my feasible solve gives 194,759,840,596, the published 194,764,695,263 sits above it by 4,854,667, and a recourse bound with the floor relaxed to 69.9 percent gives 194,935,604,260 above the published value, so the published margin is optimal within 0.003 percent on my evidence. The capped margins at 0 percent reproduce to the pound in my solve; the capped 70 percent margin in my solve is 169,269,111,549, which is 1,808,356 above the published 169,267,303,193, so the published capped figure is very slightly below the optimum, by 0.0011 percent, inside solver tolerance for this problem class and worth a footnote only.

**Benchmarks and information values.** The probability weighted true mean budget from the scenario table is 10.9844 billion cubic metres by my replay, from budgets of 8.588, 10.502, 13.434 and 9.580 billion cubic metres at probabilities 0.2275, 0.4451, 0.3165 and 0.0110. True mean prices by my replay are 10,761 pounds per tonne for wheat, 852 for sugar beet, 8,641 for potatoes, 20,398 for rice and 15,824 for maize, matching the log. My own expected value problem at those true means produces a plan whose maximum staple evaluation fails the 70 percent floor in exactly 10 of the 12 scenarios and holds in the normal inflow state at high prices and the high inflow state at high prices, as the note reports. The penalised evaluation of that plan reproduces the log shortfalls scenario by scenario, for example 2,718,226 tonnes in the dry inflow state at low prices and zero in the high inflow state at high prices, and gives the penalised expected result to the pound. The penalty of 25,000 pounds per tonne is stated in the note and the log, both penalised figures carry the word penalised in the note, and the note nowhere presents a standard value of the stochastic solution; it states that none exists. That discipline is correct.

**Solver integrity.** With water carried in cubic metres rather than thousands, and at both loose and zero relative gap settings, my objectives agree with the published ones as follows: identical to the pound for the unconstrained frontier level, the 50 and 60 percent levels, the capped unconstrained level, the winter potato cost sensitivity at 462,413,066,422, the wait and see value in each of the twelve scenarios, and the stress margins; within one pound at the 0.80 stress scale; within 0.003 percent at the 70 percent frontier level; and identical between the linear and binary forms for both ceilings. On my evidence the published objectives are optimal within a tolerance of 0.003 percent, with the single exception of the capped 70 percent margin, where the published value is 0.0011 percent below my feasible solve and therefore also within that tolerance band.

**Stress pairing.** At budget scale 0.90 my capacity run gives 8,560,095.3 tonnes and at 0.80 it gives 7,873,925.6 tonnes, matching the published 8,560,095 and 7,873,926. In my own margin maximisation at both scales, expected staple tonnage is exactly zero and planted staple area is exactly zero for wheat, rice and summer maize, in the binary form and in the linear relaxation. The zero is a genuine optimum of the profit objective in this parameter set, driven by potato returns, not a floor or sign artefact: no floor is present in these runs, and the capacity runs beside them prove the same budgets can hold 69.85 and 64.25 percent of the reference tonnage when staples are the objective.

**Robustness.** Covered in Error 5. The sampling construction as logged, 100 samples alternating random single years and random ten year window means with seed 20261114 and budgets at 11.00 times the drawn ratio, is what the code does. My fourteen sample test reproduced every printed sample value to the tonne. The worst decile figure of 663,137 tonnes against the floor reference of 8,578,085 tonnes, which itself replays exactly as 70 percent of the reference, cannot be recomputed because the per sample results are not saved.

**Wording and discipline.** The floor is described in the note exactly as the foundation words it: every represented scenario delivers at least the stated share of a modeled reference quantity of aggregate wheat, paddy rice and summer maize tonnage. No average return is called a price anywhere in the note. Duals are labelled LP relaxation duals. The historical comparison is labelled accounting only and carries no causal verb on any input. Every forecast in Sections 10 and 12 of the note is quoted with its backtest error, and all ten forecast values, errors and percentage errors match data/processed/forecast_prices_yields_v4.csv and docs/FORECASTING_RESULTS.md. The beet variant carries the warning that its two runs differ in price concept, year and marketing basis together and attributes nothing to the price concept alone. Absorption warnings appear in the logs at every frontier level where a crop passes twice its observed area, and Section 4 of the note prints the multiples at the unconstrained and 70 percent levels and attaches the warning to every margin in the note. The contribution claim in Section 13 of the note matches the wording the prior work review supports and claims nothing beyond it. My scan of the note file found no dash character of any kind in it. The failures under this attack are Errors 2, 6 and 9 only: the rescaling sentence, the two dual ranges, and the dual floor label.

**WaPOR.** The modeled baseline demand replays at 16.398 billion cubic metres and the WaPOR mean total at 24.416 billion cubic metres over 2018 to 2025, with the modeled figure below the WaPOR total in each of the eight governorates, as the note states. The note and the log both state that WaPOR measures total consumption over all surfaces with no cropland mask, both use it as validation only, and no scalar derived from WaPOR appears anywhere in the water duties, which I traced to the applied water values in the source files. This part of the build is clean.

## 5. What I could not verify, and why

**The robustness distribution in full.** The per sample staple tonnages and shortfalls are not saved in any file; the robust log prints nine samples and the summary statistics. I verified the sampling construction, the fixed plan evaluation method and fourteen samples to the tonne, which supports the method and finds no contradiction, but the median, the tenth percentile, the minimum, the worst decile mean and the hold share cannot be recomputed without the hundred values. Saving the per sample table in the run would close this.

**The historical solver claim.** The builder reports that CBC returned suboptimal solutions labelled Optimal until water was rescaled to thousands of cubic metres. No run from before the rescaling is saved, so the claim itself cannot be tested. What can be tested is the published output, and Section 3 reports that test: the published objectives are optimal within 0.003 percent under my different scaling.

**A second solver engine.** The project venv carries PuLP with CBC and scipy, but no HiGHS module is installed, so I could not re solve the problems in a second engine. The mitigation in my work is that the linear and binary forms of both ceiling problems agree to a tenth of a tonne, that two water scalings and two binary linkage bounds were run against each other, and that the loose bound failure in Error 7 was caught and corrected by exactly that comparison. A HiGHS or commercial solver pass remains a worthwhile final check on the 70 percent frontier margin, where my bracket is widest at 0.003 percent.

**The capped frontier margins at 50 and 60 percent as solves.** I re solved the capped run at 0 and 70 percent and verified the middle levels as arithmetic on the log margins. Nothing in the pattern suggests they would fail, but they are graded on log arithmetic, not on my own solves.

**Source authenticity.** As with the version 3 audit, my replay establishes that the code and the logs trace to the CSV files in the project, not that those files faithfully transcribe the ministry bulletins, the FAOSTAT domains or the WaPOR rasters they name. The original publications were not before me.

**The dual table cell by cell from my own relaxation.** I verified the relaxation objective at the stated floor, the conversion from raw to conditional values across all 89 nonzero cells, and every range the note quotes, two of which fail as recorded in Error 6. I did not re derive all 96 dual values from my own relaxation, so individual cells beyond the quoted ranges rest on the log.

The strongest part of version 4 is that almost every number in it can be rebuilt by a stranger from the saved files, and this audit rebuilt them. The work that remains is not arithmetic. It is to make the land layer consistent with the pattern it benchmarks, to print the ceiling with the two bounds that define it, and to save the robustness detail so the next audit does not have to take the tail of the distribution on trust.
