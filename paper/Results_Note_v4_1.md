# Results note, version 4.1, repair build

I rebuilt the allocation model a second time, repairing the faults the independent audit of version 4 found, and ran it in full. The one line a reader should take away is this: in this model a staple guarantee can be held up to 80.25 percent of the reference tonnage under the reserve construction stated below and on published district yields, each step up the ladder has a price in farm margin that I can state level by level, every floored plan holds its floor with zero clearance in its hardest scenario, and the profit objective on its own chooses no staples at all under stress. Every margin in this note is on one basis: forecast prices at the 2024 level with official 2022 costs, in pounds, and the same basis applies to both sides of every comparison.

## 1. What I repaired

* The reserve is rebuilt so that it admits the pattern it benchmarks against. The audit of version 4 replayed my reserve construction and found that the observed historical planting pattern did not fit inside the seasonal land limits in seven of sixteen pools. The new construction has two steps, and I state both. First, the annual unmodeled crop area of each governorate, its annual cropped area minus the total observed modeled area, is split across the three season pools in proportion to the observed modeled area in each pool. Second, each pool reserve is capped at the physical area that the observed modeled pattern leaves free in that pool, because in the observed season the unmodeled crops demonstrably fitted into that free area, so a larger reserve would contradict the season it describes. The pool limit is the physical cultivated area minus its capped reserve, and no pool limit can fall below the observed modeled area of that pool. The cap binds in five pools: Kafr El Sheikh winter, Menoufia summer, Qalyubia summer, and Damietta winter and summer, where the limit equals the observed modeled area exactly. The main run log prints an acceptance test for this construction, the observed modeled area against the pool limit in all 24 governorate pools, and the result is 24 of 24 pools pass.
* Yields are the published district yields throughout. Version 4 scaled district yields so their area weighted mean equalled the forecast national yield, while its reference tonnage used the unscaled yields, so its ceiling ratio mixed two bases. In this build the expected yield state is the published district yield itself, the low and high states apply the forecast band ratios to the published yields, and the reference tonnage uses the same published yields. The scaled construction survives only as a labelled sensitivity in Section 3.
* Land binds in three season pools, winter, summer and Nili. Version 4 pooled the summer and Nili crops into one pool, which counted a field that carries a summer crop and then a Nili crop twice against one season limit.
* The baseline years are stated here in plain words, not only in a code comment. The baseline pattern mixes two cropping years. Areas for wheat, sugar beet, rice, summer maize and Nili maize come from the 2020 to 2021 bulletin panel. Potato areas in all three seasons come from the 2021 to 2022 season split file, because that split exists on disk for that year only. One rice cell, Menoufia, comes from the 2019 to 2020 panel, because its own source leaves the 2020 to 2021 cell blank. The potato year mix enters the reserve construction, the land limits, the baseline margin and the market absorption denominators. It does not enter the reference tonnage, which uses staples only. The single Menoufia rice cell does enter the reference tonnage, as one cell of one crop.
* The robustness run now saves every sample result to a file, data/processed/robustness_samples_v4_1.csv, so the distribution in Section 8 can be recomputed from the file, and it states its summer planting convention in the log and in Section 8 of this note.
* Two numerical faults of my own making were found and fixed during this build, and I record them rather than bury them. First, the solver tolerance. I set the CBC primal and dual feasibility tolerance to 0.000000001, tighter than the default of 0.0000001, and at that setting CBC presolve falsely proved a feasible fixed plan infeasible. The build now runs at the default tolerance of 0.0000001 with relative gap zero, and no floor verdict in this note rests on a solver status read alone: floors above the ceiling are infeasible by the ceiling maximisation, and every floored plan carries a clearance maximisation of its own. Second, the linkage bound in fixed plan evaluations. A plan taken from a solver can sit a fraction of a hectare above a pool limit inside solver tolerance, and bounding its recourse linkage by the pool limit then makes its own recourse infeasible, which is what my first clearance and benchmark evaluations hit. Fixed plans are now bounded by the planted area of the cell itself in their evaluations, which is exact. Plans being optimised keep the pool limit as their bound.
* The dual table is labelled with the exact floor it was computed at, the water value ranges in Section 5 are the full ranges across every binding governorate, and the penalised value of the stochastic solution in Section 6 is the exact difference of its two printed components.

## 2. The reference quantity and the floor

The floor is exactly this: every represented scenario delivers at least the stated share of the modeled reference quantity of aggregate wheat, paddy rice and summer maize tonnage. The reference quantity is built from observed areas times published district yields and comes to 12,254,407 tonnes: wheat 4,617,795 tonnes, paddy rice 4,086,080 tonnes and summer maize 3,550,533 tonnes. The model and the reference now stand on the same yield basis. The floor says nothing about each crop on its own, about calories, or about milled rice.

## 3. The guarantee frontier, primary run, no crop caps

* Floor 0 percent (unconstrained): expected margin 403,078,762,407 pounds.
* Floor 50 percent: expected margin 297,739,198,932 pounds. Cost against the unconstrained optimum 105,339,563,475 pounds. Cost against the next lower level 105,339,563,475 pounds.
* Floor 60 percent: expected margin 262,520,406,731 pounds. Cost against the unconstrained optimum 140,558,355,676 pounds. Cost against the next lower level 35,218,792,200 pounds.
* Floor 70 percent: expected margin 223,222,240,454 pounds. Cost against the unconstrained optimum 179,856,521,953 pounds. Cost against the next lower level 39,298,166,277 pounds.
* Floor 75 percent: expected margin 202,983,384,826 pounds. Cost against the unconstrained optimum 200,095,377,581 pounds. Cost against the next lower level 20,238,855,628 pounds.
* Floor 80 percent: expected margin 175,143,528,384 pounds. Cost against the unconstrained optimum 227,935,234,023 pounds. Cost against the next lower level 27,839,856,443 pounds.
* Floors of 85, 90, 95 and 100 percent: infeasible.

A separate capacity run that maximises the tonnage held in every scenario puts the exact ceiling at 9,833,867 tonnes, which is 80.25 percent of the reference quantity. I print the ceiling with its conditions, because the audit showed it moves when they move. The 80.25 percent rests on the reserve construction of Section 1, with its cap rule, and on the published district yields. With no reserve held at all, pool limits equal to physical cultivated area, the ceiling is 11,144,080 tonnes, which is 90.94 percent. On the labelled sensitivity that scales district yields to the forecast national level, the version 4 construction, the ceiling is 9,266,626 tonnes, which is 75.62 percent. The ceiling is a result about this stated model, with its budgets, duties, yields and land formulation, and I do not claim that the Delta itself cannot protect more.

On clearance, I report the headroom of each plan plainly. For every floored plan I maximised staple tonnage in each of the twelve scenarios in turn, with the planting fixed. At every level the smallest headroom sits in the dry inflow state at low prices and low yields, and it is zero tonnes, zero to within a tenth of a tonne. A margin optimal plan at a binding floor is exactly tight in its hardest scenario: the promise carries no slack there, and any reader of a floor in this note should hold that fact beside it. The full clearance table is printed in the main run log.

With the named constraints added (rotation and market sanity limits, a modelling device: wheat 0.50, rice 0.35, maize 0.40, sugar beet 0.30 and potatoes 0.15 of district physical land), the ceiling falls to 9,490,156 tonnes, which is 77.44 percent of the reference quantity. The capped ladder gives margins of 203,702,111,599 pounds unconstrained, 197,178,572,288 at 50 percent, 188,203,914,415 at 60 percent, 174,128,054,485 at 70 percent and 163,615,562,282 at 75 percent, with 80 percent and above infeasible. Feasibility is therefore reported with and without the named constraints, as the foundation requires.

Two controlled sensitivities did not move the ceiling at all: the sugar beet duty across its arbitrated range of 5,600 to 6,600 cubic metres per hectare (the primary run uses the midpoint 6,100), and the rice yield response factor across 1.00 to 1.40 (the primary run carries 1.20, labelled unverified).

## 4. Market absorption warning

The unconstrained optimum rests on potatoes at a scale where the fixed price stops being credible. Against observed areas it plants winter potatoes at 16.91 times, summer potatoes at 22.69 times and Nili potatoes at 366.22 times. At the 80 percent floor the multiples are still 9.32 times for summer potatoes and 97.76 times for Nili potatoes. I read every margin in this note with that warning attached: the model prices potatoes as if any area could sell at the forecast price, and no reader should believe that at these multiples.

## 5. Water values

Water values are marginal duals from the linear relaxation of the same formulation at a floor of 80.15 percent, the ceiling of 80.25 percent minus a tenth of a point, and I label them with that exact floor. I label them LP relaxation duals and nothing else. Inside a scenario, one more cubic metre is worth, in the dry inflow state at expected prices, between 47.39 and 64.32 pounds across the eight governorates, and water binds in all eight. In the normal inflow state at expected prices the range is 19.17 to 53.54 pounds, again with water binding in all eight governorates, Damietta the lowest at 19.17 and Sharkia the highest at 53.54. In the high inflow state at expected prices the range is 11.81 to 14.64 pounds, all eight binding. In the dry inflow state at low prices the relaxation duals spike to between 1,073.50 and 1,240.34 pounds per cubic metre in every governorate. That spike is a property of the relaxation at a near ceiling floor in the hardest scenario, and I print it because it is in the log, not because I read it as a price anyone would pay. The full table of 96 governorate and scenario cells, and a range summary that lists every governorate value for the expected and low layers of each inflow state, are in the main run log. No average return is called a price anywhere in this note.

## 6. Information values against true means

The true mean budget, probability weighted across the four inflow states, is 10.9844 billion cubic metres. I computed it from the state budgets and their probabilities; it is not the middle scenario. At the stated floor of 80 percent, the highest feasible ladder level, the recourse problem value is 175,143,528,384 pounds and the wait and see value is 198,927,633,193 pounds, so the expected value of perfect information is 23,784,104,809 pounds.

The expected value problem, planned at the true means of budget, prices and yields, gives a finding I report plainly: that plan cannot hold the 80 percent floor in 10 of the 12 combined scenarios. It holds only in the normal inflow state at high prices and in the high inflow state at high prices. A standard value of the stochastic solution therefore does not exist for this benchmark, and I do not report one. Evaluated with no floor, the true mean plan reaches 194,969,660,805 pounds, which is an upper bound that flatters it and not an expected result under the guarantee. Under a named penalised variant, with a penalty of 25,000 pounds per tonne of staple shortfall, its penalised expected result is 167,535,664,790 pounds and the penalised value of the stochastic solution is 7,607,863,594 pounds, which is the exact difference of the two printed figures above it. Both penalised figures carry the word penalised wherever they travel.

## 7. Stress runs, capacity beside choice

* Budgets scaled to 0.90, no floor: maximum staple capacity is 9,049,077 tonnes held in every scenario, 73.84 percent of the reference quantity. The profit objective chooses zero staple tonnes, with an expected margin of 375,091,832,795 pounds.
* Budgets scaled to 0.80, no floor: maximum staple capacity is 8,264,287 tonnes, 67.44 percent of the reference quantity. The profit objective chooses zero staple tonnes, with an expected margin of 343,134,636,238 pounds.

The first number in each pair is physics. The second is a choice of the profit objective, and I keep the two apart in every sentence that uses them.

## 8. Sampled robustness

I fixed the plan at the 80 percent floor, the highest feasible ladder level, since the ceiling of 80.25 percent rules out the 85 percent level. The convention of this run is stated here as plainly as in the log: the fixed plan carries four late season planting variants, one per inflow state, and a sampled budget carries no inflow state label, so every sample is evaluated with the late season planting, summer and Nili together, frozen at the normal inflow variant of the fixed plan, and winter planting at its single common variant. The normal variant is the central commitment of the plan. The maximum share across samples is therefore the staple limit of that one variant at a full budget, and the distribution describes that variant, not a plan that chooses its late season pattern again for each sample.

I then drew 100 samples from the inflow record, alternating random single years and random ten year window means with a fixed seed, set each sample budget to 11.00 times its ratio to the record mean, and re solved the recourse for that fixed plan under expected prices and yields, maximising staple tonnage. Every sample result, its ratio, its budget, its maximum staple tonnage, its share and its shortfall, is saved in data/processed/robustness_samples_v4_1.csv, and the figures below recompute from that file. The maximum feasible floor for the fixed plan across samples has a minimum of 55.28 percent, a 10th percentile of 75.08 percent, a median of 82.03 percent, a mean of 80.15 percent and a maximum of 82.67 percent of the reference quantity. The maximum, 10,130,719 tonnes, is the staple limit of the normal inflow variant at a full budget. The fixed plan still holds its 80 percent floor in 65.0 percent of samples. In the worst decile of samples, the expected staple shortfall against the floor reference of 9,803,526 tonnes is 1,103,686 tonnes. The mean shortfall across all samples is 156,385 tonnes.

## 9. The historical pattern, accounting only

On the same price and cost basis, the historical planting pattern earns an expected margin of 86,006,146,997 pounds, delivers expected staple tonnage of 8,908,274 tonnes, about 72.69 percent of the reference quantity, and delivers 7,367,358 tonnes in its worst scenario, about 60.12 percent of the reference quantity, so the historical pattern holds no guarantee in this model. The unconstrained optimised margin is 403,078,762,407 pounds. The accounting difference between the two margins is 317,072,615,410 pounds, 368.66 percent of the baseline margin. This decomposition is accounting only: it has two components, the baseline margin and the optimised margin, and I attach no causal verb to any input. I also attach the absorption warning of Section 4 to the optimised side, because most of that margin sits on potato area the market would not absorb at a fixed price.

## 10. The sugar beet variant

One variant repeats the frontier at the unconstrained, 70, 80 and 90 percent levels with sugar beet at the documented factory procurement price of 2,400 pounds per tonne, all else equal. The margins come out identical to the primary run at every level tried, 403,078,762,407 pounds unconstrained, 223,222,240,454 at 70 percent and 175,143,528,384 at 80 percent, with 90 percent infeasible, because the model plants no sugar beet at either price: potatoes rule beet on both land and water in this parameter set. The two runs differ in price concept, year and marketing basis together, and I attribute nothing about their comparison to the price concept alone. In the primary run beet sits at its forecast farm gate level of 820.52 pounds per tonne, with a backtest root mean squared error of 92.26 pounds and a mean absolute percentage error of 10.21 percent.

## 11. WaPOR calibration verdict

I computed the modeled baseline consumption of the eight governorates as the baseline pattern at full applied duties, which comes to 16.398 billion cubic metres in all, and compared it with the WaPOR totals, which average 24.416 billion cubic metres over 2018 to 2025. The modeled figure sits below the WaPOR total in every governorate, which is the order the two definitions require. I derived no efficiency or requirement scalar from this comparison, because the two sides measure different things: the modeled side covers the modeled crops only on the applied basis, while WaPOR measures total consumption over all surfaces in each governorate, with no cropland mask. WaPOR totals are never cropland consumption in this note. My verdict is that direct calibration is not defensible here and WaPOR serves as validation only.

Yields for 2025: wheat 6.6789 tonnes per hectare (error 0.3250, 4.05 percent); paddy rice 9.1315 (error 0.2696, 2.12 percent); maize 7.6834 (error 0.6912, 6.16 percent); sugar beet 56.0570 (error 5.8925, 6.39 percent); potatoes 31.5165 (error 1.4879, 3.10 percent). ## 13. Standing assumptions and gaps

* The minimum viable water share for a kept crop is 0.30, stated here and in the code.
* The Nili maize duty of 7,433 cubic metres per hectare is printed in the source note of the water file, so I used it rather than the summer duty. No Nili potato duty exists, so Nili potatoes carry the summer potato duty of 6,145, stated as an assumption.
* Nili potato yields are published for Beheira only, at 18.12 tonnes per hectare, and that published value fills the other governorates. The Menoufia rice yield cell and four Nili maize yield cells are filled with the area weighted published mean of their crop, and every fill is printed in the main run log.
* The contribution I claim is the one the prior work review supports: I implement an exploratory governorate scale stochastic allocation model for eight Nile Delta governorates, compare outcomes against historical crop areas, and report staple floor feasibility, conditional expected margins, and scenario specific water duals. I claim nothing beyond that combination.
