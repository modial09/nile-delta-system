# Independent audit of the Nile Delta allocation model, version 4.1 repair build

Audit only, written 14 November 2026. I did not build version 4.1. I read the governing spec in docs/FOUNDATIONS.md, the results note in paper/Results_Note_v4_1.md, the model code in src/allocation_model_v4_1.py, the four run logs, the saved plans and the saved robustness samples, and I attacked the build at the standard set by the version 4 audit. Every number below comes from my own code under data/processed/audit_v4_1_work/, loading the raw CSV files in data/external/ and data/processed/forecast_prices_yields_v4.csv, with my own formulation and my own solver settings. Water in my formulation is carried in cubic metres; the builder carries thousands of cubic metres, and I ran a second form of my own code with water in thousands of cubic metres as a scaling check. Where a plan is fixed, my binary linkage uses the planted area of the cell itself as its bound, unless a test below says otherwise. I did not import the model file anywhere in my audit code, and no solution value from it is used as evidence anywhere below.

## 1. Verdict

No formulation level fault of the class of the version 4 reserve fault was found. The rebuilt reserve admits the historical pattern in all 24 governorate pools on my replay, the yields basis is now one basis throughout, land binds in three season pools, and the ceiling is printed with its conditions. I state this at the top because the task asked me to say so here if I found such a fault, and I did not.

The arithmetic of version 4.1 is again the strongest part of the build, and it survives an independent reconstruction to a degree version 4 did not reach. The reference tonnage, all four ceiling figures, the frontier margins at every level, the wait and see value in all twelve scenarios to the pound, the penalised figures to the pound, the stress pairings, the baseline evaluation and the whole robustness distribution recomputed from the saved sample file all reproduce, several of them exactly. The version 4 audit limitation on robustness is closed: the per sample file exists and twenty of its samples rederive exactly under my own code.

What remains is presentation, in three places. The water value ranges in Section 5 of the note are ranges of one optimal dual solution where the dual solution is not unique, and on my own equally optimal dual solution one binding governorate falls outside the quoted range. Two frontier costs against the next lower level disagree with the note's own printed margins by one pound each. And the clearance statement names the dry inflow state as the unique home of the smallest headroom where my solves find a four way tie across the low price and yield layer. Minor revision is required; the numbers below the presentation largely stand.

## 2. Errors found, largest first

**Error 1. The water value ranges are properties of one dual solution, not of the model, and one quoted range fails on an equally optimal dual solution.**

My own linear relaxation at the same floor, the ceiling share minus a tenth of a point, gives an objective of 174,088,745,148.42 pounds against the log value of 174,088,745,148, so the primal problem I solved is the builder's primal problem. The raw to conditional conversion also verifies across all 96 cells of the log table, with a largest gap of 0.018 pounds per cubic metre, consistent with printed rounding. The dual values themselves do not all reproduce. In the dry inflow state at expected prices and yields, five of my eight governorate values match the log to the printed two decimals and three differ, Beheira by 7.71, Gharbia by 2.93 and Menoufia by 7.85 pounds per cubic metre. In the normal inflow state at expected prices and yields, three match and five differ. This pattern, an identical primal objective with different dual values on linked constraints, is dual degeneracy: the relaxation has more than one optimal dual solution, and CBC returns whichever one its path reaches. The consequences for the four ranges the note quotes are these. The dry low range of 1,073.50 to 1,240.34 reproduces at both ends exactly, and every one of my binding values sits inside it. The dry expected range of 47.39 to 64.32 reproduces at the top only; my values span 48.63 to 64.32, inside the quoted range but with a different minimum. The high expected range of 11.81 to 14.64 likewise reproduces at the top only; my values span 12.00 to 14.64, inside the quoted range. The normal expected range of 19.17 to 53.54 fails at the bottom: Damietta is the lowest governorate in both solutions and Sharkia the highest in both, but my Damietta value is 16.68 pounds per cubic metre, below the quoted minimum of 19.17, on a dual solution with the same optimal objective. A reader who quotes these ranges as the water values of the model overstates what the model says. The ranges should be printed as the ranges of the printed table, with a sentence saying the dual solution is not unique and that individual governorate values move between optimal dual solutions.

**Error 2. Two frontier costs against the next lower level disagree with the note's own printed margins by one pound.**

Every cost against the unconstrained optimum recomputes exactly from the margins printed in the note. Two costs against the next lower level do not. At 60 percent, the printed margins 297,739,198,932 and 262,520,406,731 subtract to 35,218,792,201 pounds; the note prints 35,218,792,200. At 80 percent, the printed margins 202,983,384,826 and 175,143,528,384 subtract to 27,839,856,442 pounds; the note prints 27,839,856,443. The two errors run in opposite directions, which is the signature of costs computed from unrounded margins and printed beside rounded ones. This is the same class of one pound inconsistency the version 4 audit flagged in the penalised value of the stochastic solution, and Section 1 of the note records that instance as repaired while these two stand. Immaterial in scale; exactly the class of fault this build set out to close.

**Error 3. The smallest clearance is a four way tie across the low layer, not a property of the dry inflow state alone.**

The substance of the clearance claim survives and is stated in Section 3 below: at every floored level the smallest headroom in my solves is within about a tenth of a tonne of zero. Its location as printed does not. With the planting fixed at each saved plan, my maximum staple tonnage in the four low layer scenarios differs by hundredths of a tonne across the four inflow states, because at the low layer the staple maximum of a fixed plan is set by its planting and its low yields and water does not bind it. At the 60 percent level my smallest headroom falls in the high inflow state at low prices and yields, at 70 percent in the drought state, and at 75 percent in the normal inflow state, each by less than a tenth of a tonne, inside tie noise. The note's sentence that at every level the smallest headroom sits in the dry inflow state at low prices and low yields takes one member of a tie and names it as the location. The honest sentence is that every floored plan is exactly tight throughout the low price and yield layer, in all four inflow states at once.

**Error 4. The reserve acceptance test cannot fail, and the cap writes off 30,136 hectares of annual unmodeled area without a seasonal home.**

I replayed the construction exactly as the note states it. The uncapped proportional split fails the baseline check in exactly the five pools the note names, with the observed modeled area above the uncapped limit by 11,084 hectares in Kafr El Sheikh winter, 10,933 in Menoufia summer, 7,031 in Qalyubia summer, 636 in Damietta winter and 452 in Damietta summer. The cap binds in those five pools and nowhere else, and after the cap all 24 pools pass, as printed. Two things follow that the note does not print. First, the acceptance test is true by construction: the cap sets each pool limit at or above the observed modeled area by definition, so 24 of 24 passes proves the arithmetic of the cap and nothing about the season it describes. Second, the cap deletes tonnage from the books. The capped reserves sum to less than the annual unmodeled area by 11,084 hectares in Kafr El Sheikh, 10,933 in Menoufia, 7,031 in Qalyubia and 1,088 in Damietta, 30,136 hectares in all, and no pool receives that area. The version 4 contradiction, a proportional share larger than the free land of its pool, is resolved by assuming the free land reading wins; it is not resolved by evidence, and part of the contradiction may be an artefact of the input years, since the modeled totals that enter the split mix the 2020 to 2021 panel with potato areas from 2021 to 2022, a mix the note discloses for the baseline. On materiality the cap is small: under the uncapped proportional split my ceiling is 9,818,690 tonnes, 80.12 percent, only 15,177 tonnes and 0.12 points below the primary ceiling, so the cap does the consistency work and almost none of the ceiling work. One soft spot of the split rule deserves naming beside it. The Nili reserve is proportional to modeled Nili area, so where modeled Nili planting is near zero the Nili reserve is near zero and the pool limit sits near physical area: Sharkia carries a Nili reserve of 67 hectares against a pool limit of 375,208 hectares. Plans use that room, Nili potatoes at 366 times the observed area in the unconstrained optimum, under the market absorption warning the note prints.

**Error 5. The saved plan files round areas to two decimals and put six cells above their pool limits, so the saved 80 percent plan fails its own hard floor recourse by two hundredths of a tonne.**

In the saved plans, six planted cells sit a fraction of a hectare above the pool limit of their cell, among them wheat in Beheira at 204,854.87 hectares against a limit of 204,854.868 and wheat in Sharkia at 234,787.11 against 234,787.109, the rounding of the saved CSV files at two decimals. The consequence is measurable in my own recourse. The maximum staple tonnage of the saved 80 percent plan in the dry inflow state at low prices and yields is 9,803,525.71 tonnes against a floor of 9,803,525.73 tonnes, so a hard floor recourse evaluation of the saved plan in that scenario is infeasible at every solver tolerance I tested, short by 0.02 tonnes. The note's clearance figures were computed on the in memory plans and are correct for them; a reader who rebuilds from the saved files meets a shortfall of two hundredths of a tonne and a solver status of infeasible. Immaterial in size, and exactly the class of trap Section 1 of the note warns about, now sitting in the shipped files. Saving the plans at more decimals closes it.

## 3. What the attacks established, attack by attack

**Baseline feasibility replay.** Physical cultivated area replays at 1,676,580.36 hectares and annual cropped area at 3,176,473.86 hectares, matching the note. The reference tonnage rebuilds from observed areas times published district yields at 12,254,407 tonnes, split 4,617,795 wheat, 4,086,080 paddy rice and 3,550,533 summer maize. The capped reserve construction passes all 24 pools on my replay and the uncapped split fails in the five pools of Error 4. The construction itself is attacked in Error 4: the cap does real consistency work in five pools, almost no ceiling work, and resolves the version 4 contradiction by assumption rather than by evidence. I found no pool in which the capped construction excludes or misstates the observed pattern it benchmarks against, which was the version 4 fault; the pattern fits, by construction, in every pool.

**Ceiling and its conditions.** My own maximisation of held staple tonnage gives, in the linear form and in the binary form with identical values: 9,833,866.8 tonnes, 80.25 percent, under the stated reserve construction; 11,144,080.0 tonnes, 90.94 percent, with no reserve held; 9,266,626.2 tonnes, 75.62 percent, on the labelled scaled yields sensitivity; and 9,490,156.2 tonnes, 77.44 percent, with the named caps. All four printed ceiling figures verify. At my primary ceiling solution all twelve scenarios attain the ceiling value, winter land binds in all eight governorates, no summer or Nili pool binds, and water binds in 47 of the 96 governorate scenario cells. Relaxing one family at a time in the linear form: removing land gives the no reserve value of 90.94 percent; tripling dry inflow water gives 10,740,977 tonnes, 87.65 percent, and dropping the dry state from the minimum gives the same value; dropping the drought state changes nothing; holding the minimum in the expected layer only gives 10,160,683 tonnes, 82.91 percent, so the low yield layer binds; removing the minimum viable water share changes nothing. The ceiling solution plants no rice in any inflow state and no sugar beet, which is why the sugar beet duty across 5,600 to 6,600 and the rice response factor across 1.00 to 1.40 leave my ceiling unmoved, as the note claims.

**Frontier.** My own solves reproduce the primary margins as follows. In my cubic metre formulation: 403,078,762,407 pounds at 0 percent, 262,520,406,731 at 60 percent, 223,222,240,454 at 70 percent and 202,983,384,826 at 75 percent, each to the pound, and values below the printed ones by 7,713,181 pounds at 50 percent and by 325,601 pounds at 80 percent. In my thousands of cubic metres formulation the 50 and 80 percent margins reproduce to the pound, 297,739,198,932 and 175,143,528,384. The 85 percent floor is infeasible in my solve, and the ceiling at 80.25 percent covers the higher levels. Every printed cost was verified as arithmetic on the printed margins: all costs against the unconstrained optimum are exact, and the costs against the next lower level are exact except the two one pound cases of Error 2, so the levels I solved bracket and confirm the rest. The capped margins verify the same way: my solves give 203,702,111,599 pounds unconstrained and 163,615,562,282 at 75 percent, and in the scaled formulation 197,178,572,288 at 50 percent, 188,203,914,415 at 60 percent and 174,128,054,485 at 70 percent, each to the pound against the note, with the capped 80 percent level infeasible in my solve.

**Clearance.** I loaded the saved plans at all five floored levels and maximised staple tonnage in each of the twelve scenarios in turn, with the binary linkage bound set to the planted area of the cell itself. My maximum staple values match the main run log to a tenth of a tonne in every scenario I compared, for example 8,877,266.0 tonnes in dry expected at the 70 percent level and 10,130,719 tonnes in dry expected at the 80 percent level. The smallest headroom at each level, in tonnes, is 0.03 at 50 percent, 0.03 at 60, 0.03 at 70, 0.03 at 75 and 0.05 at 80, in absolute value, so the claim of zero headroom to within a tenth of a tonne in the hardest scenarios survives at every floored level, subject to the location wording of Error 3 and the saved file rounding of Error 5. On bound choice: with the bound set to the district physical area instead, the 70 and 80 percent verdicts are identical to the tonne, so the verdict survives my bound choice; with the bound set to the pool land limit, every one of the twelve recourse solves for both plans fails, which is the subject of the numerics attack below.

**Duals.** Covered in Error 1. The relaxation objective at the builder's floor, the ceiling share minus a tenth of a point, is 174,088,745,148 in my solve as in the log; at the literal labelled floor of 0.8015 my objective is 174,061,181,459 pounds, a difference of 0.016 percent, so the printed label of 80.15 percent is the two decimal rounding of the exact floor of 80.1476 percent and not an error. The conversion from raw to conditional values verifies across all 96 log cells. Water binds in all eight governorates in each of the four quoted scenarios in my solve as in the log, and Damietta is lowest and Sharkia highest in the normal expected scenario in both solutions. The ranges are graded individually in Section 4.

**Information values.** The true mean budget replays at 10.9844 billion cubic metres from the state budgets of 8.588, 10.502, 13.434 and 9.580 at probabilities 0.2275, 0.4451, 0.3165 and 0.0110. My twelve deterministic wait and see solves at the 80 percent floor match the log scenario by scenario to the pound, from 58,031,940,268 pounds in dry low to 386,018,165,660 in high high, and sum to a wait and see value of 198,927,633,193 pounds; minus my recourse value at 80 percent this gives the printed expected value of perfect information of 23,784,104,809 pounds exactly. My expected value problem at the true means gives a plan whose staple maximum fails the 80 percent floor in exactly the ten scenarios the log names and holds in normal inflow at high prices and high inflow at high prices. Evaluated with no floor, that plan reaches 194,969,660,805 pounds in my recourse, to the pound. My penalised recourse at 25,000 pounds per tonne reproduces the log shortfalls scenario by scenario, for example 3,383,616 tonnes in dry low and zero in high high, and gives a penalised expected result of 167,535,664,790 pounds. The printed consistency also checks exactly: 175,143,528,384 minus 167,535,664,790 equals the printed penalised value of the stochastic solution of 7,607,863,594, with no pound missing this time.

**Robustness from files.** Recomputed from data/processed/robustness_samples_v4_1.csv alone, the distribution is: minimum 55.28 percent, tenth percentile 75.08, median 82.03, mean 80.15, maximum 82.67, hold share at the 80 percent floor 65.0 percent, worst decile shortfall 1,103,686 tonnes and mean shortfall 156,385 tonnes, every figure matching the note, against a floor reference of 9,803,526 tonnes which itself replays exactly. I then redrew the sampling sequence with seed 20261114, alternating single years and ten year window means from the Aswan series, loaded the saved robustness plan, froze late season planting at the normal inflow variant as the note states, and maximised staple tonnage under expected prices and yields at each sampled budget with my own code for twenty samples, the first fourteen and samples 50, 75, 95, 96, 98 and 99. Every one of the twenty reproduces the saved file value to within float noise, ratios and sample kinds included. The convention is material and the note's plain statement of it is load bearing: with late planting frozen at the dry variant instead, sample 2 gives 9,152,885 tonnes against the printed 8,389,444 and sample 96 gives 10,130,719 against the printed 9,200,504.

**Stress, baseline and beet.** At budget scale 0.90 my capacity run gives 9,049,077 tonnes, 73.84 percent, and my margin run gives zero staple tonnage, zero planted staple area and a margin of 375,091,832,795 pounds. At scale 0.80 the pair is 8,264,287 tonnes, 67.44 percent, and zero staples with a margin of 343,134,636,238 pounds. Capacity and choice are correctly paired in the note, and the zero is a genuine optimum of the profit objective in my solves, not a floor artefact, since no floor is present in these runs. The historical baseline evaluation in my recourse gives an expected margin of 86,006,146,997 pounds, expected staple tonnage of 8,908,274 tonnes and a minimum of 7,367,358 tonnes, each matching the note, and the accounting difference of 317,072,615,410 pounds, 368.66 percent, recomputes exactly from the two verified margins. On beet: every saved primary plan at 50, 60, 70, 75 and 80 percent and the robustness plan plant zero hectares of sugar beet in my reading of the files, my own unconstrained optimum plants zero sugar beet, and my beet variant solves at the factory price of 2,400 pounds per tonne give margins identical to my primary solves at 0, 70 and 80 percent, so the identity claim and its stated reason verify. The winter potato cost sensitivity also verifies: at the Nili potato cost my unconstrained margin is 436,283,724,443 pounds, to the pound.

**Numerics claims.** The second recorded incident verifies in my own code, in two distinct forms. With the binary linkage bound set to the pool land limit, the recourse of the saved 70 and 80 percent plans fails in all twelve scenarios, because of the rounded cells of Error 5. After clipping those six cells down to their pool limits, changes of less than 0.005 hectares per cell, the same pool bound solve in my cubic metre formulation returns a status of Optimal with a maximum staple tonnage of 8,605,771 tonnes in dry low for the 80 percent plan, understating the true value of 9,803,526 tonnes by 1,197,754 tonnes, while the identical solve with water in thousands of cubic metres returns the true value, and the cell bound and physical area bound agree with each other. A pool limit bound can therefore both make a solver produced plan infeasible against its own recourse and silently understate a feasible recourse, and the builder's repair, the planted cell bound for fixed plans, is the right one. The first recorded incident does not reproduce: at a CBC tolerance of 0.000000001, the margin evaluations and staple maximisations of the saved 70 and 80 percent plans return Optimal in both of my scalings, with maximum staple values identical to the default tolerance values. The hard floor recourse failures I do find at that tolerance are genuine, for the rounding reason in Error 5, at the default tolerance as well. Section 5 below records what this leaves unverified. A related finding from my own frontier work belongs here: my cubic metre formulation understates three published margins by up to 0.0026 percent where my thousands formulation reproduces them to the pound, so replication of this build is sensitive to water scaling in CBC, in both directions, and no published figure falls to it.

**Wording and discipline.** The floor is described in the note in the foundation's words: every represented scenario delivers at least the stated share of the modeled reference quantity of aggregate wheat, paddy rice and summer maize tonnage. No average return is called a price anywhere in the note; the one use of the word average in Section 11 describes the WaPOR totals and calls nothing a price. The historical comparison is labelled accounting only and carries no causal verb on any input. Every forecast in Sections 10 and 12 is quoted with its backtest error, and all ten forecast values, root mean squared errors and mean absolute percentage errors match data/processed/forecast_prices_yields_v4.csv at the printed rounding. The ceiling is printed with its conditions, the reserve construction of Section 1, the no reserve bound of 90.94 percent and the scaled yields basis of 75.62 percent. The baseline year mix is disclosed in plain words in Section 1, including the single Menoufia rice cell from 2019 to 2020. The contribution claim in Section 13 is the wording the foundation and the prior work review support, an exploratory governorate scale stochastic allocation model for eight governorates, outcomes compared against historical crop areas, with staple floor feasibility, conditional expected margins and scenario specific water duals reported, and it claims nothing beyond that combination and uses no word of first or new. My scan of the note file found no dash character of any kind in it. The WaPOR figures also replay from the source files: modeled baseline demand of 16.398 billion cubic metres against a WaPOR mean total of 24.416, with the modeled figure below the WaPOR total in every governorate, used as validation only. The failures under this attack are Errors 1, 2 and 3 only.

## 5. What I could not verify, and why

**The tolerance incident at 0.000000001.** The note records that at that CBC tolerance presolve falsely proved a feasible fixed plan infeasible during the build. No saved file identifies the plan, the scenario or the problem form it happened on, and the build's logs print only the repaired runs.
