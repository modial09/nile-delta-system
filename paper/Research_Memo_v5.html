Mohamed Diallo
November 2026

Every result in this memo is a verified model result on the basis stated in Section 1. None of it is a measured fact about Egypt, and no sentence in this memo should be read as one. The full run record sits in paper/Results_Note_v5.md, and the label that governs every figure sits in docs/foundation_rebuild/Gate_A_Final_Statement.md.

1. The question, the method, the central result

The question. Egypt grows most of its food in the Nile Delta, and each year the same water is divided among eight governorates and the crops they plant. A planner who wants to promise that staple output will hold in a dry year has no published price for that promise. I built this model to produce the price list: which aggregate staple promises the study area can support inside the model, what each promise costs in expected farm margin, and under which stated assumptions each price holds.

The method. The model is a two stage stochastic programme over eight governorates and five crops, with potatoes carried as three seasonal crops. Winter planting is fixed before the inflow state is known. Summer and Nili planting are decided after the inflow state is observed. Inside each scenario a planted crop may be abandoned at zero water and zero output with its cost sunk, and a kept crop must receive at least 0.30 of its full water duty, a stated assumption. The scenario set has nine members: three inflow states from the gauged Aswan record of 1871 to 1970, with probabilities 0.2300, 0.4500 and 0.3200, crossed with three price and yield layers weighted 0.25, 0.50 and 0.25. The guarantee is a hard floor, held in every scenario at once, on the aggregate tonnage of wheat, paddy rice and summer maize against a reference tonnage of 12,254,401 tonnes built from observed areas times published district yields.

The basis. Prices are 2025 forecasts from the FAOSTAT farm gate series, observed to 2024 for wheat, maize and potatoes and to 2023 for paddy rice and sugar beet. Costs are official 2022 values from the ministry cost bulletin. Yields are the published district yields of 2020 to 2021. All money is in nominal pounds. The water budgets scale from a calibration anchor of 11.00 billion cubic metres that no source publishes, split across governorates by physical land share. Section 4 prices what a wrong anchor does to every result.

The central result. Read the capped price list first, because it is the list a decision reader can act on. Under named rotation and market sanity caps, a guarantee of 75 percent of the reference tonnage costs 32,666,817,585 pounds of expected margin against the capped optimum of the same model, and 80 percent cannot be held at all, because the capped ceiling is 79.50 percent. In the relaxed world with no caps, the same 75 percent promise costs 242,759,240,394 pounds and the ladder reaches 80 percent at a cost of 268,610,886,052 pounds, with a ceiling of 82.41 percent. The pattern Egypt actually plants holds the 50, 60 and 65 percent levels in every scenario and fails from 70 percent. A tenth less water than the anchor deletes the 80 percent rung entirely. The price and yield layer probabilities are nominal, for the reason Section 5 states, and every expected money figure in this memo inherits that thinness. The hard floor, ceiling and feasibility results do not depend on the layer weights.

The implication. A guarantee level is a budget decision, and it should be taken from a price list with its conditions printed beside it. This memo hands over that list at three water anchors and under two planting worlds, with the benchmark pattern tested on the same basis, and with every weakness that survives verification named in Section 5.

2. The decision, the prior work, and the narrow contribution

The reader this work serves is a planning authority for the Delta: a ministry level planner who sets seasonal planting guidance and divides irrigation water among districts, and who may be asked to promise that staple output will hold in a dry year. The model does not choose the guarantee level. It publishes what the choice needs: the margin at each level, the cost of each level against the free optimum and against the level below, the highest level that can be held at all, and the value of water at the margin in each governorate under each inflow state.

I am not the first to model Egyptian water, and this work claims no such thing. Egypt has a sector model tradition of three decades. Lofgren (1996) modelled the cost of managing with less water. He and co authors (2006) derived shadow prices for irrigation water at national scale. McCarl and co authors (2015) carried the same lineage into climate scenarios. Gohar and Ward (2010) optimised water and land jointly along the Nile with food security constraints held, and Gohar, Cashman and El bardisy (2021) built the fullest Egypt allocation model I found, with 22 crops, three seasons and welfare tracked. Stochastic programming reached Egyptian cropping early, in Rosa and Yates (1994) and in the two stage model of El Shazly and co authors (2009). On the food floor, Adly and co authors (2018) computed the income cost of one fixed wheat self sufficiency floor at national scale. Abdelkader and Elshorbagy (2021) set out the national trade off between margin, water demand and food self sufficiency as patterns for policy makers. Water values for Egypt exist at national scale and by farm survey inside one Delta district.

What that record leaves open is the combination a Delta planner faces in one place: a model restricted to the Delta at governorate level, with planting decided before the inflow is known, a staple floor swept level by level with the margin cost of each level reported, and a water value for each governorate under each inflow scenario, all benchmarked against the observed current cropping pattern rather than a planned one. That combination is the contribution, and it is deliberately narrow. The method is not new. The claim is that this price list, on this basis, with these checks, did not exist in the open before.

Two lessons from the field literature discipline every reading. Ghazouani and co authors (2014) show that Delta farmers plant thirsty crops at canal tail ends for household food security and know how, not for profit alone, so an optimised pattern is a direction and a price signal, not a planting instruction. El Agha, Molden and Ghanem (2011) measured about half of main canal supply returning to drains and groundwater in a Delta command, so field level savings are not system level savings. My model works on applied duties at district level and claims no saving of that kind.

3. Data, cleaning, timing, and the model

Land, cropping pattern and district yields come from the Agricultural Statistics Bulletins of the Ministry of Agriculture, read first hand: the eight governorate panel for 2020 to 2021, with the potato season split from 2021 to 2022. The model keeps two land quantities apart at all times. Physical cultivated area, 1,676,580 hectares, limits each season. Annual cropped area, 3,176,474 hectares, describes the year and limits nothing, because a field carrying two crops is still one field. Land binds by season against physical area, minus a reserve for the crops I do not model, built from their observed areas. That reserve construction writes off 30,136 hectares of annual unmodeled area, and Section 4 reports the measured alternative split beside the primary one.

Costs taught me the largest lesson of the project. My first pass, in English, found only the CAPMAS income bulletin and stopped at 2017. The fuller official series, the Bulletin of Cost Statistics and Net Return of the Economic Affairs Sector, sits in the ministry catalogue under its Arabic title and gives component costs by crop and governorate through 2022. The model stands on those 2022 costs, rent included. No official cost table exists after 2022 in the sources this project holds, and Section 4 prices that gap with labelled bracketing assumptions instead of hiding it. One cost cell is a named gap: no winter potato cost table exists in the bulletin, so winter potatoes carry the summer potato cost in the primary run, with a sensitivity at the Nili potato cost of 54,176.19 pounds per hectare that raises the unconstrained margin to 592,918,438,384 pounds.

Prices come from the FAOSTAT prices domain, December 2025 vintage, and yields and areas from a direct pull of the FAOSTAT crops domain. Water duties are applied water figures under Delta conditions: the CAPMAS duty table for most crops, and field trial evidence from Egyptian journals for sugar beet, arbitrated at 5,600 to 6,600 cubic metres per hectare with the midpoint 6,100 in the primary run. The rice yield response factor of 1.20 remains unverified in its original source and is carried with a tested range of 1.00 to 1.40, across which the ceiling does not move. Satellite data plays a scale check role only: WaPOR measures total water consumption over all surfaces at a mean of 24.416 billion cubic metres a year over 2018 to 2025, and my modeled baseline demand of 16.398 billion cubic metres sits below that total, which is the order the two definitions require. WaPOR calibrates nothing in this model.

Cleaning is on the printed record, not silent. The loader acts on twelve blank yield cells. Eleven are fills by one stated rule, the area weighted mean of the published district yields for the crop: Nili maize in Kafr El Sheikh, Menoufia, Qalyubia and Damietta at 8.358 tonnes per hectare, and Nili potatoes in seven governorates at 18.120 tonnes per hectare, the single published Nili potato yield, from Beheira, carried to the governorates that publish none. Those seven potato cells are inert in the baseline, because those governorates plant no Nili potatoes in the observed pattern. The twelfth action is a substitution, not a fill: rice in Menoufia at 8.395 tonnes per hectare, Menoufia own published yield for 2019 to 2020, the year its baseline area of 7.98 hectares comes from. Missing, zero and not reported stay distinct throughout the pipeline.

The model does not eat hand set prices or yields. I built a forecasting module first, and it races five candidates per series: a ridge regression, a random forest, a gradient boosting model, and two simple lines, last value carried forward and a drift line. Backtests are rolling origin and selection is per crop at the horizon actually used. The verdict on prices is plain: no learning model beat the simple lines on any price series, and drift was selected for every crop. The 2025 price forecasts are wheat 17,891.58 pounds per tonne (backtest RMSE 2,373.38, MAPE 16.48 percent), paddy rice 20,027.28 at horizon two (RMSE 5,941.14, MAPE 25.93 percent), maize 20,532.94 (RMSE 2,359.95, MAPE 16.35 percent), sugar beet 846.03 at horizon two (RMSE 118.98, MAPE 15.07 percent) and potatoes 10,753.27 (RMSE 1,452.25, MAPE 19.53 percent). On yields the learners won in places: a random forest carries wheat at 6.6789 tonnes per hectare, maize at 7.6834 and sugar beet at 56.0570; a ridge regression carries potatoes at 31.5165; the naive line carries paddy rice at 9.1315, because every learner lost to it. One event is recorded, not hidden: the ridge model won the paddy rice price backtest and then diverged on the full data refit, and drift was selected in its place under a stated rule. The low and high layers of the model are set by the quartile bands of the backtest residuals, so the layer weights of 0.25, 0.50 and 0.25 describe the same construction as the bands.

Timing is stated exactly, because it decides what the guarantee means. Winter planting is one plan for all nine scenarios, fixed before the year type is known. Summer and Nili planting carry one variant per inflow state, decided with the inflow observed. No planting decision conditions on the price and yield layer, which is why the floor is written as a hard floor in every scenario rather than in expectation. One information advantage remains inside the model and is named here: once a scenario is realised, water allocation and abandonment respond with the full scenario in view, including its yield layer. No real planting season sees that much, and the size of that advantage is unmeasured.

The pipeline runs in one direction and the paper and this memo read from the same records: original sources, then extracted tables, then cleaned data, then parameter sets, then scenarios, then optimisation, then checks, then result records, then the written account. A reader can follow one governorate and crop record through the whole path in the project files.

4. The guarantee trade off

The capped price list is the headline. The relaxed frontier of the next table prices the guarantee in a world where planting may leave the observed pattern without limit, and its optima plant potatoes at up to several hundred times their observed areas at fixed prices. At those multiples the fixed prices stop being credible. The capped list prices the same ladder under named caps carried from the earlier audited version: wheat 0.50, rice 0.35, maize 0.40 in each season, sugar beet 0.30 and potatoes 0.15 of district physical land in each season. Inside the caps a guarantee is far cheaper at every shared rung, because the free plan the promise is priced against is itself a sane plan.

The reading is three sentences. A tenth less water removes the top rung of the ladder entirely, and the dearest surviving rung at 9.90, the 75 percent guarantee at a cost of 263,919,142,021 pounds, costs within 5 billion pounds of what the 80 percent guarantee costs at the full anchor, 268,610,886,052 pounds. A fifth less water removes everything above 65 percent, and the 65 percent guarantee then costs 226,520,935,005 pounds, more than the 70 percent guarantee costs at the full anchor. The ceiling moves with the anchor: 82.41 percent of the reference tonnage at 11.00, 75.86 percent at 9.90 and 69.30 percent at 8.80. The anchor remains unmeasured. What this table measures is the price of being wrong about it, and that price is now part of the record wherever the ladder is quoted.

The corrected baseline finding. The earlier version of this work printed that the pattern Egypt actually plants holds no guarantee at any level inside the model. That sentence was false on that build own numbers, and it is withdrawn. Tested on the version 5 basis with staple maximising recourse, scenario by scenario, the pattern capacity runs from 8,056,850 tonnes in the dry state at the low layer to 11,392,530 tonnes in the high state at the high layer. The pattern holds the 50, 60 and 65 percent levels in every scenario and fails from 70 percent, with a worst scenario capacity of 8,056,850 tonnes, 65.75 percent of the reference. Under profit seeking recourse the same pattern delivers an expected staple tonnage of 9,270,566 tonnes, 75.65 percent, and a worst scenario delivery of 7,684,799 tonnes, 62.71 percent, at an expected margin of 144,167,922,884 pounds. The accounting difference between the optimised unconstrained margin and the pattern margin is 415,545,553,464 pounds, 288.24 percent of the pattern margin, and no input is said to have caused any share of that difference. The two readings answer two different questions, capacity and delivery, and the corrected record keeps them apart.

Stress and robustness, stated honestly. Stress results always travel in pairs, capacity beside choice. At budgets scaled to 0.90, maximum staple capacity is 9,295,584 tonnes, 75.86 percent of the reference, while the profit objective plants no staples at a margin of 520,907,656,409 pounds. At 0.80, capacity is 8,491,820 tonnes, 69.30 percent, while the profit objective plants no staples at 476,620,802,204 pounds. A drought stress at its own budget of 9.580 billion cubic metres, the lowest ten year window mean of the record, carries maximum capacity of 11,104,798 tonnes, 90.62 percent, and a profit choice of 502,602,629,209 pounds with no staples planted. The drought object carries no probability and enters no expected value.

The value of information. At the 80 percent level, the wait and see value is 304,561,061,130 pounds against a recourse value of 291,102,590,296 pounds, so the expected value of perfect information is 13,458,470,835 pounds on comparable sets. The plan built on true means cannot hold the floor in 7 of the 9 scenarios, so no standard value of the stochastic solution exists for this benchmark and none is reported. On one consistent penalised definition, a penalty of 25,000 pounds per tonne of shortfall on both sides, the penalised value of the stochastic solution is 75,931,039,486 pounds. The earlier figure of 7,607,863,594 pounds paired two different definitions and is withdrawn as a definition, not only as a number.

5. Limitations, what changed after audit, and what evidence is needed next

The limits that bind hardest are these. First, the water anchor is unmeasured: every budget scales from a calibration of 11.00 billion cubic metres that no source publishes, split across governorates by physical land share. Section 4 bounds the damage a wrong anchor does and does not reduce it. Second, the money basis is mixed: prices are 2025 forecasts and costs are official 2022 values, bridged only by assumed escalation factors. With every cost multiplied by 1.50, the cost of the same 80 percent guarantee moves to 224,549,654,133 pounds, and with costs multiplied by 2.00 it moves to 180,141,961,977 pounds, so no margin in this memo is the figure for a single real year. Third, the layer probabilities are nominal: measured sequential coverage of the price bands runs from 0.0 percent for wheat to 22.2 percent for potatoes against a construction value of 50 percent, because the pooled residual history is dominated by the low price era, so the layers understate the spread of the current price regime. One label inside the same construction also misleads: both residual quartiles for potato prices are positive, so the low price layer for potatoes, 10,876.83 pounds per tonne, sits above the expected layer of 10,753.27. The arithmetic is right and the word low is wrong for that one series. Fourth, the guarantee protects aggregate tonnage of three staples summed over eight governorates. It does not protect any single crop, milled rice, calories or household access, and the floor can in principle be met while one crop falls far. Fifth, the saved 80 percent plan holds its floor within solver tolerance, not exactly: imposed as a hard constraint on the saved plan, the floor returns infeasible in the three low layer scenarios where the plan is tight, while clearance maximisation returns 9,803,521.000 tonnes against a floor of 9,803,520.985 tonnes in those scenarios, and easing the floor by a single tonne makes all nine scenario solves return optimal. The guarantee of the saved plans is real, it is established by maximisation, and it is exactly as thin as that sentence says. The floor also lives in the plan together with its floor holding recourse: replaying the saved planting with margin seeking recourse and no floor imposed delivers a minimum scenario tonnage of 8,926,542 tonnes, below the floor.

What changed after audit is the spine of this version. An external audit of version 3 proved six defects my own audit had missed: a land pool that let winter planting exceed physical land, a stochastic value computed against the wrong benchmark, a ceiling stated as physics instead of as a property of the formulation, crop failure that was not real failure, a guarantee that had never been priced, and stress language that confused a profit choice with physical collapse. Version 4.1 repaired those, and its own audits then caught more. Version 5, built under the foundation rebuild, corrected the price vintage and the forecast target year, rebuilt the bands at the quartiles so the weights and the construction agree, took the drought state out of the probability weights, priced the cost gap and the reserve split, ran the wheat procurement price variant that the critique demanded, documented all twelve loader actions, and withdrew the false baseline sentence and the mixed robustness headline. An independent verification then reran the whole build in a scratch copy, regenerated the saved outputs exactly, recomputed every headline figure with separate arithmetic, and replayed the floor 80 problem and the ceiling problem under a different solver, with agreement to 3,206 pounds on 291.1 billion for the floor and to a third of a tonne for the ceiling. The numerical half of the acceptance gate is met. The empirical half stays closed.

What evidence is needed next is specific. A published release series for the Delta would replace the calibration anchor with a measurement, and none exists in the sources this project holds. An official cost table after 2022 would close the mixed money basis. A winter potato cost table would close the named cost gap, and a verified source for the rice response factor would close the carried parameter. Market evidence on how much potato output the Delta markets absorb at near current prices would turn the caps from a modelling device into an estimate. Two closing events remain before any figure here may be called a validated empirical result: the band calibration decision, taken in the open, either rebuilding the layers on a stated recent window with the new coverage printed, or printing the coverage shortfall as a standing limitation beside every expected figure, as this memo already prints it; and a human review by an Egyptian irrigation or agricultural specialist, of the model as much as of the numbers. Until both events have happened, the label of this work is the one this memo carries on its first page: a numerically verified exploratory model study on a stated basis, its proved defects closed, its remaining weaknesses named, and in the cases of the water anchor and the band coverage, now measured.
