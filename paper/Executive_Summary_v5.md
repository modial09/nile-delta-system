# Executive Summary: Pricing the Food Guarantee, Version 5

Mohamed Diallo, November 2026

I asked a plain question. What does it cost, in farm income, to promise that staple output in the Nile Delta will hold in a dry year, level by level?

I built a two stage stochastic allocation model for eight governorates and five crops to answer it. Winter planting is fixed before the inflow is known, summer and Nili planting follow it, and a hard floor on aggregate wheat, paddy rice and summer maize tonnage must hold in all nine scenarios. The basis is stated once and carried everywhere: 2025 forecast prices, official 2022 costs, published district yields, and water budgets scaled from a calibration anchor of 11.00 billion cubic metres that no source publishes.

The central result is a price list. Under named planting caps, a guarantee of 75 percent of the reference tonnage of 12,254,401 tonnes costs 32,666,817,585 pounds of expected margin, and 80 percent cannot be held. In the relaxed model the ladder reaches 80 percent at a cost of 268,610,886,052 pounds, with a ceiling of 82.41 percent. The pattern Egypt actually plants holds 65 percent and fails from 70 percent. A tenth less water deletes the 80 percent rung. The scenario layer probabilities are nominal, so every expected figure carries that thinness, and no result here is a measured fact about Egypt.

The implication is simple. A guarantee level is a budget decision. It should be chosen from a priced list, with its conditions printed beside it, and planned together with the instruments that cover the years when the promise breaks.
