# Pricing the Wheat Guarantee: Executive Summary, Version 6

Mohamed Diallo
November 2026

Egypt eats about 20.6 million tonnes of wheat a year and grows part of it in the Nile Delta. This study prices a promise: that the Delta will grow a stated share of that wheat even in a dry year. The promise is stated in wheat cover and in months of stock, the units the decision is taken in. Every figure is a verified model result on a stated basis, not a measured fact about Egypt. Money is in billions of pounds, rounded to one decimal.

Under planting caps that keep the plan near the observed pattern, a guarantee of 25 percent wheat cover, which is 3.0 months of stock, costs 28.9 billion pounds of expected farm margin. The steps are 11.5 billion pounds to reach 15 percent cover, 8.0 billion more to reach 20 percent, and 9.4 billion more to reach 25 percent. A promise of 30 percent cannot be held at all: the capped ceiling is 27.8 percent cover. Priced on the worst quarter of scenarios instead of the average, the 25 percent promise costs 31.5 billion pounds. The pattern Egypt actually plants already holds 20 percent cover in every scenario, so the live question is the price of the step from 20 to 25 percent.

In a relaxed world where planting may leave the observed pattern without limit, the same 25 percent promise costs 83.1 billion pounds and the ceiling rises to 42.1 percent. The two lists are printed together on purpose: a guarantee has no single price, only prices, each with its world attached.

Version 6 repairs the four foundations an expert review challenged. The promise is denominated in wheat, not in mixed tonnes. The scenarios describe the managed river of today, storage in Lake Nasser, inflow year types and one stated release rule, not the natural river of 1871 to 1970. Rice is bounded by the state decree, and berseem, the winter crop that truly competes with wheat, stands inside the model. Costs stand on one year through a sourced bridge, and district water can now be supplied by the decision maker through a template the model reads cell by cell, with the source of every value printed in the run log.

Three tests discipline the headline. When the water anchor falls by a fifth, the capped ceiling does not move and every rung gets dearer: water binds on price, not on possibility. Replayed against the actual prices and yields of 2015 to 2024, the 25 percent plan holds its floor in two years of ten, so the promise is a property of the scenario set and must be bought with open eyes. And the input that moves the price most is not water. It is wheat yield: yields about 11.4 percent above the published basis would halve the cost of the guarantee. Inside the model, recourse sees the full scenario before it allocates water, so every printed price is a lower bound. An independent verification of this build passes fifteen of sixteen checks; the one failure, a robust ordering inside the weight sensitivity, is recorded in full in the verification file.
