# Executive Summary: Pricing the Food Guarantee, Version 5.1 DRAFT

Mohamed Diallo, November 2026

Status: draft for my own read before the send to Professor Anusha. It replaces no earlier file. It applies the text fixes from the clarity assessment and from my own reading on the morning of 16 November 2026. It does not include any new model run.

I asked a plain question. What does it cost, in farm income, to promise that staple output in the Nile Delta will hold in a dry year, level by level?

I built a two stage stochastic allocation model for eight governorates and five crops to answer it. Winter planting is fixed before the inflow is known. Summer and Nili planting follow it. Nili is the third planting season of the year. A hard floor on aggregate wheat, paddy rice and summer maize tonnage must hold in all nine scenarios. The basis is stated once and carried everywhere: 2025 forecast prices, official 2022 costs, published district yields, and water budgets scaled from a calibration anchor of 11.00 billion cubic metres that no source publishes.

## The two price lists, kept apart

There are two lists, and they must never be mixed. Each figure below is the total cost of that level of promise, in billions of pounds with one decimal. The step figure is the extra cost of moving up from the level below it. Exact pounds stay in the results note.

List one, with planting caps on. This is the list that stays near what real markets can absorb, so it is the list to read first.

1. A promise of 50 percent costs 3.0 in total. The step from no promise is 3.0.
2. A promise of 60 percent costs 8.6 in total. The step is 5.6.
3. A promise of 65 percent costs 14.0 in total. The step is 5.3.
4. A promise of 70 percent costs 22.0 in total. The step is 8.1.
5. A promise of 75 percent costs 32.7 in total. The step is 10.7.
6. A promise of 80 percent cannot be held at any price in this list. The highest promise this list can hold is 79.5 percent.

List two, with planting caps off. This list is a benchmark only. It lets the model plant without limit at one fixed price, so its free plan earns money that was never real, and every promise in it is priced against that high starting point.

1. A promise of 50 percent costs 129.4 in total. The step from no promise is 129.4.
2. A promise of 60 percent costs 173.3 in total. The step is 43.9.
3. A promise of 65 percent costs 196.0 in total. The step is 22.7.
4. A promise of 70 percent costs 219.4 in total. The step is 23.4.
5. A promise of 75 percent costs 242.8 in total. The step is 23.4.
6. A promise of 80 percent costs 268.6 in total. The step is 25.9. The highest promise this list can hold is 82.41 percent.

Read this way, there is no jump from 32.7 to 268.6 inside one list. The first figure is the cost of 75 percent in list one. The second figure is the cost of 80 percent in list two. Inside each list the total cost rises at every rung, because the first land moved into staples is the cheapest land to move, and higher promises start cutting the most profitable land. The step sizes vary because the rungs are not evenly spaced. The promise must also hold in the worst scenario, so the sacrifice is paid in good years too.

## What else the reader needs at the headline

The pattern Egypt already plants holds 65 percent in every case the model tests, and fails from 70 percent. The real question is what the steps above 65 cost.

The capped list is also tested at lower water, and it weakens fast. At a tenth less water, the highest promise it can hold falls to 72.94 percent, so the 75 percent promise does not survive the cut. At a fifth less water, the ceiling is 66.38 percent. In the relaxed list, a tenth less water deletes the 80 percent rung entirely.

Once a scenario is known, the model allocates water and abandonment with the full scenario in view. No real planting season sees that much. That advantage can only make a promise easier to hold, so the printed costs are lower bounds on the true cost of the promise.

The scenario layer probabilities are nominal, so every expected figure carries that thinness, and no result here is a measured fact about Egypt.

## The point

A guarantee level is a budget decision. It should be chosen from a priced list, with its conditions printed beside it, and planned together with the instruments that cover the years when the promise breaks.
