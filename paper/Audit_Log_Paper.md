# Independent audit of the paper, Nile_Delta_Paper.md

Audit only, plus the direct corrections recorded in Section 4 below. Written 14 November 2026. I did not write the paper. I read the paper in full, the corrected results note in paper/Results_Note_v4_2.md, the version 4.1 audit in paper/Audit_Log_v4_1.md, docs/FOUNDATIONS.md, docs/PRIOR_WORK.md and paper/PAPER_SPEC.md. I reconstructed the figures from the four run logs, the forecast file, the robustness sample file, the saved plans at full precision and the external CSV files. Where a figure needed recomputation I used my own code, including the independent audit code in data/processed/audit_v4_1_work/au.py, which never imports the model file. My own solves in this audit include the ceiling maximisation, the 80 percent frontier in the cubic metre formulation, the linear relaxation duals at the printed dual floor, the reference tonnage and land totals from the raw CSV files, and a full recomputation of the robustness distribution from data/processed/robustness_samples_v4_1.csv.

## 1. Verdict

Minor revision, now applied. The numbers in the paper are the strongest part of it, as they were in the version 4.1 build underneath it. Every pound figure in the frontier table, both of the one pound corrected costs among them, every ceiling figure and its band, the historical pattern figures, the stress pairs, the information values, the robustness figures recomputed from the sample file, and every forecast value and error in Section 4 and Appendix A, traces to the logs and files on disk and reproduces, several of them to the pound in my own solves. No headline number in the paper is fabricated and none is withdrawn on its numbers.

What failed was wording around sound numbers, in six places, listed largest first in Section 2. The largest is a method statement in the limits section that describes a drought the model never contained. Two are discipline failures: a forecast printed without its error despite the paper claiming the opposite on the same subject, and a blanket backtest length that is wrong for two of the five price series and is contradicted by the paper own Appendix A. One is the abstract calling 80 percent the highest level the model can hold when the paper own ceiling is 80.25 percent. The rest are apparatus: a wrong internal section reference, an appendix order that put Appendix B before Appendix A, and two reference entries that were missing or conflated. All six are corrected in the paper text where the correct form was unambiguous, and every correction is recorded in Section 4. The paper should not be sent in its pre audit form; in its corrected form the audit finds nothing left that misstates a number, a method or a source.

## 2. Errors found, largest first

**Error 1. Section 8 said the 1978 to 1987 drought enters the drought state construction. It does not enter the model at all.**

As printed, Section 8 read that the state probabilities rest on the 1871 to 1970 record, and that the 1978 to 1987 drought enters only as a period mean inside the drought state construction. The evidence says the opposite on both halves. The drought state in src/allocation_model_v4_1.py is built from the 91 ten year rolling window means inside the 1871 to 1970 record itself: its ratio of 0.8709 is the lowest window ratio in that record, and its probability of 0.0110 is one window in 91. Appendix C of the same paper describes exactly that construction, correctly, so the paper contradicted its own methods appendix. The 1978 to 1987 drought falls after the record ends, and the hydrology research notes record it as a gap: it is known only as a published period mean of about 72 billion cubic metres a year, with no year by year table obtainable, and no part of it enters any state, probability or budget in the model. The numeric damage is nil, because the version 4.1 reaudit showed that dropping the drought state changes the ceiling not at all, but a limits section that claims a feared historical drought is represented inside the model, when it is not represented at all, is the most serious misstatement in the paper. Corrected in the text; see Section 4.

**Error 2. Section 4 claimed that no forecast appears anywhere in the paper without its error beside it. That claim was false inside the same paper, twice.**

The claim is a foundation rule of this project, and the paper prints it as a fact about itself in Section 4. Section 6.8 then printed the sugar beet forecast farm gate level of 820.52 pounds per tonne with no error beside it. Appendix C printed all ten forecast values, with their low and high bands, for prices and for yields, with no backtest error beside any of them. The values themselves are all correct, verified against data/processed/forecast_prices_yields_v4.csv in Section 3 below; what failed is the discipline the sentence asserts. A reader who checks the claim against the appendices finds it false, and the claim is the paper own honesty pledge. Corrected by printing the errors in both places; see Section 4.

**Error 3. Section 4 gave one backtest length for all price series. For two of the five it is wrong, and Appendix A of the same paper says so.**

As printed, Section 4 said the backtests are rolling origin with thirteen test seasons for prices and twenty four for yields. The score file data/processed/backtest_scores_v4.csv, the forecasting run log and docs/FORECASTING_RESULTS.md all agree: wheat, maize and potato prices have 13 test seasons; paddy rice and sugar beet prices have 10 test seasons, the observed seasons 2011 to 2018 and 2022 to 2023, because no price was published for 2019 to 2021; yields have 24 test seasons for every crop. Appendix A of the paper prints the rice and beet rows with exactly those observed seasons, so Section 4 contradicted the paper own data appendix. Corrected in the text; see Section 4.

**Error 4. The abstract called 80 percent the highest level the model can hold. The paper own ceiling is 80.25 percent.**

As printed, the abstract said a guarantee of 80 percent is the highest level the model can hold in every scenario, in the same sentence that priced it at 227,935,234,023 pounds. The price belongs to the 80 percent ladder rung, and the capacity result printed in Section 6.1 and Appendix D puts the exact ceiling at 80.25 percent, so a guarantee above 80 percent, up to 80.25, can in fact be held in this model, and its margin cost was never computed. The sentence as printed understated the ceiling and attached the ladder price to a superlative that belongs to a different number. The results note states it correctly as the highest feasible ladder level. Corrected in the text; see Section 4.

**Error 5. A wrong internal reference sent the reader to the stress section for the sampled hydrology results.**

At the end of Section 6.1 the paper said that Section 6.4 measures how often a tight plan falls below its floor under sampled hydrology. Section 6.4 is the stress section. The sampled hydrology results are in Section 6.7. Small in size, and exactly the kind of fault a careful reader finds first. Corrected in the text; see Section 4.

**Error 6. The appendix order and two reference entries were wrong.**

The paper printed Appendix B, the glossary, before Appendix A, the data appendix, so the appendices ran B, A, C, D. In the references, Cobb (1978), the source of the Aswan series cited by name in the references text and twice in Appendix A, had no entry of its own. The single Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation entry dated 2016 carried both the 2016 Water Strategy and the National Water Balance total of 65.35 billion cubic metres, which Appendix A correctly attributes to the National Framework for Water Resources Allocation of August 2026, a document with no reference entry. All three are corrected; see Section 4. One further apparatus fault is flagged and left, because the right edit is an author choice: the Basheer and co authors (2021) entry appears in the references but is never cited anywhere in the body or the appendices.

## 6. What I could not verify, and why

**The authenticity of the source files.** My reconstruction establishes that the paper traces to the logs and CSV files on disk, and that those files are internally consistent. It does not establish that the CSV files faithfully transcribe the ministry bulletins, the FAOSTAT domains or the WaPOR rasters they name, because the original publications were not before me. This is the same limit the version 3, version 4 and version 4.1 audits recorded, and it stands.

**Eighty of the hundred robustness samples, at the level of their own solves.** The distribution in Section 6.7 recomputes in full from the saved sample file, which I did with my own code. I did not resolve the recourse of the fixed plan sample by sample. The version 4.1 reaudit rederived twenty of the samples under its own code and all twenty reproduced exactly, and the builder log prints all hundred; the remaining eighty rest on the saved file and that verified method, not on a solve of mine.

**Whether a floor at exactly 80.25 percent would return the ceiling tonnage as a margin optimal plan.** The ceiling is a capacity maximisation, and no frontier run at 80.25 percent exists in any log. The correction to the abstract therefore says only what the evidence supports, that 80 percent is the highest ladder level and 80.25 percent is the exact ceiling, and claims no margin at the ceiling level. Any sentence that priced a guarantee at 80.25 percent would be unverified.
