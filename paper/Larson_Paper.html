The Procurement Price as a Decision Variable: Formulation, Estimation and Verification Notes on a Nile Delta Wheat Model

Mohamed Diallo, November 2026 Prepared for Richard C. Larson, who offered to read and comment

Egypt consumes about 20.6 million tonnes of wheat a year and grows roughly half of it at home. Each autumn the state announces the price it will pay for the coming harvest, and each spring farmers choose how much of their crop to deliver to the state at that price and how much to sell elsewhere. What the state fails to collect, it imports later, at world prices, usually in a hurry. This note describes an extension of my Nile Delta allocation model that treats the announced price as a decision variable. I lead with the formulation, the estimation and the verification standard, and I put the weaknesses in a section of their own, including the two I would attack first: the delivery response underpredicts both of its out of sample seasons, and the berseem price against which wheat is tested is a construction, not a measurement.

1. The base programme

The foundation is a two stage stochastic linear programme over eight Delta governorates, three planting seasons and nine crops, solved with CBC through PuLP. First stage: one winter planting plan for all scenarios. Second stage: summer and Nili planting adapt to the observed water state, and recourse reallocates water among planted crops or abandons a planted crop at zero water with its cost sunk; a kept crop must receive at least 0.30 of its full water duty, with yield falling linearly below full watering. A scenario is a Lake Nasser storage state (low, normal, high) crossed with an inflow year type (dry, normal, high), turned into a budget by one stated release rule, crossed with a price and yield layer (low, expected, high): 27 scenarios, with stated planning weights. The inflow ratios that define the year types come from the gauged Aswan record of 1871 to 1970; its frequencies are not used as weights, because the High Dam ended the river those frequencies describe, and the release rule replaces them. Planting never conditions on the layer, so a wheat floor can be imposed as a hard constraint in every scenario at once. The objective is expected farm margin, read through its duals as well as its plan: the shadow price of water in each governorate and the shadow cost of the floor are the quantities a planner actually trades in. Water scales from a built anchor of 11.00 billion cubic metres split by land share; rice is bounded by the state decree area of 451,164 hectares; planting caps keep the headline plans near the observed pattern.

The foundation headline: a capped ceiling of 27.83 percent wheat cover, cover being home production over consumption; a 25 percent guarantee costing 28.9 billion pounds of expected margin and 31.5 billion on the worst quarter of scenarios; and a historical replay in which the capped 25 percent plan holds its floor in two of the ten years 2015 to 2024, while the observed pattern holds that level in none of them. The extension keeps the programme untouched and adds three blocks around it.

2. The extension: three blocks

The delivery response. Let p be the state price per tonne, q the farm gate alternative price per tonne, and r the ratio p over q. Let s(r) be the share of national production delivered to state collection points, and Q the production basis. Deliveries are

D = s(r) * Q,   with   s(r) = a + b * r

fitted by ordinary least squares on the public record, as set out in Section 3. Q is held at 10.2 million tonnes in the headline grid, the FAO record 2026 harvest carried as the coming season planning basis because no 2027 forecast exists.

The coupling. At each grid price the cropping programme is resolved twice. Wheat revenue inside the programme is valued at a blended price: the delivered share at the state price, the remainder at the farm gate price of the same layer. The free plan imposes no wheat floor; the floor plan holds production at 4,610,705 tonnes in every scenario, the level the foundation study found the observed pattern can hold. The two plans answer two different questions: what the price plants on its own, and what the standing production promise costs to keep at that price.

The financial layer. With T the announced delivery target of 5.0 million tonnes, w the world price per tonne in pounds at the arrival quarter scenario, f the financing rate, h the holding period in months, and c the storage cost per tonne per month, the total programme cost at price p is

C(p) = p * D  +  w * max(0, T - D)  +  p * D * f * h / 12  +  D * c * h

The three terms are the procurement bill, the import cost of the target gap, and carrying: financing on the bill plus storage, charged on delivered local tonnes only. Central parameters: w at 313 dollars per tonne cost and freight converted at 55.25 pounds per dollar, with scenarios at 265 and 400 dollars and exchange rates of 54.47 and 57.00; f at 0.25, on treasury bill yields of 0.23956 to 0.2635 observed from August to November 2026; h at 5 months, with 3 and 7 as the range; c at 60 pounds per tonne per month, with 40 and 90 as the range. Gap imports carry a stated shipping lag of 75 days, priced by valuing gap tonnes at the arrival quarter world scenario rather than by a separate premium. The total excludes the baseline import programme for the rest of consumption, about 10.4 million tonnes on this basis, which does not vary across the grid, and the bread subsidy downstream of the stock.

One identity is worth stating before the numbers, because it drives the grid its main lesson. Between adjacent grid prices, the step cost per extra delivered tonne is dominated by the inframarginal term: the price rise is paid on all D tonnes, not on the increment. Numerically the step cost runs from 47,156 to 63,350 pounds per extra tonne against prices per tonne of 14,667 to 23,333. A price instrument buys its marginal tonne at roughly three times its face value in this system. That is arithmetic, not estimation, and it survives every curve in Section 3.

3. Estimation, and why I trust it only so far

The response is fitted on six seasons, 2019 to 2024, the seasons with a published farm gate price: share equals 0.2611 plus 0.1682 times ratio, slope standard error 0.0331, intercept standard error 0.0286, residual standard deviation 0.0105, R squared 0.866. The record behind those six points is policy heavy in ways that should worry any reader. In 2022 and 2023 the published farm gate price equals the procurement price exactly, so the ratio reads 1.000 in those years by construction of the sources: the official alternative series is partly administered, and two of my six abscissae are set by the data publisher rather than observed in a market. In 2022 a decree required delivery of a minimum of 12 ardebs per feddan, so part of that season share was mandated, not chosen; refitting without 2022 drops the slope to 0.1384, about a fifth lower. I keep 2022 in the headline fit because the mandate bound only a minimum per feddan and the season still carries price information, but the refit travels with the result.

The out of sample record is worse, and I report it before the results because it conditions them. With the alternative price for 2025 and 2026 set to the project forecast of 17,892 pounds per tonne, the fitted line gives 0.399 for 2025 against an actual share of 0.430, and 0.418 for 2026 against an actual 0.480: underprediction of about 0.3 and about 0.6 million tonnes. The misses run in one direction. The public record suggests the response in those seasons had a planting component the share equation cannot see: planted area reached a record of about 3.7 million feddans in 2026, payment within 48 hours was publicised, and the 2026 price was announced before sowing and raised again in April. A share only response on a fixed production basis is therefore a conservative device, not a belief about the world.

The build handles this with three curves rather than with a better equation it cannot support. Low: the fit with the slope reduced by one standard error, share equals 0.2893 plus 0.1351 times ratio. Central: the fit as above. High: a refit on all eight seasons, share equals 0.2464 plus 0.1989 times ratio, resting on a constructed alternative price for 2025 and 2026 and labelled as a construction wherever it is used. Every headline number is computed on the central curve with the band beside it, and the Monte Carlo of Section 4 mixes the three curves and varies production across its 2015 to 2024 historical multipliers. One further discomfort is stated plainly: the observed ratio range is 0.654 to 1.000, and every grid row at 2,800 pounds per ardeb and above sits above it, at ratios to 1.304. The demand range the policy argument is about lives in the extrapolated region.

At the announced price of 2,650 pounds per ardeb, the bill is 77.0 billion pounds, the gap of 642,721 tonnes costs 11.1 billion at the central world price, carrying is 9.3 billion, and the total is 97.4 billion. The Monte Carlo column is from 2,000 draws per price mixing the three curves, production history, the foundation price layers and the world price scenarios: at the announced price no draw reaches the 5.0 million tonne target, and the worst quarter mean total is 100.5 billion pounds. At 3,500 the central curve still falls 99,226 tonnes short of the target, while the high curve delivers 5.159 million tonnes and passes it. The target question is thus, in large part, a question about which curve the last two seasons support. One reading from the same simulation is reported only to be quarantined: the share of draws reaching the promised cover at each price sits near one half by construction, because the promise is the central case and the draws are centred near it. It is not a confidence level, and I do not use it as one.

Two plan readings complete the picture. The free plan first plants wheat on its own at a state price of about 3,060 pounds per ardeb, the point where the blended price reaches about 19,027 pounds per tonne; below that point, on this basis, Delta wheat stands on the imposed pattern and on motives outside margin, and even at 3,500 the free plan plants only 116,250 hectares against a pattern of about 672,606 hectares. And the cost of holding the pattern production floor falls from 30.2 billion pounds at a price of 2,200 to 13.1 billion at 3,500: raising the price makes the purchase dearer and the production guarantee cheaper at once. That second reading is where the extension repays the foundation. The guarantee ladder of the base programme was priced as if the crop arrived by itself; priced with the announcement inside the model, part of the guarantee bill turns out to be paid by the price itself.

5. The berseem break even, a negative result I would rather report than resolve

The natural structural story is that deliveries answer the price because wheat must outbid berseem, its winter rival, for land. The model can price that contest exactly, and the answer kills the story. Berseem earns a margin of 129,327 pounds per hectare on the model basis, so wheat needs revenue of 211,394 pounds per hectare against a cost of 82,067 to match it. The break even state price at the blended value runs from 5,412 pounds per ardeb in Sharkia to 6,261 in Kafr El Sheikh; at full delivery, the case most favourable to wheat, from 4,321 to 5,022. The demand range tops out at 3,500. On margins, no price under discussion plants or delivers one extra tonne against berseem.

Two readings are possible. The first is that the fitted response rides on channels a margin table cannot see: payment speed, the delivery obligation, access to subsidised inputs tied to delivery, and the anchor the state price sets for the trader price, which would make the ratio itself partly endogenous to the announcement. The second reading attacks my berseem price, and it has real force: the price is a constructed feed value, 0.125 times the maize price, because no official berseem price series exists, and Pakistan, the mirror system used elsewhere in this project, publishes fodder only as one combined category, so the construction has no cross check anywhere. Berseem also feeds the grower own animals, so much of its margin is avoided feed cost rather than cash, and wheat straw and household flour carry value the model does not price. I report the tension rather than resolving it by construction: the response is fitted to the record precisely because the margin route cannot be trusted to generate it, and the break even table is printed so a reader can see what the fitted line is being asked to carry.

6. The verification standard and the procedure behind it

Verification is a separate routine, written apart from the build, and it recomputes by paths the build did not use. For this extension, src/check_v7.py passes thirteen of thirteen checks. The response fit is recomputed with numpy polyfit against the build lstsq call: intercept 0.261064 and slope 0.168235, identical, and the R squared recomputes to 0.865938. Grid arithmetic at 2,200, 2,650 and 3,500 recomputes from the saved inputs. The two announced price plans are rebuilt and replayed under HiGHS against the build CBC solutions: free plan margins of 221,699,178,210 against 221,699,177,650 pounds, floor plan margins of 197,007,449,422 against 197,007,448,659, agreement at the level of solver tolerance on objectives of about 200 billion. The Monte Carlo shares at the announced price recompute from the saved sample file, including the worst quarter mean total of 100,533,845,324 pounds exactly. One break even price, Sharkia at full delivery, recomputes directly from parameters to 4,321.21 pounds per ardeb.

One pass is weak, and I record it as one rather than letting the count flatter the work. The rice decree check reads the saved floor plan and finds a largest state rice area of 0.0 hectares against the decree bound of 451,164 hectares. The optimised plans plant no rice at all, because at the model prices rice earns the least per unit of water among the summer crops; the decree binds the observed pattern, which plants 447,153 hectares, and does not bind these plans. The check therefore tests almost nothing for this extension. The foundation check, src/check_v6.py, is honest in the harder direction: fifteen of sixteen checks pass, and the sixteenth, a distributionally robust ordering check, fails. Under a common replay the robust plan holds a marginally lower worst case than the nominal plan at cover of 20 and 25 percent, by about 20 million pounds. The failure is recorded in full in the project verification file, and no dominance claim for the robust plan appears anywhere in the work. I would rather hand you a verification record with a failure in it than a clean count I had curated.

The rest of the procedure is of the same kind, and it is the part of the project I am most willing to be judged on. Every headline claim in the papers is entered in a claim register with its inputs, its generating file and a status of confirmed computation, reported, or unverified. The register has caught real errors: an earlier printed claim that the historical pattern holds no guarantee at any level was contradicted by the saved outputs, and the text was corrected with the correction left in the record. The work is versioned in a repository, github.com/modial09/nile-delta-system, held private while under review, with code, data, run logs and documents committed together: version 5 rebuilt the foundation, version 6 repaired the four faults a blind persona review had named, and version 7 added this extension, each version checked before the next was built on it. The largest data gap, district water by source, is handled by a blank input template shipped with the model, 42 rows of district water by source, crop costs and crop expected prices, each labelled with its unit and its public default; its reader sits at the data loading stage only, a filled cell replaces only its named input, the run log prints the provenance of every cell, and verification confirms the blank template reproduces the default parameters exactly while one filled cell changes only that cell. What the verification establishes is arithmetic, solver agreement and internal consistency. It does not establish that the response describes the world, and Section 3 records the two seasons in which, out of sample, it did not.

7. Where I expect attack

The response is six points. Everything behavioural in this extension hangs on a line fitted to six observations from a market where the state sets the price and buys the crop, with an administered alternative series and one mandated season. The honest defence is that the alternative was deriving the response from margins, and Section 5 shows where that road ends. It remains six points.

Half the grid is extrapolation. The policy argument concerns prices whose ratios run to 1.304 against an observed range ending at 1.000. A linear share in the ratio, extrapolated, is a modelling choice a reader may reject; a saturating form would lower the top rows. I kept the line because six points cannot choose between forms.

Production is fixed where the evidence says it moves. The validation misses of Section 3 point at a planting response. The grid holds Q fixed and lets the Monte Carlo vary it historically, which prices the uncertainty without modelling the mechanism. A two equation version, area and share, is the obvious next build and the obvious next place to be wrong.

The largest sensitivity is an assumption. At the announced price, moving the holding period between three and seven months swings the total by 7.5 billion pounds, ahead of the world price at 4.8 billion and far ahead of the response curve at 0.5 billion. No public source prices the average holding period of the state stock. The total at 2,650 should never be quoted without that sentence attached, and the sensitivity ranking is printed in the results log so it cannot be detached.

The inherited limits stand. District water is split by land share from a built anchor of 11.00 billion cubic metres, so governorate level readings inherit a construction. The price and yield layers have measured coverage of 0.0 to 22.2 percent against a construction value of 50 percent, so expected money figures are thinner than feasibility and step results. Recourse observes the full scenario, including its yield layer, before allocating water, so margins and guarantee costs are lower bound readings. Scenario weights are stated planning weights, and the robust runs of the foundation exist because of that; they change the price very little and, on the ordering check, fail to dominate. None of this is new in this extension, and none of it is cured by it.

The target carries political weight the model did not choose. The 5.0 million tonne target is an announced number, carried here as the gap reference because the ministry itself announced it. A reader could fairly ask whether pricing a gap to an announced target, rather than to a cover level derived from consumption and stocks, gives the announcement more authority than it has earned. The grid reports cover and months beside tonnage so that the reader can rebase the gap if he wishes.

8. What comment would help most

Three questions, in order. Is a share line on a price ratio a defensible reduced form for a state buyer market, or does the endogeneity of the trader price to the announcement undermine the ratio itself? Is the fixed production grid with a historical Monte Carlo an acceptable interim treatment of the planting response, or does it understate the top of the grid enough to change the policy reading? And does the financial layer belong inside the same study as the allocation model, or does joining them lend the bill a precision the behavioural layer cannot support? The label stands as it stood for the foundation: a numerically verified exploratory model study on a stated basis, not validated empirical evidence. I would rather have that label attacked and repaired than have the numbers quoted without it.
