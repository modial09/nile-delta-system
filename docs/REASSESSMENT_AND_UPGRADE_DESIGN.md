# Reassessment and upgrade design for the Nile Delta water allocation project

Written 14 November 2026 at the founder order, after he stepped back from version 3 and ruled its analytic value too thin for an academic flagship. This document is the hostile but fair examination he asked for. It names what version 3 actually is, it designs the upgrade in buildable order, and it states what the upgrade costs and how it can fail. Nothing in this document is written to protect earlier work.

## Summary for the founder ruling

Version 3 is a correctly solved textbook linear program standing on a real data foundation, wrapped in an audit of genuine quality. It is not yet an analytics project. The model formulation is standard since at least the year 2000 and was applied to Egyptian cropping in 1994 and again in 2009. The scenario probabilities are counted from a real record, the duals are read correctly, the arithmetic replays to the pound, and the feasibility ceiling of 93.65 percent is a real result. But the project contains no computation the founder designed. The forecasting module he specified on 1 November 2026, with ridge against random forest and gradient boosting, rolling origin backtests, and forecast spread setting the scenario band width, was never built. It still sits in the code as a scaffold that raises an error when called. Prices are 2023 values and costs are 2016 to 2017 values, so every margin is a hybrid of two different economies separated by devaluations, and no margin figure measures any year that ever existed. The water budget that anchors every absolute number is a calibration, not a measurement. The headline gain of 47.74 percent is mostly the current pattern being graded against applied water duties it never faced, a fact the project own audit proved and the project presentation still led against.

The upgrade in one paragraph. Build the forecasting module exactly to the 1 November specification, with naive baselines it must beat, at least ten years of rolling origin backtest for every series, and errors printed beside every forecast the model consumes. Feed the allocation model with forecast prices and yields, with the backtest spread setting the price and yield band around them, and keep the inflow scenarios grounded in the Aswan record while adding a multi year drought state and a sampled robustness run so the guarantee ceiling is reported as a distribution and not as one point. Calibrate water requirements and district efficiency against satellite actual evapotranspiration from WaPOR behind an access gate in the first week, and let it fall back to a validation role if the gate fails. Add one risk measure, the expected staple shortfall in the worst decile of scenarios, because a food guarantee is a promise about the tail and expected value hides the tail. Put every stale or missing series under one rule: find it recent, or forecast it from history with its backtest error printed beside it, or carry it as a named gap with the cost of the gap stated. Kill the net water basis results, the decoration sensitivities, and layer two. The build runs four to six weeks at the current project hours, and the paper is written last, from the upgraded run only, or it is not written at all.

Decisions the founder is asked to rule on:

1. The forecasting module is built first, before any new allocation run, and no result ships from hand set prices or yields again.
2. The guarantee ceiling is reported as a distribution under sampled hydrology, with the single point figure demoted to one line inside it.
3. WaPOR enters behind a one week access and coverage gate, as calibration if the gate passes and as validation if it does not.
4. Layer two, the fertilizer routing model, is cut. The upgraded core plus the paper is the whole flagship.
5. If the build slips past the middle of December, the paper ships on the forecasting module plus the audited allocation core, and the satellite layer moves to an appendix, rather than the paper slipping past review time.

## Part 1. What version 3 actually is, without decoration

### 1.1 The computation is a formulation, not a programme of computation

The model is a two stage stochastic linear program in eight governorates, five crops and three inflow scenarios, solved by a library solver. Two stage stochastic programming for water resources has been textbook material since Huang and Loucks in the year 2000. It reached Egyptian cropping in 1994 in the work of Rosa and Yates and ran as a named two stage model on the Egyptian cropping pattern in 2009 in the work of El Shazly and co authors. The project prior work review established all of this and killed every novelty claim for the structure. What remains true and worth saying plainly is this: formulating the Delta problem in this structure is competent work, and it is the same competence a good textbook exercise demands. There is no algorithm in the project that the founder designed, no estimation, no learning from data, and no computation whose difficulty is the point. The one module that would have supplied that computation, the forecasting module of the 1 November design, exists only as a file of function signatures that raise errors. Three versions of the allocation model shipped around that empty file. An examiner from the programme this paper targets will find that file, or will ask the question that finds it, within minutes.

### 1.2 The headline gain does not mean what a reader will think it means

At the 90 percent floor the optimised plan beats the current pattern by 47.74 percent at the farm gate beet price and by 52.92 percent at the procurement beet price. The audit decomposed the growth of that gain against version 2 and found that 69.5 percent of the growth, in the cleaner of the two variants, comes from the baseline margin falling, not from the optimised margin rising. The baseline falls because the current pattern is evaluated under applied water requirements, rice at 16,600 cubic metres per hectare, while its full watering demand of 16.724 billion cubic metres stands against an expected budget of 10.505. Under that grading the current pattern delivers 69.50 percent of its full water staple output in expectation. So the 47.74 percent is mainly a statement that the observed Delta, judged by one second hand table of applied duties and one calibrated budget, is water starved. That may be true and important. It is not a statement that reallocation creates half again as much farm margin, and any reader who takes it that way has been misled by the presentation, however honest the footnotes are. The audit said this in its own verdict. The results were still summarised upward from the gain. That is the bias the founder named, and on the evidence he is right.

### 1.3 The money figures are hybrids of two economies

Prices are FAOSTAT 2023 levels. Costs are CAPMAS 2016 to 2017 levels. Between those years Egypt passed through steep inflation and currency devaluation, a fact the foundation itself records when it notes that the one later wheat cost anchor, about 40,476 pounds per hectare for 2022 to 2023, sits at nearly double the 2016 to 2017 wheat cost of 21,473.81. A margin built as 2023 revenue minus 2016 to 2017 cost is larger than the margin of either year. It measures no season any farmer lived. Ratios built from two such margins partly cancel the error, which is why the gain percent is less damaged than the levels, but the pound figures printed throughout the results record, margins of 95 to 262 billion pounds, carry a precision their construction cannot support. The honest treatments are two: put prices and costs in the same year by forecasting one of them, with the error shown, or report margins as an index against the baseline and demote the pound levels to an appendix with the year warning on every page. Version 3 does neither. It labels the years, which is better than hiding them, and then prints the levels anyway, which is where the labelling stops protecting the reader.

### 1.4 The water budget is a calibration, and the whole result set leans on it

No official water budget exists for the eight governorate model area. The expected budget of 11.00 billion cubic metres is a calibration set just under the water demand of the current cropping pattern, and the foundation and the audit both say so. The sensitivity record shows what that costs. Scale all budgets by 0.95 and the headline gain falls from 47.74 percent to 8.15 percent. Scale by 1.05 and it barely moves. A result that falls off a cliff when an unmeasured anchor moves five percent in one direction is a result about the anchor as much as about the Delta. The feasibility ceiling of 93.65 percent is the strongest finding in version 3 precisely because the audit attacked it with an independent solver and it held, and because it depends on physical quantities, land, yields, requirements and the low budget ratio, more than on prices. Even the ceiling moves with the budget derivation, to 95.94 percent under the tercile derivation. Version 3 reports that. It does not report how the ceiling behaves across the hydrology the record actually contains, because it never samples that hydrology. Three hand placed points carry the whole uncertainty story of a river with a hundred years of record sitting in the project own data folder.

### 1.5 The scenario set is thinner than the project own evidence demands

The foundation warns, in its own hydrology section, that Lake Nasser stores about two years of mean flow, that single year flow probabilities therefore overstate single year supply risk, and that the real risk is a multi year run like 1978 to 1987 arriving on a drawn down reservoir. The published mean for that decade is about 72 billion cubic metres a year, a ratio of about 0.83 against its reference mean, and it sits outside the extracted series, which ends in 1970. Version 3 then models risk as three single year states and never builds the multi year drought state its own foundation calls for. The expected value objective compounds this: a guarantee is a promise about bad years, and an expected value criterion is the criterion least able to see bad years. The project reports the value of perfect information and the value of the stochastic solution, which are textbook ornaments here, while reporting no measure of how bad the worst outcomes are. A food security reader will ask for the shortfall in the bad tail before anything else. Version 3 cannot answer.

### 1.6 Satellite data was found, praised and left in a footnote

The prior work review recorded that FAO WaPOR remote sensing tools hosted by the Ministry cover the Middle and West Delta, about 1.5 million hectares, showing water use, productivity patterns and crop water deficit. The foundation recorded exactly one efficiency anchor for the whole country, gross irrigation of 42 billion cubic metres against a computed net requirement of about 21, an efficiency of 50 percent, and flagged every net to applied conversion as an assumption resting on that single anchor. The obvious move, estimating actual evapotranspiration by governorate from WaPOR and deriving district level consumption and efficiency from measurement rather than from one national ratio, was identified by the founder in his instruction and was available throughout. It was not attempted. The water requirements that drive the feasibility ceiling, the single most important physical input in the model, still come from a second hand printing of a 2017 table whose basis the source paper does not state, arbitrated against a CROPWAT study whose Delta wheat row the foundation itself flags as internally inconsistent. That is the weakest load bearing wall in the project, and it is the one the satellite layer exists to test.

### 1.7 What version 3 genuinely has, stated fairly

Hostility without fairness is just noise, so the credit side, briefly. The data foundation is real work: FAOSTAT prices pulled in full and cross checked row by row, Ministry bulletin panels for land, pattern and yield across eight governorates, costs read from the official bulletin tables, and a gap list of 25 items that hides nothing. The audit is real work: it replayed the baseline without the model code and matched it to five parts in a million, attacked the infeasibility verdict with an independent solver, killed three defective sentences in the project own record, and graded every version 2 headline as survives, shrinks or dies. The claims discipline held: no word first, no average return dressed as a price, no saving claimed. The guarantee price list and the governorate by scenario water value map remain, on the prior work evidence, a combination no published work searched contains. The problem with version 3 is not dishonesty and it is not arithmetic. The problem is altitude. It is a well audited answer to a small question, presented in the clothes of a flagship.

## Part 2. The computation layer

### 2.1 The forecasting module, built to the 1 November specification

The founder specification of 1 November 2026, restated exactly: next season price and yield forecasts per crop; a ridge regression baseline against a random forest and gradient boosting; rolling origin backtests with errors reported in full; forecast means setting expected prices and yields in the allocation model; backtest spread setting the scenario band width. The scaffold in the code already fixes the feature discipline: lagged target values, a time trend, lagged fertilizer price and lagged planted area, and no feature may use information from the season being forecast. The upgrade builds that module, with three additions that the specification implies but does not spell out. First, two naive baselines, last value carried forward and a drift forecast from the recent trend, because a flexible model that cannot beat last year value is not a forecast and the paper must be able to say which case holds for each crop. Second, model selection per crop by backtest score, never by preference, so the paper can report that the forest won for one crop and the ridge won for another, or that nothing beat the naive line, in full. Third, prediction bands from the backtest residuals themselves, as empirical quantiles of one step ahead errors, so the spread that enters the allocation model is measured forecast error and not a chosen percentage.

Series to forecast, named one by one:

1. Producer prices in Egyptian pounds per tonne for wheat, rice paddy, maize, sugar beet and potatoes. Source: the FAOSTAT prices file already in the project, annual, 1991 to 2023, thirty three observations per crop, with rice and sugar beet missing 2019 to 2021. The hole is handled by the backtest design below, never by silent interpolation.
2. National yields in tonnes per hectare for the same five crops. Source: a direct FAOSTAT crops domain pull, which the foundation already owes to replace the Our World in Data mirror. The FAOSTAT yield series for Egypt runs from 1961 in the crops domain, which gives more than sixty annual observations per crop, the longest series the project owns. Yields are the forecast that matters most for the food floor, because the floor is a tonnage.
3. Planted area per crop is a feature, not a target, taken from the same FAOSTAT pull, lagged one season.
4. Fertilizer price as a feature, from the World Bank Pink Sheet series already in the project as a cross check file, lagged one season. Where the Pink Sheet lacks a clean annual fertilizer line for early years, the feature starts when the data starts and the models are compared on the common window only.
5. Production costs are not forecast by this module, because the honest series does not exist: two CAPMAS years per crop plus wheat anchors cannot train or test anything. Costs are handled in the data layer, in Part 3, under the gap rule. Naming this limit here matters. A module that pretended to forecast costs from two points would be exactly the decoration this document exists to kill.

Backtest protocol, binding:

1. Rolling origin evaluation with an expanding training window. One season ahead is the primary horizon, because the allocation model plans one season. Two seasons ahead is reported as a secondary table.
2. For prices: first training window 1991 to 2010, first test season 2011, then roll forward one year at a time to the final test season 2023. That gives thirteen test seasons, above the ten year floor the founder set. Origins that would train across the 2019 to 2021 hole for rice and sugar beet skip the missing years in both training and scoring, and the methods text says so.
3. For yields: first training window 1961 to 2000, test seasons 2001 to 2023, twenty three test seasons.
4. Scores reported in full for every crop and every model, naive lines included: root mean squared error, mean absolute error, mean absolute percentage error, and skill against the naive forecast as a ratio of errors, where a ratio above one means the model lost to doing almost nothing. No table may show only the winning model.
5. The forecast that enters the allocation model is for the next planting season after the last observed year, for prices the season priced at the forecast year level, and every forecast is printed beside its backtest error in the same table, in the paper and in the run log. A forecast without its error beside it does not enter the model.

How the outputs feed the allocation model:

1. Expected price per crop is the selected model forecast. Expected yield per crop is the selected model forecast. The 2023 price column and the 2014 to 2023 mean yield column retire as model inputs and survive only as history in the data appendix.
2. The band around each expected price and yield is set by the backtest spread: the low state at the lower residual quantile, the high state at the upper residual quantile, using the one step error distribution of the selected model for that crop. This is the founder sentence, forecast spread setting the scenario band width, made mechanical.
3. The scenario set becomes two layered. Inflow keeps its three record derived states, because the decision paper needs named inflow years a planner can repeat. Price and yield states enter as a second layer at low, expected and high, giving nine combined scenarios, with probabilities from the inflow record for the water layer and stated independence between the layers as a named assumption. An LP of this size, eight districts by five or more crops by nine scenarios, is still a small problem for any modern solver, so the cost of the second layer is honesty work, not computing time.
4. The target season is named in every output. A margin is then the forecast margin of a stated future season at stated forecast prices minus the stated cost basis, and the mixed year hybrid of version 3 dies by construction. Where costs remain at 2016 to 2017 levels under the gap rule, the margin is reported as an index against the baseline set to 100, with pound levels demoted and carrying the year warning, exactly as Part 1.3 demands.

### 2.2 Satellite evapotranspiration from WaPOR: calibration behind a gate

WaPOR, the FAO Water Productivity Open Access Portal, publishes actual evapotranspiration and biomass production from satellite data, with Delta coverage that the ministry already uses in hosted tools for the Middle and West Delta. It earns its place for one reason: it measures water actually consumed, by place, in recent years, which is the quantity the project currently infers from a second hand duty table and one national efficiency ratio. The uses, in order of value:

1. District efficiency from measurement. Aggregate WaPOR actual evapotranspiration over each governorate cropland area for recent seasons, divide by the applied requirement volume the model assigns to the observed pattern in that governorate, and the result is a district level ratio of consumed to applied water. That replaces the single 50 percent country anchor with eight measured ratios, each with a year on it, and it directly tests the applied requirement table the ceiling leans on.
2. Requirement arbitration. Where the satellite implied consumption for a crop and district sits far outside the span between the net and applied table values, the table value is wrong for that place, and the foundation records it. Sugar beet, whose applied figure of 9,426 is more than double its net figure of 3,927 with no third source to arbitrate, is the first case this test exists for.
3. Pattern cross check. WaPOR seasonal coverage gives an independent read on where crops actually grew, against the 2020 to 2021 bulletin pattern the baseline stands on.

The gate, because this layer can also eat the project: in the first week, one bounded task downloads WaPOR evapotranspiration for the Delta for three recent seasons and aggregates it to the eight governorates. If download, resolution or cropland masking fails, or the aggregation cannot be made to reconcile with governorate boundaries inside that week, WaPOR does not enter calibration. It enters instead as a validation appendix, comparing total model water consumption for the baseline pattern against the satellite basin total, one number against one number, with the agreement or disagreement printed. Either way the satellite evidence appears in the paper as evidence. What it may not do is appear as a promise the methods section cannot cash.

### 2.3 Scenario generation beyond three points, and one risk measure

Three additions were judged. Two earn their place. One is killed.

Earns its place, first: a sampled robustness run. Draw multi year inflow histories from the hundred year Aswan record by block resampling, build the water budget path for each draw on the same calibration anchor with its calibration label intact, and recompute the guarantee ceiling and the 90 percent floor gain for each. The paper then reports the ceiling as a distribution, for example the share of sampled histories in which a 95 percent floor can be held at all, instead of one point that flips from 93.65 to 95.94 when the derivation changes. This is cheap computation on data the project already holds, and it converts the most fragile claim in version 3 into the most robust one.

Earns its place, second: a multi year drought state. Add one scenario family built on the published 1978 to 1987 period mean, ratio about 0.83, representing a low run arriving in sequence rather than a single dry year, with the Lake Nasser storage caution from the foundation stated beside it. The guarantee then gets tested against the event the system actually fears.

Earns its place, third: one risk measure. Report, for the recommended plan and for the current pattern, the expected staple shortfall in the worst decile of scenarios, that is the average amount by which staple production falls below the floor in the worst ten percent of outcomes, alongside the expected margin. One number, computed from runs the project already performs, that answers the food security reader first question. A minimax regret check on the recommended planting may ride in the sensitivity appendix if it costs nothing extra. Nothing more.

Killed: a full many scenario stochastic program as the headline model, machine learning inside the optimisation, and any price endogenous supply response. Each is a thesis of its own, none is needed for the decisions in Part 4, and each would arrive after the application deadline wearing the costume of rigour. Decoration is anything whose absence changes no decision and no number a reader acts on. By that test the version 3 ornaments also die: the value of perfect information and the value of the stochastic solution move to the appendix, and the net water basis run is withdrawn entirely, because it rests on a wheat row the foundation flags as internally inconsistent and its guarantee never binds, so it can only confuse.

### 2.4 The seasonal structure fix the data now supports

The foundation records that the model land constraint binds winter and summer plantings in one pool, which matches neither published land concept, because one physical hectare can carry a winter crop and then a summer crop while the model makes them compete. The bulletin panels are season split, so the fix is available: write the land constraint as two pools, a winter pool and a summer pool, each bounded by cultivated area, with potatoes split into winter and summer parameter sets as the foundation gap list demands. This is a genuine model improvement bought with data already in hand. It removes a standing modelling apology, it makes the land base a measured concept instead of a chosen one, and it changes the baseline accounting enough that every version 3 land figure must be rerun, which Part 5 prices honestly.

## Part 3. The data layer: one rule for every stale or missing series

The rule, applied to every series with no exceptions and no silent carries: find it recent, or forecast it from history and print the backtest error beside every use of the forecast, or carry it as a named gap with the cost of the gap stated in the same place the affected result is printed. The table of series follows. Hunt means a bounded search with a hard stop, including Arabic language sources, ministry and CAPMAS publication routes, university repositories in Egypt, and the FAO document repository, because the founder is right that the first pass stopped at the accessible minimum for several of these.

1. Production costs by crop. Status now: official tables end at 2016 to 2017, one wheat anchor at 2022 to 2023, one wheat forecast at 2023 to 2024. Hunt: the later CAPMAS income bulletin editions, including the 2023 to 2024 edition the foundation knows exists, through the CAPMAS site and its Arabic publication lists; the Ministry Economic Affairs Sector cost studies as standalone documents; Egyptian university journals that republish ministry cost tables. If the hunt fails: costs enter through a constructed escalation, the 2016 to 2017 crop costs moved by a named input price index built from the wheat anchor movement and the Pink Sheet fertilizer and energy lines, labelled constructed wherever it prints, and every margin is also reported at costs twenty percent above and below that basis. The cost of the gap, stated: margin levels carry construction error of that order; the guarantee ceiling does not depend on costs at all, because feasibility is physical, and the gain percent moved only between 47.64 and 47.91 percent across the version 3 cost swings, so the gap taxes the levels and leaves the rankings and the ceiling standing. That sentence, with its numbers, is what a named gap looks like when it is done properly.
2. Producer prices for 2024 and 2025. Status now: FAOSTAT annual values end at 2023. Treatment: forecast under Part 2.1, with the two honest anchors used as cross checks rather than inputs: the wheat procurement price of 14,667 pounds per tonne for 2025 and the sugar beet factory price of 2,400. The farm gate series and the procurement concept stay separate, as the foundation demands; the model keeps both beet concepts as named variants, forecasting the farm gate series and carrying the procurement price as the policy variant. The 2019 to 2021 hole for rice and sugar beet stays a hole in the training data, handled in the backtest design, and the monthly 2024 values in the FAOSTAT file are used as a validation read on the first forecast year, not averaged into a constructed annual figure.
3. National yields. Status now: riding on a mirror pull. Treatment: the direct FAOSTAT crops pull, which the prices pass proved is technically open, closes this in one task. Then forecast under Part 2.1. No gap remains here after the pull, and any delay in pulling it is a choice, not a constraint.
4. Nile inflow after 1970. Status now: no publicly extractable year by year table after 1970; reconstructions to 2015 and 2018 exist as figures and model inputs. Hunt: the data availability routes of the published reconstructions, MWRI yearbooks through Arabic sources, and Egyptian and Sudanese university theses that print gauge tables. If the hunt fails, the named gap stands and its cost is stated wherever probabilities print: the probabilities describe the gauged record of 1871 to 1970 and the published period means after it, not the modern river, and the drought state of Part 2.3 exists precisely to keep that gap from silently flattering the risk picture.
5. Crop water requirements. Status now: applied values from a second hand printing of a 2017 table, net values from a CROPWAT study with one flagged row, no single consistent source. Hunt: the original CAPMAS table and the MWRI duty tables in Arabic ministry publications. In parallel, the WaPOR gate of Part 2.2. Whatever the hunts return, sugar beet applied water is carried as a range with the model run at both ends as a structural sensitivity, never as one picked number, until a measurement arbitrates. The cost of the gap, stated: the guarantee ceiling moves with the requirement basis, as the version 3 net basis run proved by never binding, so every ceiling figure prints its basis beside it.
6. Rice yield response factor. Status now: 1.20 carried unverified, possibly a growth stage value mistaken for a seasonal one. Hunt: the FAO Paper 33 original through the FAO document repository. If it stays unverified, the model runs rice at 1.0 and at 1.2 as a stated pair and the results print both, because a factor that multiplies the production of the most water hungry crop in the system may not ride on a guess.
7. Menoufia rice and the missing panel cells. Status now: not reported in the 2020 to 2021 source table. Hunt: the 2019 to 2020 summer bulletin row for Menoufia, which the partial panel may already hold. If it stays missing, the neutral fill of 1.00 used in version 3 is removed; Menoufia rice is excluded from the floor accounting with the exclusion and its size printed, because a filled cell that no source contains is an invented number wearing a flag.
8. Nili maize. Status now: tables located in the 2020 to 2021 summer bulletin, not extracted, understating maize by about 9 percent nationally. Treatment: extract them. This is not a hunt, it is unfinished extraction of a source already in hand, and it closes before any rerun.
9. Governorate panels for recent years. Status now: complete for 2020 to 2021, partial after. Hunt: the 2021 to 2022 and 2022 to 2023 bulletin editions and the summer half of 2023 to 2024, same routes as the first pass plus Arabic search terms for the bulletin titles. If they land, the baseline pattern updates to the newest complete year and the paper says which year the Delta it describes is. If they do not, the baseline stands at 2020 to 2021 with the year printed in the abstract, because a reader is owed the age of the pattern the whole comparison rests on.

## Part 4. The value layer: what a decision maker gets that nothing public gives today

Each item below names its reader, the decision it changes, and the numbers the current record supports. Where a number comes from the upgrade rather than from version 3, the item says so and claims nothing yet.

1. The guarantee price list, kept and repaired. Reader: the planning desks of the Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation and the Ministry of Agriculture, and the supply side reader who weighs domestic guarantee against imports. Decision: what staple floor to promise for a season, and what that promise costs in farm margin against the alternative of importing the shortfall. Numbers the record supports: at the 90 percent floor the optimised margin is 140.51 billion pounds at the farm gate beet price against a baseline of 95.11, with the decomposition printed beside it; the 95 and 100 percent floors cannot be held under the primary derivation; the ceiling is 93.65 percent. The upgrade adds the comparison the prior work review asked for and version 3 never built: the margin cost per guaranteed tonne set beside the cost of importing that tonne, from the Pink Sheet wheat line converted at a stated rate. A price list without the import comparison is a number looking for its decision. With it, the list answers the only question its reader actually brings.
2. The governorate water value map, kept. Reader: governorate water directorates and the ministry allocation desk. Decision: where an extra cubic metre, a transfer between districts, or a maintenance priority buys the most margin and the most staples in a dry year. Numbers the record supports: dry year conditional values from 49.78 to 75.52 pounds per cubic metre at the farm gate beet price and from 43.91 to 78.57 at the procurement price, against expected year values of 6.17 to 13.82 and 12.31 to 16.63. The ministry own productivity maps rank crops by average return per unit of water; this map prices the marginal cubic metre by place and by year type, which is the number a transfer decision actually turns on, and no published work searched contains the governorate by scenario set.
3. The next season outlook, new from the forecasting module. Reader: the same planning desks before the season opens, the wheat procurement price setters, and farmer cooperatives choosing plantings. Decision: the procurement price announced before the wheat season, the planting guidance issued with it, and the import cover arranged against the expected shortfall. This output does not exist in any public tool the prior work review found, because the ministry stack simulates and monitors but does not publish a crop price and yield outlook with a measured error record. Its value is exactly its honesty: a forecast that prints its own backtest beside it can be weighted by its reader; a forecast that hides its record cannot. If the backtests show no skill beyond the naive line for a crop, the outlook says so for that crop, and that sentence is itself information no current public source gives.
4. The guarantee ceiling as a distribution, with the tail shortfall, new from Part 2.3. Reader: food security decision makers above the allocation desk. Decision: how much import cover, reserve or contract cover to arrange in advance for the seasons in which no planting can keep the promise at home. Version 3 gives one ceiling point and no tail measure. The upgrade gives the share of sampled hydrologies in which each floor level can be held, and the expected shortfall when it cannot. That is the difference between a model that describes an average year and a model a food security reader can insure against.
5. District efficiency from satellite measurement, new if the WaPOR gate passes. Reader: the irrigation modernisation and canal improvement programmes that must choose where capital goes first. Decision: which governorates get lining, metering and improvement money in which order. The measured ratio of consumed to applied water by governorate, against the single national 50 percent anchor the whole field currently leans on, is a targeting instrument. No paper in the prior work map publishes it for the eight Delta governorates, because the academic models use table duties and the satellite tools stop at monitoring maps.
6. What is not value, and stops being presented as if it were: pound margin levels built from mixed years; the value of perfect information as a headline; any district ranking printed without the requirement basis beside it; and the net basis results, withdrawn. A reader who is handed fewer numbers, each of which survives attack, has been given more than a reader handed forty that need footnotes to live.

## Part 5. Build order and the honest cost

Sequence, with the gate at each step stated:

1. Freeze version 3 as the audited baseline. No version 3 figure is edited in place after this document. The kill and withdraw list in step 6 is written into the results record header.
2. Week one, the data hunts of Part 3 and the WaPOR gate of Part 2.2, run in parallel, each with a hard stop. Every hunt closes as found, forecast, or named gap, and the foundation is reissued as version 2 of itself with the outcomes recorded, including the failures.
3. Weeks one to two, the forecasting module of Part 2.1: the direct FAOSTAT crops pull, the feature build under the scaffold discipline, the five model lines including the naive pair, the rolling origin backtests, and the forecast table with errors. Gate: if a crop forecast shows no skill against the naive line, the module reports that and the allocation model consumes the naive value with its wider band. The gate is a reporting gate, not a stopping gate.
4. Weeks two to three, the allocation model version 4: seasonal land pools, potatoes split by season, Nili maize extracted, forecast prices and yields as inputs, the nine scenario set, the drought state, the sampled robustness run, the tail shortfall measure, and the import parity comparison. Then an audit of version 4 at the same hostility as the version 3 audit, replaying from raw parameters and attacking the ceiling under the sampled hydrology.
5. The satellite layer lands where its gate put it, calibration inside version 4 if the gate passed early enough, validation appendix if it did not.
6. Withdrawn or demoted under the upgrade, named so nothing drifts: the version 3 pound margin levels as current claims, replaced by same season forecast margins or by indexed margins where costs stay constructed; the net water basis results, withdrawn in full; the single point ceiling as a headline, replaced by its distribution; the value of perfect information and the value of the stochastic solution as headlines, demoted to the appendix; the Menoufia rice fill, removed. Kept intact: the feasibility method and its independent solver test, the decomposition discipline, dual reporting in conditional units, and the baseline built from the observed pattern.

## Part 6. What could make the upgrade fail

1. Forecast skill no better than the naive line, for prices especially. Egyptian farm gate prices are policy moved as much as market moved, and a series with administered jumps may simply not be forecastable a season ahead from its own history and a fertilizer line. The design survives this, because the band widens and the paper reports the failure as a finding, but the outlook output of Part 4 loses its shine and the paper must not pretend otherwise.
2. WaPOR access or coverage fails the gate: download friction, resolution too coarse against governorate boundaries, or a cropland mask that cannot be reconciled with the bulletin areas. The fallback is designed, but the efficiency map, one of the two genuinely new outputs, dies with the gate.
3. The recent cost tables stay behind a wall. Then margins remain partly constructed, the paper leads with the ceiling, the price list in indexed form and the water map, and the pound levels stay demoted. The flagship survives, thinner.
4. The inflow record stays shut after 1970. The probabilities then describe a river that no longer exists in that form, with the drought state carrying the honesty. An examiner may still mark the hydrology as the paper limit. It is a real limit and the paper should carry it in the abstract rather than meet it in review.
5. Time. The most likely failure of all: five honest layers attempted, three finished, the paper written in a rush from a half audited run. That outcome is worse than version 3, because version 3 is at least finished and audited. The build order exists to prevent it, and the fallback ruling exists for the day it starts happening anyway.

## Part 7. Recommendation

Stop improving version 3 and build the project the founder designed on 1 November and then watched get built around: a forecasting module with a public backtest record feeding a stochastic allocation model whose scenarios come from measured hydrology and measured forecast error, whose water requirements are tested against satellite measurement, whose guarantee is priced against the import alternative and reported as a distribution with a tail, and whose every stale input is either found, forecast with its error showing, or named as a gap with its price attached. That project is substantial on its face, because each of its outputs answers a named reader decision that no public tool or paper in the searched record answers today, and its computation is the founder own, which is the whole point of a flagship. Write the paper last, send nothing to Professor Anusha or to any MIT reader before the upgraded run has survived its own audit, and let the record show one clean arc: a textbook model, honestly audited, then rebuilt into an analytics system. The rebuild is the story. Version 3 is only its first chapter, and it should stop being presented as the book.
