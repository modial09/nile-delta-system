# Foundations of the Nile Delta project

Combined version 2, written 14 November 2026. This is the version the build starts from. It combines the project foundation of the same day with the independent GPT audit of version 3 (docs/GPT_AUDIT_v3.md), read claim by claim. The earlier version is kept as FOUNDATIONS_v1_preGPT.md so the change is visible.

## What the external audit changed, errors first

The audit proved six defects in version 3 that our own audit did not catch. Each one is adopted here as a repair, not debated.

First, the land pool was physically wrong. The old model let all five crops compete for the total annual cropped area of each governorate. Annual cropped area counts a field twice when it carries two crops, so the pool let winter planting exceed the physical land. In the high beet price run, wheat and sugar beet alone took about 1.896 million hectares of winter land against 1.677 million physical hectares in the eight governorates. That plan cannot be planted. The gain of 52.92 percent attached to it is withdrawn as an implementable result. The repair is in Section 5: land is modeled by season, against physical cultivated area, with the observed area of unmodeled crops held as a reserve.

Second, the value of the stochastic solution was computed against the wrong benchmark. The code planned at the middle scenario budget of 10.505 billion cubic metres and called it expected. The true probability weighted mean budget is 11.0011. Worse, when the audit planned at the true mean, the resulting plan could not hold the 90 percent floor in the dry scenario at all. The two VSS figures in version 3 are withdrawn as standard values. The repair is in Section 6: benchmarks use true means, and infeasibility of a benchmark plan is reported as a finding, never replaced silently.

Third, the feasibility ceiling was overstated in kind. Two solvers reproducing 93.65 percent proves the arithmetic, not the formulation. The audit showed that removing the crop area caps alone lifts the ceiling to about 95.52 percent. The correct claim, adopted everywhere from here: the 95 and 100 percent floors are infeasible under the stated budgets, caps, yields and land formulation. The ceiling is a result about the stated model, and its sensitivity to the caps and to the water duties is printed beside it, never a claim that the Delta physically cannot protect 95 percent.

Fourth, crop failure was not what the text said. With the production floor at zero and a response factor above one, the formula forced a minimum irrigation share on planted crops, about 16.67 percent for rice and 20 percent for maize, even when the harvest was zero. A written off crop in the old text was not a crop that received no water. The repair is in Section 5: abandonment is a real decision in the model, with water at zero, output at zero and the planting cost sunk.

Fifth, the guarantee was never actually priced. Comparing the floored plan with the historical pattern does not price a guarantee. The price of a floor is the margin given up against the same model with no floor, and against the next lower floor. The audit reconstruction puts that cost at about 26.6 and 38.3 billion pounds for the 90 percent floor inside the old formulation. Those numbers inherit the old defects and will be recomputed, but the comparison itself is now the headline output of the model, specified in Section 6.

Sixth, the stress language confused choice with fate. Under a 20 percent water cut, the unfloored profit objective chose zero staples, while the physical capacity to produce staples at that budget was about 76 percent of the reference output. From here, every stress result reports both numbers: the maximum staple capacity, and what the profit objective chooses. The first is physics. The second is a choice, and the paper will say so.

Two further corrections are adopted. The district yields in the old model were rescaled to national means, which put modeled maize about 16 percent below the published district yields; the floor was therefore a share of a constructed reference, not of observed output. Published district yields are now the primary basis, the rescaling is a sensitivity, and the quantity is labelled by its true name, an area weighted aggregate yield. And the gain decomposition is an accounting identity, not a cause. No input is said to have caused a share of any change until controlled swap runs, one input at a time, isolate it.

What the audit confirmed stands with equal weight: the two stage architecture is correct, the dual conversion is correct in all 48 cells, the scenario arithmetic reproduces, the headline percentages reproduce as conditional model arithmetic, and the sugar beet contrast survives. The build keeps all of it.

## 1. The problem and the end goal

Egypt grows most of its food in the Nile Delta with a fixed share of Nile water. Each season, someone decides, in practice if not on paper, which districts and which crops get that water. Today that decision has no published price for a guarantee: nobody can say what it costs, in farm income, to promise that staple food output will hold in a dry year, or what one more cubic metre of water is worth in one district against another.

The end goal of this project is to make that decision priced and visible. The model must answer three questions with numbers a decision maker can use. What cropping plan holds staple output at a stated floor across dry and normal years. What that promise costs, measured against the same model free to maximise income, and between one floor level and the next. And what water is worth, district by district and year type by year type, at the margin. Every computation in this project exists to serve those three answers. A result that does not serve one of them does not ship.

## 2. The value, by stakeholder

For farmers, the value is a plan that protects income in the bad year, not only in the average year. The model reports what a cropping plan earns in expectation and what it loses in the worst years, so a farmer facing it can see the trade in his own terms: margin kept in a normal year against margin protected in a dry one. The guarantee floor is stated as a share of a reference output built on observed crop areas, and the paper uses that exact wording, never the shorter claim that it is a share of current output.

For government agencies, in the Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation and the Ministry of Agriculture, the value is a priced guarantee and a district water value table. A planner who must defend an allocation can point to the cost of each level of food security, to the districts where water earns most at the margin, and to the years where the promise breaks. The project also returns something the agencies do not publish today: measured water consumption by governorate from satellite data, computed in this project from public rasters.

For my professors and the academic team who will read this work, the value is method they can examine. Every forecast carries its own backtest error. Every stale series is either forecast under a stated rule or carried as a named gap with its cost stated. The physical plan the model proposes can actually be planted, season by season, on the land that exists. The claims are limited to what the prior work review proved, and the contribution is stated in the form the external audit accepted: this project implements an exploratory governorate scale stochastic allocation model for eight Nile Delta governorates, compares outcomes against historical crop areas, and reports staple floor feasibility, conditional expected margins, and scenario specific water duals.

## 3. How the parts connect

The project has four layers, and each layer passes a defined product to the next.

The data layer holds every input with its source, its year and its unit. It produces one clean parameter set and one gap register. Nothing enters the model from outside this layer.

The forecasting layer reads the long price and yield series from the data layer. It produces, for each crop, a forecast for the next planting season and a band around that forecast taken from measured backtest error. Those two products, the expected value and the band, are what the allocation layer consumes. No hand set price or yield enters the model again.

The satellite layer reads public WaPOR rasters and produces measured actual water consumption by governorate and season for 2018 to 2025. That product calibrates the water side of the allocation model and replaces the conversion from net to applied water that the old versions rested on.

The allocation layer is the model itself. It reads the parameter set, the forecasts and their bands, the measured consumption, and the inflow record. It produces the plan, the priced guarantee frontier, the water value for each district in each year type, and both stress numbers, capacity and choice. The paper is written from these outputs and from nothing else.

## 4. The data the model stands on

Land, cropping pattern and yields by governorate come from the Ministry of Agriculture Agricultural Statistics Bulletins, read first hand. The eight governorate panel for 2020 to 2021 is complete for the five crops, and two further seasons were added in the second sweep. The model uses two land quantities, kept apart at all times: physical cultivated area, which constrains each season, and annual cropped area, which describes the year and constrains nothing by itself. Seasonal areas of the five crops come from the bulletin season tables, and potatoes are carried as separate seasonal crops, winter, summer and Nili, with their own areas, costs and water figures where the sources give them.

Costs stand on two official bulletin families. The CAPMAS income bulletin gives 2015 to 2017. The Ministry cost bulletin, the Bulletin of Cost Statistics and Net Return (نشرة إحصاءات التكاليف وصافى العائد), published by the Economic Affairs Sector (قطاع الشئون الاقتصادية), gives full component costs by crop and governorate from 2019 to 2022. No official cost table exists after 2022. The years after 2022 are forecast territory under the rule in Section 6, carried from the 2022 base with the wheat anchor for 2022 to 2023 as a check, and every such figure is labelled an estimate. Because cost escalation is crop specific, no uniform percentage band is accepted as a sensitivity for costs; each crop carries its own range or its own named gap.

Prices come from the FAOSTAT prices domain. Wheat, maize and potatoes now run to 2024 in the December 2025 vintage. Rice and sugar beet farm gate prices stop at 2023 and are carried as named gaps. The sugar beet factory procurement price is documented separately, to an announced 2,300 pounds per tonne for 2026 to 2027, because the farmer sells to the factory and the two prices answer different questions. The model runs the beet price as a stated choice between the two concepts, never as a silent default, and no comparison between the two runs attributes their difference to the price concept alone, since year and marketing basis move together.

Water stands on three legs. Field measured applied water and consumptive use, read in the original Egyptian journals, now exist for wheat, rice, maize and sugar beet under Delta conditions, with sugar beet arbitrated at about 5,600 to 6,600 cubic metres per hectare. Satellite measured consumption from WaPOR covers the whole Delta for 2018 to 2025. And the inflow record is the gauged Aswan series, 1871 to 1970, labelled as gauged flow and never as natural flow. The national frame is the National Water Balance for 2025, 65.35 billion cubic metres of fresh water, which supersedes the older conflicting totals. Water accounting is kept at one boundary throughout: a budget and a requirement are never mixed across the applied and net bases in the same run, and any run that changes the basis changes the supply side with it, so the old net basis sensitivity, which changed requirements against an unchanged budget, does not return.

The gaps that remain are named here so the build never trips over them silently: no year by year flow record after 1970; the 1978 to 1987 drought known only as a period mean; the rice price after 2023; the rice yield response factor unverified in its original source, carried with a tested range instead of a point; no published release series for the Delta, so the model area water budget stays a calibration on the release side while becoming measured on the consumption side; and the Menoufia rice cell for 2020 to 2021, which its own source leaves blank and which no fill in this project treats as a zero.

## 5. The physical model

The allocation model is a stochastic program over eight governorates and five crops, with planting decided by season. Winter planting is fixed before the year type is known. Summer planting is decided with the year type observed, because the inflow is known by then. Water is allocated inside each season and each year type. This two decision point structure replaces the single planting moment of the old model, which forced winter and summer crops to share one information state they do not share in the field.

Land binds by season. In each governorate and each season, the area of modeled crops cannot exceed the physical cultivated area minus the observed seasonal area of unmodeled crops, which is held as a reserve. A plan that needs land that does not exist in that season is infeasible by construction, and the solver never sees the annual cropped area as a resource. Crop area caps from the old model are retired as a silent device: where a cap is needed to keep a plan agronomically sane, it is stated as a named constraint with its reason, and the feasibility results are reported with and without it, because the audit proved the ceiling moves when the caps move.

Crop failure is a decision, not an arithmetic side effect. For each planted crop in each scenario, the model may abandon the crop: water zero, output zero, planting cost sunk. The yield response relation applies only to crops the model keeps. This makes the program mixed integer at a size the solver can carry, eight governorates by five crops by the scenario set. If solve times prove otherwise in the build, the fallback is stated now, before it is needed: keep the linear form, rename the restriction honestly as a minimum irrigation to a viable stand, and print the audit diagnostic on the baseline beside the results.

Prices in the model are planning prices for stated scenarios. Where an optimized plan expands a crop to several times its observed area, as the old model did with potatoes at about five times the baseline, the plan must carry a market absorption note: the area at which the fixed price stops being credible, stated as a bound or a named limitation. Land, labour and market limits are part of a plan a reader can believe, not footnotes after it.

## 6. The method

The forecasting module is built first, before any new allocation run. For each crop it forecasts next season price and next season yield. The candidates are a ridge regression, a random forest and a gradient boosting model, and two naive lines that any real model must beat: last value carried forward, and a drift line from the recent trend. Features use only information available before the season being forecast: lagged values, a time trend, lagged fertilizer price and lagged planted area. Selection is per crop, by backtest score, and the full score table ships, including the crops where nothing beats the naive line. The backtest is rolling origin over at least ten test seasons for prices and over twenty for yields, and it reports root mean squared error, mean absolute error, mean absolute percentage error and skill against the naive line. The band that enters the model is the empirical spread of the one step backtest errors. A forecast is never printed, in the paper or in a run log, without its error beside it.

One rule governs every stale or missing input in the project: find it recent, or forecast it from history with the forecast tested on at least ten years and its error printed beside every use, or carry it as a named gap with the cost of the gap stated. No silent carries.

The guarantee is reported as a frontier, not as one row. The model runs the staple floor at a ladder of levels from low to the feasible ceiling, and for each level reports the expected margin, the cost against the unconstrained optimum of the same model, and the cost against the next lower level. The floor itself is described exactly: every represented scenario must deliver at least the stated share of a modeled reference quantity of aggregate wheat, paddy rice and summer maize tonnage. The paper does not claim protection for each crop separately, for calories, or for milled rice, because the model does not hold those.

Information values are computed against true means. The expected value problem plans at the probability weighted mean of the uncertain inputs. If that plan cannot hold a floor in some scenario, the paper reports that fact and any penalized variant under its penalty, named as such. No middle scenario is ever labelled expected again.

The scenario set has two layers: inflow states from the Aswan record, and price and yield states set by the forecast bands, with the independence between the layers stated as a named assumption. A multi year drought state enters the inflow layer, because storage makes the multi year run the real risk. A sampled robustness run over the inflow record reports the guarantee ceiling as a distribution, and the expected staple shortfall in the worst decile of scenarios is printed as a headline measure, because a food guarantee is a promise about the tail.

The outputs are read under four disciplines. Water values are dual values and are reported as marginal values only; an average return per cubic metre is never called a price. Every gain figure travels with its decomposition, labelled as accounting, and causal language waits for controlled swap runs. Stress results always pair capacity with choice. And every claim in the paper is limited to the combination the prior work review proved, with the word first used only where that review earned it.

Layer two of the original design, the fertilizer routing model, is outside this foundation. This foundation is written for the core alone: forecasting, satellite calibration and the allocation model, with the paper as the final product. The routing layer returns only if the core is finished and written early enough that it cannot endanger the paper.

## 7. The Arabic research layer

Part of this research must be in Arabic, because the primary record of Egyptian agriculture is in Arabic. This is a structural part of the foundation, not a translation task at the end. The rule is: for any Egyptian primary source, search in Arabic first, read the table in the original, and record the Arabic title beside the English one in the data appendix. The largest find of the project, the Ministry cost bulletin, sat in a public catalogue under its Arabic title and was missed for weeks because the first pass searched for the English republication. The field trial literature that arbitrated sugar beet water lives in Egyptian university journals, most of them Arabic first. The mining method from the second sweep stands as the project standard: follow the institutions own platforms, the ministry uploads index, the FAO catalogue and the Egyptian Knowledge Bank journals, before general web search.

## 8. The build order

First, the remaining data jobs: the direct FAOSTAT crops pull that replaces the mirror, the extractions from located bulletins including the Nili maize tables and the potato season split, and the WaPOR zonal statistics that produce measured consumption by governorate and season. Second, the forecasting module, with its full backtest tables. Third, the allocation model rebuilt to Sections 5 and 6: seasonal land with the unmodeled reserve, two planting decision points, real abandonment, the guarantee frontier, true mean benchmarks, and paired stress reporting. Fourth, the audit of the new run, by the standard the external audit just set: an independent reconstruction, feasibility attacked at the formulation level and not only the arithmetic level, and errors reported first. Fifth, the paper, written last, from the audited run only.
