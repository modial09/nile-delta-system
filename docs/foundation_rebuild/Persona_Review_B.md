# Persona Review B: The Nile Delta Model Read by a Man From Inside the System

Independent review, written cold. Basis: paper/Research_Memo_v5.md, paper/Executive_Summary_v5.md, paper/Results_Note_v5.md, docs/foundation_rebuild/Gate_A_Final_Statement.md, docs/foundation_rebuild/S2_Data_Quality_Report.md, docs/foundation_rebuild/Check2_Domain_Critique.md, docs/foundation_rebuild/Memo_Clarity_Assessment.md, and the code in src/allocation_model_v5.py and src/forecasting_v5.py.

## The reviewer

Call him Engineer Adel. He is fifty eight. He started in the late nineteen eighties as a young agronomist with the agricultural directorate in Gharbia, walking mesqas in the morning and writing crop estimates in the afternoon. He spent six seasons on farm and beside farmers: tenancy disputes, night irrigation, the queue at the cooperative for subsidised fertiliser, the year the potato price collapsed and men left the crop standing. He then moved to Cairo, to the Economic Affairs Sector of the Ministry of Agriculture, where he worked on the very cost bulletin this project later found under its Arabic title. He knows who compiles it, how late it runs, and which of its numbers the minister actually reads. In his forties he retrained in statistics and operations research, and for the last decade he has been the man other departments send their models to before a model is allowed near a decision. He has killed more studies than he has passed.

How he thinks. He thinks in feddan, not hectares. He thinks in pounds per ardab for wheat, in cubic metres per feddan for water, in months of reserve cover for food, and in the self sufficiency ratio for politics. He trusts a number when he can walk it back to a field, a canal turn, or a tender. His first question for any study is never about the mathematics. It is: who holds the lever this number describes, and what does he do differently on Sunday morning because of it. His second question is: what did you leave outside the fence, and does the answer live there.

What he checks first when a study lands on his desk. The water. Then the wheat price concept. Then the winter season, because the winter contest decides the year. Then the units and the precision, because a man who quotes money to the last pound is telling him the number was born in a computer and never met a market.

What makes him stop reading. Three things. A single planner who controls both planting and water, because in Egypt those are two ministries and a cabinet committee, and a study addressed to a man who does not exist will be read by nobody. Money stated to the exact pound on a basis the author himself calls mixed. And the word guarantee used for a quantity no Egyptian instrument has ever guaranteed.

## 1. What he recognises as true about the Delta

Some of this work he nods at, and the nodding is not polite.

* Water, not land, is the binding fear. The finding in Section 4 of the memo, that a tenth less water deletes the top rung of the ladder instead of raising its price, matches every dry year he has lived through. In a dry year the question in the ministry is never what things cost. It is what stops being possible.
* The corrected baseline finding rings true to him. The pattern Egypt actually plants holds the 50, 60 and 65 percent levels in every scenario and fails from 70. Of course it does. The fallah plants the food of his house first and the market second, a fact the memo itself cites from Ghazouani and co authors and then, in his reading, does not use. The old sentence, that the pattern holds no guarantee at any level, was the sentence of a man who had not yet understood what the pattern is for. Its withdrawal is the most honest page in the file.
* The seasonal discipline is right. Land binding by season against physical area, winter planting fixed before the summer water position is known, abandonment carrying a sunk cost: this is the calendar he knows, and most models he is sent get it wrong.
* The crop geography instincts are right. Rice heavy in the north, potatoes as the volatile money crop, sugar beet as a small contracted acreage. The instincts are right even where, as below, the mechanisms are missing.
* The Arabic cost bulletin. He compiled tables like it. An author who finds the ministry series under its Arabic title, reads it first hand, and then states in print that no official cost table exists after 2022 has done the thing he asks of his own staff and rarely gets. This part of the foundation he trusts completely.

## 2. What he challenges in the first five minutes

These are not modelling theory objections. Each one is a place where the model describes an Egypt he does not live in.

**First: there is no water budget per governorate, and water does not follow land.** The model takes a calibrated total of 11.00 billion cubic metres, a number no source publishes, as the memo states openly, and splits it among governorates by physical cultivated area share. The split is in the code, in src/allocation_model_v5.py, where district budgets are built as the state budget times the district share of physical land. In his Egypt, water arrives down canals on rotation turns. A head reach district and a tail end district with the same land do not receive the same water, have never received the same water, and the difference between them is the oldest political fact in the Delta. Tail end farmers pump groundwater and lift drain water, formally and informally, and that unpriced supply decides what their fields actually drink. By construction, every governorate in this model receives identical water per hectare, so the water value table in Section 4 of the memo, the table a reader will take for a scarcity map, measures crop mix and yields only. It cannot measure scarcity geography, because scarcity geography was assumed away before the first solve. He does not object to a calibration. He objects to a map drawn from it.

**Second: the probabilities come from a river that no longer operates.** The three inflow states and their weights of 0.2300, 0.4500 and 0.3200 are frequencies of gauged natural flow at Aswan from 1871 to 1970. Since the High Dam, the water Egypt receives in a year is a release decision, smoothed by storage, and the uncertainty a planner faces today is release policy and the filling politics of the upstream dam, not the flood distribution of the nineteenth century. A dry state defined as the driest fifth of a pre dam record is a history exhibit. It is not the dry year he plans against. Every expected figure in the memo is a weighted average over that dead river.

**Third: rice floats, and in Egypt rice is the one crop whose area the state actually fixes.** Rice area is permitted and limited each year for water reasons, with legal ceilings and fines for planting beyond them, set through the water ministry. The model lets rice move freely inside a cap share of 0.35 of district land, a number from the project own earlier version. Worse, in the northern Delta rice is not only a crop. It is the salt washing instrument for land that would otherwise go out of production, which is part of why farmers in Kafr El Sheikh and Damietta plant it beyond what its margin argues. A model that moves rice area for margin reasons, with no soil consequence and no legal range, is moving the one variable that is in reality moved by law and by salt.

**Fourth: the guarantee counts tonnes that are not food.** The floor is aggregate tonnage of wheat, paddy rice and summer maize. Paddy is counted before milling, so roughly a third of the rice tonnage in the promise is husk and bran. Summer maize in the Delta feeds poultry and cattle in large share, green and as grain. A promise stated in these tonnes overstates the food it protects by a margin no reader of the memo can compute from anything the memo prints. The memo is careful to say the floor does not protect calories or household access. He answers: then the word food in the title is doing work the constraint does not do.

**Fifth: the headline list stands on invented caps.** The memo tells the decision reader to read the capped price list first, the list where the 75 percent guarantee costs 32,666,817,585 pounds. The caps are wheat 0.50, rice 0.35, maize 0.40, sugar beet 0.30 and potatoes 0.15 of district physical land. The results note, Section 8, describes them in its own words as a modelling device carried from earlier versions. No market study sets them. No ministry source sets them. No rotation agronomy in the file sets them. The clarity assessment already in this folder says the word implementable is not earned, and he goes further: in his world a cap of 0.15 on potatoes is not a neutral device, because the potato cycle is the best known boom and collapse story in Delta farming, and the distance between the capped price of 32.7 billion and the relaxed price of 242.8 billion for the same promise is exactly the distance between two inventions. He is being handed a price list whose prices are set by the fences, and the fences were built by the author.

**Sixth: shortage in the model is cleaner than shortage in the field.** A planted crop in this model is kept with at least 0.30 of its full duty or abandoned at zero water with its cost sunk, decided district by district with the full scenario in view. A real farmer short of water does not abandon by decision at district scale. He waits for his turn, irrigates at night, buys pumping, takes water out of turn and accepts a yield loss that falls on the tail of the canal and not on the head. The binary keep or abandon choice, taken with harvest knowledge no irrigator holds, makes every guarantee in the memo cheaper than the same guarantee in his Egypt. The memo admits the information advantage and does not state its direction. He states it: the printed prices are lower bounds, and a ministry reader deserves that sentence on the first page, not an admission without a sign.

**Seventh: the money.** Prices are 2025 forecasts. Costs are official 2022 values. Egypt passed through very high inflation between those years, so every margin is revenue in one year pounds minus cost in another year pounds. The memo discloses this fully and then quotes the result to the last pound: 32,666,817,585. He stops at the precision, because precision is a claim, and this document own Section 5 withdraws the claim three pages later. He also notices what the cost escalation runs quietly prove: multiply all costs by 1.50 and the 80 percent guarantee gets cheaper, from 268.6 billion to 224.5 billion; multiply by 2.00 and it falls to 180.1 billion. A promise whose price falls when farming gets more expensive is a price measured against a fantasy free plan, heavy with fixed price potatoes, and not a property of the promise. The runs are honest. Their lesson is larger than the memo draws.

## 3. The question nobody in Egypt is asking

The memo serves, in its own Section 2, a planning authority that sets seasonal planting guidance and divides irrigation water among districts, and that may be asked to promise that staple output will hold in a dry year. That authority does not exist. Planting guidance sits with Agriculture. Water releases sit with Water Resources and Irrigation. The wheat reserve and the import programme sit with Supply and the grain buyer. A promise about staple output is a cabinet matter argued across all of them, and no one of them can buy the guarantee this memo prices, because no one of them holds both levers the model optimises together.

The questions his Egypt actually asks each year are narrower and harder. What procurement price per ardab will bring enough wheat to the state silos for the bread system. How much rice area to permit, and where. How many months of wheat cover to hold in store. How large the import tender programme must be, in a year when foreign currency is the binding constraint and the grain buyer is among the largest wheat buyers on earth. The ministry manages a self sufficiency ratio and an import bill. It has never managed an all scenario tonnage floor, and it could not act on one if it were handed one, because the floor is denominated in a unit, aggregate paddy and maize tonnes across eight governorates, that appears in no budget line, no tender and no decree.

The deepest framing fault follows from this. In the model, the guarantee can only be bought with farm margin, by forcing planting. In his Egypt, the promise is kept jointly by fields, by stocks and by ships. In a dry year the state does not only replant the Delta in its head. It tenders for more wheat. A price list for the promise that prices only the field leg, and never the import leg that in practice carries the bad years, overstates the cost of the promise by the whole value of the instrument Egypt actually uses first. The memo plans, in its own closing words, to think about the guarantee together with the instruments that cover the years when the promise breaks. He reads that sentence as the author glimpsing the real problem from inside the wrong frame. The instruments are not the backup. They are half the policy.

## 4. What he expected first and did not find

* Berseem, named. The largest winter planting in the Delta is clover for livestock, and the true winter contest, the contest that decides the wheat area every year, is wheat against berseem. In this model berseem sits inside the unmodelled crop reserve, split across seasons by a formula in proportion to modelled area, although clover is a purely winter crop. The domain critique already in this folder showed the split assumption is untested in the direction that matters, and version 5 ran a winter first split whose results the memo promises in its Section 3 and then never prints in Section 4. He expected the wheat and clover contest on the first page of the data section. He found a reserve.
* The procurement price as the primary wheat price, not a variant. To its credit, version 5 ran wheat at the documented procurement price of 14,666.67 pounds per tonne, the run the earlier critique demanded. But it ran as a sensitivity, while the headline ladder pays wheat at a forecast farm gate price of 17,891.58 that now sits above the state price. In his world the announced ardab price, set before sowing, is the price that moves the wheat area, because it is the price the bread system stands on. The variant should be the primary run, or the memo should argue in the open why the market price leads.
* Groundwater and drainage reuse as supply. The model water is applied duties against one calibrated budget. The Delta he knows drinks substantially from reuse and from wells, much of it informal, and that is precisely the supply that expands in a dry year and cushions the tail ends. A model of what the Delta can hold in a dry year that contains no reuse and no wells has removed the cushion and then measured the fall.
* The delivery system. Rotation turns, head and tail, night irrigation, the cooperative and the fertiliser card through which guidance actually reaches a tenant farming two feddan he does not own. Planting guidance in Egypt is not a plan imposed on hectares. It is a set of prices, permissions and input allocations pressing on millions of small decisions. Nothing in the memo is wrong about this, because the memo says nothing about it at all, and a decision reader will notice the silence.
* A food unit. If the word food stays in the title, he expects the floor restated at least once in milled rice and wheat flour terms, or in calories, beside the raw tonnage, so a reader can see what the promise feeds.

## 5. His verdict

What survives his reading. The skeleton: two planting decision points on the true calendar, seasonal land pools, a floor stated exactly as the model holds it, stress reported as capacity beside choice. The corrected baseline finding, which is the one result he would repeat in a ministry corridor: the existing pattern already holds about two thirds of the reference tonnage in every modelled state, so the live question is the price of the steps above it. The two world construction as a bounding device, honestly labelled in the results note. The label discipline of Gate A, which refuses the words validated empirical results until a specialist review happens. He notes, dryly, that this review is the kind the gate is waiting for, and that the gate was right to wait.

What does not survive. The price list as a decision instrument. Its headline form rests on invented caps, on probabilities drawn from a pre dam river, on a water total nobody measured split by a rule no canal obeys, and on money quoted to the pound across two different years. The water value table as geography. The guarantee as a description of anything an Egyptian institution could promise, buy or be held to.

The first three fixes he would demand, in order.

1. Rebuild the water side in objects that exist. State the budget as releases to the study area under a stated release policy, with the post dam record as the probability basis and the 1871 to 1970 series demoted to history. Split district water by delivery evidence if any can be found, and if none can, print beside the water value table that the split is by land and the table therefore ranks crop mixes, not scarcity. Until this is done, no figure in the memo should travel with the word Delta in front of it.
2. Price the promise the way Egypt keeps it. Add the import and stock leg beside the field leg, with the guarantee cost stated net of the covering instrument, and run the primary ladder with wheat at the procurement price and rice area inside its legal range. A guarantee price that cannot be compared with the price of a tender is a number without a rival, and no budget holder trusts a number without a rival.
3. Put the winter contest and the delivery reality into the frame, and fix the presentation to match the honesty. Name berseem as a crop or print the winter heavy reserve results the memo already promises. State in one sentence on the first page that recourse sees the full scenario, so printed guarantee costs are lower bounds. Report memo money in billions with one decimal, and give the summary one line in feddan and months of cover, the units of the reader the memo claims to serve.

His closing word. Send this to a professor as what Gate A says it is: a numerically verified exploratory model study, by an author whose honesty about his own work is rarer than his method, which is sound and not new. Do not send it to an Egyptian official as a price list for a food guarantee. In its present frame it prices a promise nobody can make, in units nobody budgets in, against a river that stopped flowing before the High Dam. Fix the water, price the ships, name the clover, and the same machinery becomes the first study of its kind he would let through his door.
