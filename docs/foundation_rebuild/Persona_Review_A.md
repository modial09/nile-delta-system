# Persona Review A: the Nile Delta version 5 work, read by a man from inside the system

Independent review. The reviewer below is a construction, built for this task. He is not a real person. Everything he says is grounded in the files of this repository, read on 16 November 2026: paper/Research_Memo_v5.md, paper/Executive_Summary_v5.md, paper/Results_Note_v5.md, docs/foundation_rebuild/Gate_A_Final_Statement.md, docs/foundation_rebuild/S2_Data_Quality_Report.md, docs/foundation_rebuild/Check2_Domain_Critique.md, docs/foundation_rebuild/Memo_Clarity_Assessment.md, and the code in src/allocation_model_v5.py and src/forecasting_v5.py.

## Part 1. The man

Call him Mahmoud. He is fifty eight. He was born in Tanta, in Gharbia, and his family still farms a small holding near Kafr El Zayat, wheat and berseem in winter, maize and some vegetables in summer. That land is why nothing in a study like this is abstract to him.

His career ran in three long stretches. First, twelve years in a Delta irrigation directorate, where his work was rotation schedules, canal deliveries, and the daily arithmetic of who gets water and who waits. He has stood at a mesqa at five in the morning in August and explained to angry men why their turn moved. Second, nine years inside the Ministry of Agriculture, part of it in the Economic Affairs Sector, the office that publishes the cost bulletin this project eventually found in Arabic, and part of it in planning work on cropping patterns, where he learned which numbers ministers ask for and which numbers they never look at again. Third, fifteen years auditing quantitative studies for ministries, donors and banks: sector models, water models, food security models. He has been paid to find the place where a model leaves Egypt and enters the imagination of its author. He is good at it and he is not kind about it.

How he thinks. He thinks in feddan, not hectares. He thinks in ardab per feddan for wheat, in cubic metres per feddan for water, in tonnes at the silo door, and in months of consumption cover for the country. One feddan is 0.42 hectares, and he converts every hectare figure in a document back to feddan before he trusts it, because a number he cannot picture on the ground is a number he does not believe. He asks three questions of any study before he reads its results. Who decides. With what instrument. Measured how. If a study cannot answer all three, he reads it as mathematics, not as policy, and he prices his attention accordingly.

What he checks first when a study lands on his desk. The water. Where the budget came from, whether a gauge or a release record stands behind it, and how it was divided among districts. Then the crop calendar, season by season, against what actually stands in the fields. Then the money: which year the prices belong to, which year the costs belong to, and whether the author noticed the distance between them. Then the probabilities: what record produced them, and whether that record describes the system that exists now. Only after all four does he look at a single result.

What makes him stop reading. A total with no gauge, no decree and no market behind it. A probability with no living record behind it. Money quoted to the exact pound by an author whose inputs are guesses. And the word guarantee in the mouth of anyone who does not hold the import budget, because in Egypt the food promise is kept at the port and in the silos at least as much as in the field.

## Part 2. What he recognises as true

He is not generous, so what follows is short, and every line of it is earned.

The land accounting is right. Land binds by season against physical cultivated area, and annual cropped area is treated as a description of the year, not as land. A field carrying two crops is one field. Foreign studies fail this test every year, usually by inventing land that does not exist. This build does not. He checked the construction in src/allocation_model_v5.py and in Section 3 of the memo, and it is the construction a man from the directorate would use.

The corrected baseline finding is true, and it is the best page in the file. The pattern Egypt actually plants holds the 50, 60 and 65 percent levels in every scenario and fails from 70 percent (Research_Memo_v5.md, Section 4; Results_Note_v5.md, Section 3). That matches the Delta he knows. The planted pattern is not an accident and it is not stupid. It is the settled answer of millions of households to water, risk, food for the house and the market, and it is more robust than any ministry plan he ever saw. The earlier version printed that this pattern holds no guarantee at any level, which was false, and the author withdrew the sentence in print. Mahmoud has reviewed studies for fifteen years. Almost nobody does that.

Water, not land, is the binding fear, and scarcity deletes options instead of raising their price smoothly. A tenth less water removes the top rung of the ladder whole (Research_Memo_v5.md, Section 4). Any man who has run a rotation in a dry year knows this in his body. In a bad August you do not pay more for the same schedule. Whole turns vanish.

The source work is real. The author found the ministry cost bulletin under its Arabic title after an English search had stopped at the CAPMAS income bulletin, which is not a cost source at all (Research_Memo_v5.md, Section 3; S2_Data_Quality_Report.md). He read the ministry own books. He also recorded that Delta farmers plant thirsty crops at canal tail ends for household food security and not for profit alone, citing Ghazouani and co authors, and that about half of main canal supply returns to drains and groundwater, citing El Agha, Molden and Ghanem. Both are true on the ground. He wishes the rest of the model had listened to them. More on that below.

## Part 3. What he challenges in the first five minutes

These are in the order he would raise his voice.

**1. The river in this model was buried in 1970.** The inflow states and their probabilities, 0.2300 dry, 0.4500 normal, 0.3200 high, are built from the gauged Aswan record of 1871 to 1970 (Results_Note_v5.md, Section 1; the band counts and ratios are computed from that series in src/allocation_model_v5.py). That is the river before the High Dam. Since the dam, the annual release is a decision taken with the storage of Lake Nasser in view. One dry year is absorbed by the lake. The danger Egypt actually manages is a run of dry years eating the storage, and a one year model with no storage variable cannot even state that danger, let alone price it. The probabilities in this model are honest frequencies of a river that no longer exists. A ministry reader stops on this page, because every scenario downstream drinks from it.

**2. The Delta water budget is a number nobody can check, divided by a rule nobody uses.** Every budget scales from a calibration anchor of 11.00 billion cubic metres that no source publishes, split across governorates by physical land share (Research_Memo_v5.md, Sections 1 and 5; Gate_A_Final_Statement.md, blocked claim 1). Mahmoud knows what district supply actually is. It is three waters mixed: canal releases, drainage water pumped back into canals and used again, and groundwater. The reuse share is large, several billion cubic metres a year across the Delta, and it is not spread by land. The northern and middle districts live on reuse in a way Qalyubia does not. One budget per district in proportion to land gives every governorate the same water per hectare by construction, and then Section 4 of the memo prints a table of water values by governorate and invites the reader to compare districts. The comparison was decided by the split rule before the solver ever ran. He would not accept one line of that table, and he notes the Gate A statement already blocks quoting the values as prices. He would go further. He would cut the table.

**3. Rice is modelled without its law.** Rice is the most controlled crop in the Delta. Its area is set by decree, with a ceiling in feddan announced for the year, fines for planting above it, and a second reality of violations that every official knows and no official prints. In the model, rice moves against land limits, or in the capped run against a cap of 0.35 of district physical land, a share carried from the author earlier version (Results_Note_v5.md, Section 8). That number comes from no decree and no agronomy. Worse, rice in the northern districts is not only a crop. It washes salt out of the soil. A plan that moves rice out of Kafr El Sheikh or Damietta to save water is spending soil that took decades to keep sweet, and the model has no account in which that cost could appear. The crop at the centre of Delta water politics is the crop the model understands least.

**4. Berseem, the largest winter land user in the Delta, never appears.** It sits inside an annual total of unmodeled crops, and the code splits that annual total across the three seasons in proportion to the modeled crops of each season (the reserve construction in load_all, src/allocation_model_v5.py). Berseem is a winter crop. It is also the feed base of the livestock economy: squeeze winter land and you tax milk and meat, which are food too. The winter first variant in version 5 is a bounding exercise, and its ceiling of 80.38 percent is reported honestly (Results_Note_v5.md, Section 9). But a bound is not the pattern. The same ministry bulletins the author read in Arabic publish what actually stands in each season. The winter pool, the pool where the guarantee is won or lost, was built by a splitting rule when the book that answers it was already open on the author desk. The Check2 critique made this point about the split assumption. Mahmoud makes it harder: the assumption was unnecessary.

**5. The money is read across an inflation break, then quoted to the pound.** Prices are 2025 forecasts. Costs are official 2022 values. Between those two years the pound lost most of its value and diesel, fertiliser, labour and land rent moved with it. A margin built as 2025 revenue minus 2022 cost is in part an inflation reading wearing the clothes of farm profit. The memo admits the mixed basis and prices it with two round multipliers, costs times 1.50 and times 2.00, admitted as bracketing assumptions (Research_Memo_v5.md, Section 5). Mahmoud calls that a guess wearing a label. A ministry economist would restate one side to the other year with the official price index before letting any pound figure leave the building. And no figure would leave quoted as 32,666,817,585 pounds. Precision of that kind, on inputs of this kind, is the fastest way to lose a serious reader in Cairo. The clarity assessment said the same in politer words. He is not polite. Round it or do not print it.

**6. One cost for the whole Delta, from a book that gives costs by governorate.** The memo says the bulletin gives component costs by crop and by governorate (Research_Memo_v5.md, Section 3). The code holds one national constant per crop (COST_2022 in src/allocation_model_v5.py). Rent in Menoufia is not rent in Damietta. Harvest labour near Qalyubia does not cost what it costs in Beheira. In this model, district margins differ only because district yields differ, so every district comparison the reader makes is thinner than the tables suggest. The author found the right book and then used only its national page.

**7. The layers move the whole world at once, and the recourse sees the harvest before it waters.** Inside a scenario, one layer moves every crop price and every district yield in the same direction together, and once the scenario is set, water allocation and abandonment respond with the full scenario in view, including the yield layer (the build function in src/allocation_model_v5.py; the memo admits the fact in Section 3 and never states which way it pushes the price). In the field, a bad wheat year in Beheira is not a bad potato year in Gharbia, and no irrigator has ever seen the yield outcome before choosing the water. The first choice removes every hedge the Delta actually owns, crop against crop and district against district, and charges the guarantee for the removal. The second choice quietly refunds part of the charge, because a recourse that sees the future holds floors more cheaply than any real season can. The two errors fight inside every price on the list, and nobody, including the author, can say which one wins. Until one of them is measured, the pound figures are the residue of a fight, not a price.

**8. The probabilities under the price list failed their own test.** The bands are built at the quartiles of the backtest residuals, and the measured coverage of the price bands is 0.0 percent for wheat and at best 22.2 percent for potatoes, against a construction value of 50 percent (Results_Note_v5.md, Section 11; Gate_A_Final_Statement.md, caveat two). Mahmoud translates: the layers claim that half of real years fall inside a band that, measured, holds almost none of them. Every expected margin in the memo is an average taken over weights the author own test reports as wrong. The hard floor results, the ceiling and the feasibility findings do not depend on the weights, and he accepts those at full strength. The price list is made of expected values. The price list is also the product. A product built on weights known to be wrong is not a product yet.

**9. The model plants no sugar beet at any price, and reports the zero as information.** At the farm gate price it plants no beet. At the documented factory price of 2,400 pounds per tonne it plants no beet (Results_Note_v5.md, Section 9). In the Delta, beet area is written by factory contracts before the season opens. It is not chosen feddan by feddan against a spot price, and it does not vanish because a margin table dislikes it. When a model cannot plant a contract crop under any price, the correct reading is that the model margin for that crop is wrong, in its cost, its yield, or the contract terms it cannot see. The memo reads the zero as a property of Egypt. It is a property of the model.

**10. The capped list, the one list he is told to act on, rests on invented bounds and was never tested against the weakest input.** The caps are shares of district land, wheat 0.50, rice 0.35, maize 0.40, beet 0.30, potatoes 0.15, carried from an earlier version, with no market or rotation evidence behind the levels (Research_Memo_v5.md, Section 4; the memo own Section 5 concedes that market evidence would turn the caps from a device into an estimate). Inside the caps, prices still do not move, so a capped plan that doubles potato area sells every extra tonne at the forecast price. And the anchor sensitivity, the best test in the document, was run on the relaxed ladder only. The capped ladder has never been repriced at 9.90 or at 8.80 billion cubic metres. The clarity assessment found this asymmetry. Mahmoud states its meaning: the number the reader is told to act on is the one number in the memo with no test against the input the author himself calls unmeasured.

## Part 4. The question nobody in the building is asking

No Egyptian official can set, buy or enforce a guarantee of aggregate staple tonnage for the Delta. There is no budget line for it. There is no instrument that delivers it. There is no office that would answer for its failure. Planting guidance from the ministry is advice, and farmers follow it in the years when it agrees with the market and ignore it in the years when it does not. Mahmoud watched this happen every season of his ministry years. A plan that replants eight governorates by decision is a fiction. It is a useful fiction for pricing, and the memo should say in one sentence that no allocator exists, instead of addressing itself to a planning authority who will never hold this lever.

The levers that exist are these. The wheat procurement price, announced before sowing, which moves wheat area more than any circular ever written. The rice area decree. Fertiliser allocation through the cooperatives, by crop and by feddan, on the farmer card, with the market price above the quota standing as a real cost and a real signal. The rotation schedule on the canals, which is the ministry of irrigation true instrument. And above all of them, the import programme and the strategic reserve. About half of the wheat Egypt eats is imported. The state food promise is kept in months of consumption cover, watched at the level of the silos and the tender calendar, not in a tonnage sum over three crops, one of them counted as paddy before milling.

So the framed question, what does a Delta tonnage guarantee cost, is a question the building does not ask, priced in a unit the building does not use. The questions the building does ask are close cousins, and this model, repaired, could answer them. What does it cost, in farm margin and in import bills, to raise the wheat self sufficiency ratio by five points, and which lever buys it cheapest. What tonnage and what months of cover are lost if the rice decree is tightened by 100,000 feddan in a dry sequence, and what is gained in water. What does each rung of cover cost if it is bought at the port instead of in the field. None of these is computed anywhere in the work.

## Part 5. What is missing that he would expect first

Storage. The High Dam and Lake Nasser are the country real guarantee mechanism across years, and they appear nowhere. A dry year promise modelled with single year states and no storage is a promise about a country that does not exist.

The three waters, named. Canal releases, drainage reuse and groundwater, by district, from irrigation records. Not one calibrated total folded into a single budget and split by land.

The rice decree as the rice constraint, with the violation reality stated beside it, and salinity in the northern districts carried as a cost of moving rice.

Berseem as a crop in the winter pool, from the published seasonal pattern, with the livestock link stated: winter land taken for wheat is feed taken from animals.

Fertiliser. It is allocated, subsidised, short in most seasons, and priced twice, once at the cooperative and once in the market. It drives both costs and yields. It appears nowhere in the allocation model, and the forecasting module records that its fertiliser feature could not even be built for lack of a series (src/forecasting_v5.py, opening notes). A Delta model with no fertiliser in it is missing the input farmers argue about most.

The eaters. Population demand, the wheat import bill, milling ratios for rice and wheat, and months of consumption cover. The word guarantee is a word about people who eat. No eater appears in this model, and no tonne is ever followed past the farm gate.

The holdings. Most Delta land is farmed in small and scattered parcels, a few feddan at most for most households, split by inheritance. Decisions are household decisions, taken for the house first. The memo cites this truth and then optimises as if it were false. At minimum the text should state who would have to move, feddan by feddan, for any rung of the ladder to happen, and through which instrument they would be moved.

## Part 6. His verdict

What survives his reading. The seasonal land accounting. The corrected finding that the pattern Egypt plants already holds 65 percent, which he regards as the one result in the file that a minister should hear. The demonstration that water scarcity deletes rungs rather than raising prices smoothly. The Arabic source work. And a discipline of printing weaknesses that he has not seen matched in donor work at ten times the budget. The arithmetic itself he does not contest: the independent verification recomputed it, and his quarrel was never with the sums.

What must change before this reaches a serious Egyptian reader or a professor. The water probability law, which describes the river before the dam. The district budgets, which are invented by a land share rule and then read as geography. Rice without its decree and its salt. Berseem buried in a reserve when the seasonal book was open. Money mixed across an inflation break and quoted to the exact pound. And a headline framed as a guarantee on a sum of three crops, with no eater, no import and no silo anywhere in the frame. Each of these is a place where the model leaves Egypt. A professor will find two or three of them. An Egyptian reader will find all of them in the first five minutes, and he will not reach the good pages.

The first three fixes he would demand, in his order.

Fix one. Rebuild the water side as Egypt runs it. Releases as a managed quantity across years with storage in the model, even in a simple carryover form. District supply split into canal, reuse and groundwater from irrigation ministry records. If those records cannot be had, then say the district level cannot be supported, cut the governorate water value table, and present the work at Delta level only. Do not print duals of an invented geography.

Fix two. Put the real instruments and the real winter into the model. The rice decree as the rice bound. Berseem as a winter crop from the published seasonal pattern. Beet carried at its contract area, not priced into a zero. Wheat split between the procurement price for the share the state buys and the market price for the rest, since the wheatproc variant already proves the machinery can do it. Then reprice the ladder, including the capped ladder at the lower water anchors.

Fix three. Restate the money and reframe the headline. One year for prices and costs, joined by an official price index. Every figure in the memo rounded to billions with one decimal, exact pounds in the results note only. And the central question rewritten in the units officials watch: the wheat self sufficiency ratio and the months of consumption cover that each rung buys, with the same cover priced at the port through imports, so the reader can see what the field is being asked to beat.

His last word, given on the record. This author can read Arabic ministry books, can build a correct stochastic model, and prints his own failures before his critics find them. Those are rare gifts and they are wasted on a question nobody holds the lever for. Point the same honesty at the river as it is now managed, at the crops as they are actually governed, and at the promise as Egypt actually keeps it, and the work becomes something a decision maker could pick up. Until then it is a well built instrument, carefully calibrated, measuring a country that is not quite this one.
