# Pakistan Comparison Expanded

## Egypt and Punjab: farm costs, berseem, water and policy

Research note, 16 November 2026. This note expands the comparison in the workbook Egypt_Pakistan_Farm_Costs.xlsx, which the user supplied. Every number below carries its publisher and its season or year. A number marked calculated was worked out from published figures by the method stated beside it. Where no source could be opened and read, the note says so and offers no number.

## Method

The method follows the workbook. Costs are gross farm costs per land unit, with land rent counted inside the total wherever the source counts it, and any adjustment is stated. Local cost per tonne uses the yield printed in the same budget or in the matching official statistics. Conversion to dollars uses the workbook exchange rates: Egypt 2022 at 18.998 pounds per dollar, Egypt 2024 at 45.314, Egypt 2025 at 49.221, Pakistan 2024 at 278.485 rupees per dollar. Land units: one feddan is 4,200 square metres, about 1.038 acres. One hectare is 2.471 acres. One Pakistani maund is 40 kg.

## What the workbook already holds

Egypt wheat cost 225.78 dollars per tonne for 2023 to 2024 and 239.41 dollars per tonne for 2024 to 2025, from the Agricultural Research Center budget. Punjab wheat cost 280.67 dollars per tonne for 2023 to 2024, from the Crop Reporting Service budget, with transport, handling and bags removed to reach a farm level cost. Egypt wheat therefore costs about one fifth less per tonne than Punjab wheat in dollar terms. Egypt rice cost 247.18 dollars per tonne for summer 2022, from the Ministry of Agriculture bulletin, against Punjab rice at 191.98 dollars per tonne for 2022 to 2023, from the Agricultural Policy Institute, so Egypt rice costs about one quarter more per tonne. The workbook also holds consumer price and exchange rate series for both countries for 2021 to 2025, from the World Bank, the Egyptian Ministry of Finance and the State Bank of Pakistan.

## Section 1. More crops

### Maize

Egypt, summer 2022. Cost 15,822 pounds per feddan including rent, and 11,338 pounds excluding rent, with rent itself 4,484 pounds. Yield 22.88 ardab per feddan. At 140 kg per maize ardab that is 3.203 tonnes per feddan, so the calculated cost is 4,939 pounds per tonne, or 260.0 dollars per tonne at the 2022 rate. Source: Ministry of Agriculture and Land Reclamation, Economic Affairs Sector, Bulletin of Cost Statistics and Net Return, Part 2, 2022, Table 3 for cost and the net return tables for yield. The ministry library index lists the 2022 edition as the latest cost bulletin.

Punjab. Yield only: 7,639.9 kg per hectare for 2023 to 2024, about 3,092 kg per acre. Source: Ministry of National Food Security and Research, Agricultural Statistics of Pakistan 2023 to 2024, May 2025, Table 8, which states the Crop Reporting Service as its source. No Punjab maize cost of production could be verified. The Crop Reporting Service cost of production PDFs could not be opened from this environment, and the Agricultural Policy Institute site yielded no maize budget. Search results for Punjab maize costs are dominated by Indian Punjab studies, which are a different country and were excluded. The maize pair is therefore half built: an Egypt cost with no Punjab cost beside it.

### Sugar: Egypt beet against Punjab cane, and cane against cane

Egypt sugar beet, winter 2021 to 2022. Cost 16,571 pounds per feddan including rent, and 10,844 pounds excluding rent. Yield 21.508 tonnes per feddan. Calculated cost 770 pounds per tonne, or 40.55 dollars per tonne at the 2022 rate. Source: Ministry of Agriculture, Economic Affairs Sector, Bulletin of Cost Statistics and Net Return, Part 1, 2021 to 2022, Table 9 for cost and Table 42 for yield.

Egypt sugarcane, summer 2022, from the same bulletin series, Part 2, 2022. Cost 29,707 pounds per feddan including rent. Yield 46.706 tonnes per feddan. Calculated cost 636 pounds per tonne, or 33.48 dollars per tonne.

Punjab sugarcane, marketing year 2024 (October to September). Cost 547,688.9 rupees per hectare as published, which the publisher itself converts to 221,814 rupees per acre. Land rent is a line inside the total, at 172,839.5 rupees per hectare. Punjab yield 79.1 tonnes per hectare for 2023 to 2024, about 32.01 tonnes per acre, from Agricultural Statistics of Pakistan 2023 to 2024, Table 13. Calculated cost 6,929 rupees per tonne, or 24.88 dollars per tonne at the 2024 rate. Source for cost: PACRA Sugar Sector Study, 28 August 2026, cost of production table, which states its source as AMIS, the Agriculture Marketing Information Service of the Punjab Agriculture Department. The cost table is presented for Pakistan sugarcane as a whole, not for Punjab alone, and that limit travels with the number.

Read: cane against cane, Punjab grows sugarcane at about three quarters of the Egypt cost per tonne in dollar terms, helped by a yield about 70 percent higher per unit of land. Beet and cane are different crops with different sugar content, so the beet figure belongs in the model as the Egypt sugar crop and the cane pair belongs in the comparison as a mirror only.

### Cotton

Egypt, summer 2022. Cost 24,452 pounds per feddan including rent. Yield 8.18 qantar per feddan of seed cotton. At 157.5 kg per qantar that is 1.288 tonnes per feddan, so the calculated cost is 18,979 pounds per tonne, or 999.0 dollars per tonne of seed cotton. Source: Ministry of Agriculture, Bulletin of Cost Statistics and Net Return, Part 2, 2022, Tables 10, 54 and 63.

Punjab, Kharif 2024. Cost 146,413 rupees per acre at the market gate, up from 116,930 rupees per acre in Kharif 2023, as stated by the Director General of the Crop Reporting Service and reported by Dawn on 17 June 2024. The statement does not say whether rent is inside the figure, and it gives no seed cotton yield, so no cost per tonne can be calculated for Punjab. The official Punjab yield available is lint only, 610.2 kg per hectare for 2023 to 2024, which cannot be paired with a seed cotton cost. Cotton therefore stands as a cost per acre on both sides and a cost per tonne on the Egypt side only.

## Section 2. Berseem and fodder

Berseem matters because it enters the model as the winter crop that fights wheat for land. The Egypt evidence is strong. The Punjab evidence is not, and that asymmetry is itself a finding.

Egypt cost, season 2021 to 2022: 9,792 pounds per feddan including rent for long season berseem, and 4,963 pounds excluding rent. Source: Ministry of Agriculture, Economic Affairs Sector, Bulletin of Cost Statistics and Net Return, Part 1, 2021 to 2022, Tables 1 and 10. The two parts sum exactly to the total, which is how the figure was checked inside a poorly scanned document. An older CAPMAS figure for 2019 to 2020 puts the cost at 7,484 pounds per feddan including rent.

Egypt scale and yield, season 2024 to 2025: berseem planted on 1,253,666 feddans, of which 1,002,301 feddans on old lands, with fresh fodder yield of 28.637 tonnes per feddan and production of 35.9 million tonnes. A second, short season type adds 138,250 feddans. Source: Ministry of Agriculture, Economic Affairs Sector, Agricultural Statistics Bulletin, Part 1, Winter Crops 2024 to 2025, Table 1. CAPMAS figures reported in February 2023 show the longer trend: 1.371 million feddans in 2021, down from 2.539 million feddans in 2004, with the Delta governorates holding 935.5 thousand feddans in 2021.

Egypt water and the wheat contest, from published studies. In the Meet Yazid command in Kafr El Sheikh, berseem seasonal water use was 404 mm against wheat at 305 mm, and berseem fodder water productivity was 10.46 kg per cubic metre in the improved canal area (Abou Kheira, American Society of Agricultural and Biological Engineers, 2009). A study in the Egyptian Journal of Agricultural Economics, June 2018, built on ministry and CAPMAS data for 2013 to 2015, gives a berseem water quota of 2,928 cubic metres per feddan against wheat at 2,113, and a net return per cubic metre of 4.02 pounds for berseem against 1.9 pounds for wheat. A 2023 study in Agriculture (MDPI) estimates that replacing berseem with wheat as the dominant winter crop could add up to 0.6 million hectares of wheat in the Delta, while moving the shortage from bread to protein. These three sources together say the wheat and berseem contest is real, measured, and priced in Egypt data.

Punjab. No official berseem cost of production per acre from the Crop Reporting Service or the Agricultural Policy Institute could be found, and Pakistani official statistics do not break berseem out at all. The Ministry of National Food Security and Research publishes fodder crops only as one combined category: Punjab fodder area 3,331 thousand hectares and yield 50.95 tonnes per hectare for 2023 to 2024, rising to 3,416 thousand hectares in 2024 to 2025 (Agricultural Statistics of Pakistan 2023 to 2024, Table 44, and the district wise release of 24 March 2026, Table 37). The only verified Pakistan berseem budget is academic and from another province: total cost 23,004 rupees per acre in District Peshawar, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, from a 2010 farm survey, with rent at 52.2 percent of cost (Khan and coauthors, Sarhad Journal of Agriculture, 2012). A Punjab trial at Okara gives berseem green fodder yield up to 111 tonnes per hectare with full fertiliser, and 76 tonnes per hectare unfertilised (Pakistan Journal of Science, March 2025). None of these can carry a Punjab berseem price in the model. The honest position: berseem enters the model on Egypt data, and the Punjab mirror stays silent on fodder.

## Section 3. Water

Water productivity figures move a lot with the denominator, so each figure below carries its basis. Consumptive use means water the crop actually consumed. Diverted or applied water means water delivered to the field or the system, which is always the larger amount and gives the smaller productivity.

The Delta, best single source. In the Meet Yazid command, 82,740 hectares of old lands, in 2008 to 2009: rice water productivity 1.04 kg per cubic metre of depleted water, and wheat 1.05 kg per cubic metre, at yields of 8 tonnes per hectare for rice and 6 tonnes per hectare for wheat. Calculated reciprocals: about 962 cubic metres per tonne of rice and about 952 cubic metres per tonne of wheat. Source: El Agha, Molden and Ghanem, Irrigation and Drainage Systems, 2011. A second study of the same command, published 2009, gives wheat at 1.54 kg per cubic metre in the improved canal area and 1.44 in the unimproved area, with rice at 1.01 and 0.93. A 2024 study in the International Journal of Plant Production, using WaPOR data, reports national average water productivity of 1.3 kg per cubic metre for wheat and 1.1 for rice for 2016 to 2020, and warns that its own WaPOR based Delta rice estimate is a large underestimate and not usable.

Punjab. In the Lower Jhelum Canal system, from 218 farms in the 2000 to 2001 rabi season: wheat water productivity 1.37 kg per cubic metre of consumed water, and 1.11 kg per cubic metre of diverted water, at an average yield of 4.11 tonnes per hectare (IWMI Research Report 65, 2003). A basin assessment by Cai and Sharma (2010), reported in a 2025 Springer chapter, gives wheat at about 0.76 kg per cubic metre of consumptive use for the Pakistan Punjab, and rice at 0.69 kg per cubic metre for the Pakistan part of the Indus Basin, falling to about 0.16 kg per cubic metre of applied water. Punjab farm trials from 2001 to 2003 give rice at 0.27 to 0.32 kg per cubic metre of evapotranspiration (Mann, Jehangir and Masih, NARC and IWMI Lahore). The Punjab numbers disagree with each other by method and scale. Any table that prints them must print the basis and the site beside each one.

Read: on the closest like for like basis, consumptive use, Delta wheat at 1.05 and Delta rice at 1.04 kg per cubic metre stand against Punjab wheat somewhere between 0.76 and 1.37 and Indus rice at 0.69. The Delta grows more grain per unit of water consumed, at higher yields per hectare, while Punjab spreads water over far more land with a large groundwater share. That is the structural difference the mirror can honestly show.

Canal system facts. Punjab has 25 main canals, with main canals and branches of 3,993 miles, distributaries and minors of 19,191 miles, inter river link canals of 528 miles, and about 58,000 outlets commanding 23.35 million acres, as listed in a 2023 Punjab government plan document from the Urban Unit reproducing Irrigation Department figures. FAO AQUASTAT (2011, data year 2008) puts the whole Indus Basin system at 60,800 km of canals and about 1.6 million km of watercourses and field channels across 45 canal commands. Overall irrigation efficiency for the Indus system is put at 40 percent by FAO AQUASTAT, at about 55 percent for Punjab by the Punjab On Farm Water Management wing in a 2023 analysis reported at the 4th World Irrigation Forum in September 2025, and at 30 percent in a 2019 forum paper citing a 2007 source. The range itself is the fact: no single efficiency figure should be quoted without its source and year. Groundwater carries a large share in Punjab: more than 45 percent of irrigation requirements, from about 1.2 million tubewells (3rd World Irrigation Forum paper, 2019), and about 41 billion cubic metres of groundwater inside a total Punjab supply of about 112 billion cubic metres in the 2023 water budget. The Delta has no comparable published system efficiency percentage in the sources opened, and El Agha and coauthors report instead that 53 percent of the Meet Yazid main canal supply returned to drains and groundwater in 2008 to 2009.

Allocation. Punjab canal water is shared by warabandi: a fixed rotation, generally a seven day cycle, in which each farmer receives water for a time proportional to his land inside the watercourse command (IIMI Research Report 17, 1998; FAO AQUASTAT, 2011). The Delta runs its own rotation turns on branch canals. The mirror point for the model: both systems ration a scarce flow by time shares fixed in advance, which is the world the model's district water budgets are trying to describe.

## Section 4. Policy mirror

Egypt holds its wheat price up by decree, and pays for it in the budget. Pakistan has just walked away from the same instrument. The two countries now stand on opposite sides of the question the model prices.

Egypt wheat procurement: 2,200 pounds per ardeb of 150 kg at the top cleanliness grade for the 2025 harvest, set by Cabinet decision of 2 October 2024 and applied by Ministerial Decree 46 of 2025. For the 2026 harvest the Cabinet first set 2,350 pounds per ardeb at the top grade in August 2025, then raised the price in season to 2,500 pounds per ardeb in April 2026 (Reuters, 21 April 2026). For the 2027 planting, 2,650 pounds per ardeb was announced on 24 October 2026 and confirmed in the FAO GIEWS country brief with reference date 13 November 2026. Egypt food subsidy: 160 billion pounds allocated to food commodities and bread in the fiscal 2025 to 2026 budget, up 20 percent on the year before, inside 732.6 billion pounds of total subsidies, grants and social benefits (Cabinet approval reported by the State Information Service and Reuters, 26 March 2025). A bread only split of that figure could not be verified in a Ministry of Finance document.

Egypt sugar beet procurement: 2,400 pounds per tonne for the 2025 season, cut to 2,000 pounds per tonne for 2025 to 2026 by Cabinet decision published in August 2025, and corroborated by the USDA Sugar Annual of 1 May 2026. A rise to 2,300 pounds per tonne was announced on 24 October 2026, but the two reports opened disagree on whether 2,300 is the general base price or only the price for newly reclaimed land, so the figure is carried here as announced and disputed, not settled.

Pakistan wheat: the last fixed support price was 3,900 rupees per 40 kg for 2023 to 2024, set by the Punjab Cabinet in April 2024. For the 2024 to 2025 crop no support price was set at all, by the federal government or by Punjab, under the IMF programme, and market prices fell to about 2,100 to 2,400 rupees per 40 kg. Punjab replaced procurement support with a direct payment of 5,000 rupees per acre to wheat farmers for the 2025 harvest, inside a relief package of 25 billion rupees (Punjab government portal, 21 April 2025; Dawn, 22 April 2025). For 2025 to 2026 the federal government approved procurement of about 6.2 million tonnes of strategic reserves at 3,500 rupees per 40 kg, described as an illustrative price set at the international import price rather than a support price (Dawn, 19 October 2025). Reporting in The Express Tribune of 26 December 2025 states that from the following season the federal government withdraws from wheat procurement entirely, holds only strategic reserves bought through private companies, and expects to save about 570 billion rupees a year. No operating consumer flour subsidy replacing procurement could be verified; the only mention found was a proposal in an opinion piece.

Read: Egypt's procurement price is a live policy lever that the model can price, season by season, against cover. Pakistan shows the counterfactual: what happens to farm prices, and to the state bill, when the lever is removed. That is the strongest policy use of the mirror.

## Section 5. Verdict

What the comparison can carry. First, a method check: both countries publish crop budgets in the same shape, cost per land unit with rent inside, and the wheat pair proves the method travels. Second, a cost bridge for the model: the Egypt wheat series now runs from the 2022 ministry bulletin to the 2024 to 2025 Agricultural Research Center budget, so the wheat cost in the model can be updated on sourced figures instead of guessed multipliers. Third, a scale proof for berseem: Egypt area, yield and cost are all official, so berseem can enter the model as a real winter crop on Egypt data alone. Fourth, a policy mirror with a live edge: Egypt raises its wheat procurement price each season while Pakistan has abolished its support price, and the model can price the Egypt lever against the Pakistan outcome. Fifth, a water structure contrast: high yield, high water productivity, fully surface supplied Delta against lower yield, groundwater heavy Punjab.

What it cannot carry. It cannot fill the Delta water data gap: no Punjab figure tells us canal, reuse and groundwater supply by Delta district, and the water input stays a user input by design. It cannot price Punjab berseem, Punjab maize, or Punjab cotton per tonne, because the sources do not exist in open form or do not pair cost with yield. It cannot be read as a league table: Punjab is one province, seasons differ between pairs, and the two budget systems count slightly different things, as the workbook already warns for rice transport. And it cannot settle the Egypt sugar beet price for the coming season until the 2,300 pound announcement is clarified.

Numbers strong enough to quote in the memo, each with its source attached: Egypt wheat at 239.41 dollars per tonne for 2024 to 2025 against Punjab wheat at 280.67 dollars per tonne for 2023 to 2024. Egypt rice at 247.18 dollars per tonne for summer 2022 against Punjab rice at 191.98 dollars per tonne for 2022 to 2023. Egypt berseem at 1,253,666 feddans and 28.637 tonnes per feddan for 2024 to 2025, with cost at 9,792 pounds per feddan including rent for 2021 to 2022. Delta wheat and rice water productivity at 1.05 and 1.04 kg per cubic metre of depleted water for 2008 to 2009, against Punjab wheat at 1.37 kg per cubic metre of consumed water for 2000 to 2001, both printed with their basis. Egypt wheat procurement at 2,500 pounds per ardeb for the 2026 harvest and 2,650 announced for 2027, against Pakistan with no support price for the 2024 to 2025 crop and a 5,000 rupees per acre farmer payment in its place.
