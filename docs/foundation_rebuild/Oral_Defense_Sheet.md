# Oral Defense Sheet: the Hardest Questions on the Nile Delta Model

Mohamed Diallo, November 2026

Questions 1 to 3 are the three hardest questions of the independent domain critique, posed against version 4.1 and answered here on the version 5 record. Questions 4 to 8 come from the caveats of the independent verification and the Gate A final statement. Each answer states what the record supports and then the honest limit of that answer. Nothing here claims more than the files hold.

## Question 1. The water anchor

**The question.** Your ceiling and your price for the guarantee both scale from an 11.00 billion cubic metre budget that you calibrated and no source publishes. What are the ceiling and the ladder at 9.90 and at 12.10, and at what anchor value does your headline change its meaning?

**The answer the record supports.** The ladder and the ceiling have now been solved at scaled anchors, in the version 5.1 addendum Run 1. At 9.90 billion cubic metres the ceiling falls to 75.86 percent of the reference tonnage, 9,295,584 tonnes, the 80 percent rung is infeasible, and the dearest surviving rung, 75 percent, costs 263,919,142,021 pounds, within 5 billion pounds of what the 80 percent guarantee costs at the full anchor, 268,610,886,052 pounds on the version 5 basis. At 8.80 the ceiling falls to 69.30 percent, 8,491,820 tonnes, nothing above 65 percent survives, and the 65 percent guarantee costs 226,520,935,005 pounds, more than the 70 percent guarantee costs at the full anchor. At every surviving rung the margin falls and the cost rises as the anchor falls, with no exception in the table. The headline changes its meaning at the first step down: the sentence that an 80 percent guarantee can be held is true only at the full anchor, and false at nine tenths of it. The evidence that would settle the anchor is a published release series for the Delta. None exists in the sources this project holds, which is why the anchor is a calibration and is labelled as one wherever the ladder travels.

**The honest limit.** The full ladder was not run at 12.10. The verifier spot checks scaled all budgets by 1.05 and 0.95 for the ceiling only: the ceiling rises to 10,501,232 tonnes at the higher scale and falls to 9,697,467 tonnes at the lower one. The split of the anchor across governorates by physical land share is a construction, stated as such, and no alternative split of the anchor itself has been run. Run 1 measures the price of being wrong about the anchor. It does not make the anchor right.

## Question 2. The baseline contradiction

**The question.** The paper gave the historical pattern a worst scenario of 60.12 percent of the reference tonnage and, two sentences later, no guarantee at any level, on a ladder that begins at 50 percent. Which sentence should I believe, and what exactly differs between the two evaluations that produced them?

**The answer the record supports.** The no guarantee sentence was false, and it is withdrawn. The two figures came from two different evaluations that the old text ran together. Delivered tonnage under profit seeking recourse answers what the fixed pattern actually delivers. Capacity under staple maximising recourse answers what the same fixed planting could deliver if water and abandonment were worked for tonnage alone, and the ladder test belongs to the second evaluation. The first independent check proved the sentence false on the version 4.1 basis itself. On the version 5 basis the test is printed in full: the floor tonnages are 6,127,201 tonnes at 50 percent, 7,352,641 at 60, 7,965,361 at 65 and 8,578,081 at 70, and the pattern worst scenario capacity of 8,056,850 tonnes clears the first three and fails the fourth. The pattern holds the 50, 60 and 65 percent levels in every scenario and fails from 70 percent. The delivered reading stands separately beside it: a worst scenario delivery of 7,684,799 tonnes, 62.71 percent, and an expected delivery of 9,270,566 tonnes, 75.65 percent.

**The honest limit.** Both readings are evaluations inside this model on its stated basis, under recourse that sees the full scenario. Neither is a measurement of a real season, and the corrected sentence must travel with the words inside this model attached.

## Question 3. The three choices upstream of the price

**The question.** Three choices sit upstream of your price for the guarantee. The floor can be met by maize alone while wheat collapses. Wheat is paid at a farm gate forecast far from the procurement price your own record documents. And your layers force every crop price and yield to move together, in every district at once. Which of the three, relaxed, moves the price of the guarantee most, and why did the paper test the beet price concept, which changes nothing, and none of these?

**The answer the record supports.** The wheat test the critique demanded has now been run. On the version 4.1 basis the farm gate forecast sat about 42 percent below the documented procurement price of 14,666.67 pounds per tonne. After the price vintage repair the direction reversed: the 2025 farm gate forecast of 17,891.58 sits above the procurement price, so procurement pricing makes the floor dearer, and the cost of the 80 percent guarantee rises to 288,889,662,759 pounds against 268,610,886,052 pounds in the primary run, a move of about 20.3 billion pounds. The unconstrained margin does not move, because the free plan plants no wheat. On the aggregate floor: it is true by construction. The guarantee protects the sum of three crops and nothing finer, and the memo says so wherever the floor is defined. On the layers: it is also true. Price and yield share a single layer index in the code, so the low price state always coincides with the low yield state inside a scenario, and the verifier confirmed the code matches that stated design. The beet variant was tested first because a documented factory price existed for beet. It changes nothing because the model plants no sugar beet at either price; the verifier confirmed beet at exactly zero hectares at both prices at the 0 and 80 percent floors. It was the weakest of the available tests, and the critique was right to say so.

**The honest limit.** Of the three choices, only the wheat price concept has been relaxed in a run. No per crop floor variant and no decorrelated layer run exists in the record, so I cannot rank the three effects, and any ranking I offered would be invented. What I can say is bounded: the one tested choice moves the price of the top guarantee by about 20 billion pounds, and the other two stand as named, untested exposures.

## Question 4. Is the 80 percent plan truly feasible?

**The question.** Your verification says the saved 80 percent plan holds its floor within solver tolerance, not exactly. Is the guarantee real?

**The answer the record supports.** It is real, and it is exactly as thin as the verification states. Imposed as a hard constraint on the saved plan, the floor returns infeasible in the three low layer scenarios where the plan is tight. Clearance maximisation, which asks how much staple tonnage the fixed plan can hold in each scenario, returns 9,803,521.000 tonnes against a floor of 9,803,520.985 tonnes in those scenarios, so the plan can hold its floor with 0.015 tonnes to spare. Easing the floor by a single tonne makes all nine scenario solves return optimal. At full precision the largest excess of any planting pool over its land limit is 0.0032 hectares on pools of tens of thousands of hectares.

**The honest limit.** The guarantee lives in the plan together with its floor holding recourse. Replaying the saved planting with margin seeking recourse and no floor imposed delivers a minimum scenario tonnage of 8,926,542 tonnes, below the floor. Anyone handed the plan files must impose the floor holding recourse to reproduce the guarantee, and any quotation of the guarantee must carry the tolerance and that condition with it.

## Question 5. Are the expected margins real expectations?

**The question.** Your expected margins rest on layer weights of 0.25, 0.50 and 0.25. Are those probabilities?

**The answer the record supports.** They are nominal, and the record prints the measurement that proves it. The bands are built exactly as stated, at the quartiles of the backtest residuals, and the weights describe that construction. But measured sequential coverage of the price bands runs from 0.0 percent for wheat to 22.2 percent for potatoes against a construction value of 50 percent, because the pooled residual history is dominated by the low price era. The layers therefore understate the spread of the current price regime. Inside the same construction, both residual quartiles for potato prices are positive, so the low price layer for potatoes, 10,876.83 pounds per tonne, sits above the expected layer of 10,753.27. The arithmetic is right and the word low is wrong for that one series. The hard floor, ceiling and feasibility results do not depend on the layer weights and are untouched by this caveat.

**The honest limit.** Every expected money figure inherits the thinness of the layers. The band calibration decision is still open: either the layers are rebuilt on a stated recent window with the new coverage printed, or the coverage shortfall stands as a printed limitation beside every expected figure, as it stands in the memo now. Until that decision is taken and executed, no expected margin from this model may be quoted as a measured expectation about a coming season.

## Question 6. Are the water values prices?

**The question.** Your table gives a value of water for each governorate and scenario. May a planner read those as the marginal value of water, or as prices?

**The answer the record supports.** No, and the table is labelled so that it cannot honestly be read that way. The values are one optimal dual solution of the linear relaxation of a model whose keep and abandon decisions are discrete, computed at the exact floor of 0.8231405524 as a share of the reference tonnage. At that floor the relaxation objective is 273,356,788,636 pounds against the mixed integer objective of 272,969,293,877 pounds, a gap of 0.142 percent. Dual solutions are not unique; another optimal solution can carry different governorate values. The ranges, dry state dearer than high state, Damietta low in the dry and normal states at the expected layer, are the robust content.

**The honest limit.** Finite increments on the full mixed integer model were never computed, so these values are not marginal values of the model that produced the plans, and they are not prices or tariffs in any wording the record allows.

## Question 7. Why should anyone trust the numbers?

**The question.** This work was built and audited with heavy AI involvement. What independent reason is there to trust any figure in it?

**The answer the record supports.** The independent verification of version 5 was run by a checker who did not build it and who formed verdicts from the primary artifacts before opening the build side documents. The checker reran the whole build in a scratch copy: the forecasting module regenerated its files byte for byte, and the allocation runs regenerated results files exactly equal to the saved ones, with identical plan files and an identical robustness sample file. Every headline figure was recomputed with separate arithmetic. The floor 80 problem and the ceiling problem were replayed under HiGHS, a different solver from the CBC the build used, with agreement to 3,206 pounds on 291.1 billion for the floor and to a third of a tonne for the ceiling. The addendum runs add three more agreements of the same kind, including a 65 percent rung the build itself never solved, returned equal to the verifier own fresh solve.

**The honest limit.** Numerical verification checks that the arithmetic is the arithmetic of the stated model. It cannot check that the stated model is the right model of the Delta. No Egyptian irrigation or agricultural specialist has reviewed any version of this work, and no cross vendor critique substitutes for that review. The empirical half of the acceptance gate stays closed until that review happens.

## Question 8. The potato problem

**The question.** Your relaxed frontier prices the guarantee against a free plan that plants potatoes at hundreds of times their observed area. Why should the relaxed numbers be quoted at all?

**The answer the record supports.** They should be quoted only as a benchmark, and the memo leads with the capped list for exactly this reason. In the unconstrained relaxed optimum, winter potatoes stand at 16.91 times their observed area, summer potatoes at 22.71 times and Nili potatoes at 369.63 times, at fixed prices, and no staples are planted at all. At those multiples the fixed prices stop being credible. Under the named caps the same promise costs far less at every shared rung: the 75 percent guarantee costs 32,666,817,585 pounds capped against 242,759,240,394 pounds relaxed, and the capped ladder tops out at 75 percent because the capped ceiling is 79.50 percent. The relaxed ladder shows what the promise costs when planting may leave the observed pattern without limit; the capped list is the price list a decision reader should be handed.

**The honest limit.** The caps are a modelling device carried from the earlier audited version, not an estimate from market data. No demand curve or absorption study for Delta potato output exists in the project record, so neither list is a market forecast. The distance between the two lists is itself the honest measure of how much the headline depends on the market assumption.
