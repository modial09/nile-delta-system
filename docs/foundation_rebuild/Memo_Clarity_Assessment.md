# Assessment of the Research Memo, Version 5: Clarity, Connectivity, Logic, Assumptions, Real Value

Independent assessor, reading the memo cold
November 2026

Basis of this assessment: paper/Research_Memo_v5.md read in full, with paper/Executive_Summary_v5.md, and spot checks of the memo figures against paper/Results_Note_v5.md. The headline figures I checked trace to the results note exactly, including the reference tonnage of 12,254,401 tonnes, the relaxed cost of the 80 percent guarantee at 268,610,886,052 pounds, the capped cost of the 75 percent guarantee at 32,666,817,585 pounds, and the penalised stochastic value of 75,931,039,486 pounds. This assessment judges the memo as a piece of thinking. It does not re verify the arithmetic.

Overall verdict in one paragraph: the memo is honest to a standard most working papers never reach, and its argument can be followed by a smart outside reader at the level of the story. It loses that reader at the level of vocabulary, and it loses him again at two places where the text promises evidence it does not print. The framework is sound in design and contains one bias whose direction the memo never states. The central idea is genuinely valuable. The weakest load bearing part is the headline list itself, because the capped ladder rests on the two least examined inputs in the model and is never tested against the weakest one.

## 1. Clarity

Verdict: clear in structure and in story, unclear in vocabulary. The central result survives one reading. The supporting machinery does not.

What works. The first page does the hardest job in the document and does it well. The governing label arrives before any number: "None of it is a measured fact about Egypt, and no sentence in this memo should be read as one." Section 1 then gives the question, the method, the basis and the central result in that order, and a reader who stopped there would still carry away the right finding: under planting caps a 75 percent guarantee costs about 32.7 billion pounds, 80 percent cannot be held, the relaxed world prices the same promise near ten times higher, and the pattern Egypt plants already holds 65 percent. The prose is plain, the sentences are short, and the signposting is direct, as in "Read the capped price list first, because it is the list a decision reader can act on." Section 4 even teaches the reader how to read it: "The reading is three sentences."

Where the text loses the reader, in order of damage.

1. The load bearing terms are never defined. Nili appears in the second paragraph of the method and is used throughout, and nowhere does the memo say that Nili is a third planting season. The words layer, recourse, dual, relaxation and wait and see value each carry a result when they arrive, and none is explained at first use. A reader outside the field can guess layer from context by Section 3. He cannot guess recourse, and Section 4 turns on it: the corrected baseline finding is presented as two readings, one under "staple maximising recourse" and one under "profit seeking recourse", and the distinction between capacity and delivery, which the memo itself calls the point of the correction, depends on a word the reader was never given. The paper carries a glossary in its own appendix. The memo is written to stand alone and does not.

2. Two numbers arrive with no story and no consequence. In Section 3 the winter potato cost sensitivity "raises the unconstrained margin to 592,918,438,384 pounds" and the text moves on. The reader is not told whether that movement matters, and it never returns in the argument. In Section 4 the accounting difference of 415,545,553,464 pounds between the optimised and the pattern margin is printed and then disowned in the same breath: "no input is said to have caused any share of that difference." A number the author refuses to interpret should not occupy the reader attention at full size. Either give it its one honest sentence of meaning, that it measures the total distance between the two worlds and nothing finer, or move it to the record.

3. The water value table prints a row it does not explain. The dry state at the low layer shows values of 826.44 to 961.74 pounds per cubic metre, near ten times the dry state at the expected layer, and the memo offers no sentence on why. The surrounding text explains, correctly and clearly, that the values are one dual solution of a relaxation and are not prices. A reader still leaves the table with the largest numbers in it unexplained.

4. Section 4 is long, and the headline dulls as it proceeds. Five results share the section: the capped list, the relaxed ladder at three anchors, the corrected baseline, the water values, the stress and robustness pairs, and the value of information. Each is well told on its own. Together they ask the reader to hold too many figures of equal visual weight, and by the robustness table the reader must work to remember which list he was told to act on. The memo knows its own headline. The section layout does not protect it.

## 2. Connectivity

Verdict: the spine connects. Basis stated in Section 1 is honoured in every later section, and the limitations in Section 5 answer the methods of Section 3 point for point. Two promises the text makes are not kept, and one body of support is never used by any conclusion.

The strong connections first. The forecast passage in Section 3 builds the layers, and Section 5 returns to those same layers with their measured coverage, so the reader meets the weakness where the construction happened and again where it is priced. The anchor introduced in Section 1 is stress tested in Section 4 and indicted again in Section 5. The corrected baseline finding in Section 4 is prepared by the timing passage in Section 3, which explains why the floor is written scenario by scenario. This is a connected document.

The unkept promises.

1. Section 2 promises the planner "the cost of each level against the free optimum and against the level below." The tables in Section 4 print only the cost against the optimum. The step cost from one rung to the next, which is the number a budget holder actually negotiates, must be computed by the reader from the margins. The earlier paper printed both. The memo should keep its own promise or stop making it.

2. Section 3 states of the reserve construction that "Section 4 reports the measured alternative split beside the primary one." Section 4 contains no reserve split result. The alternative split is mentioned again in Section 5 only as an item in the list of what version 5 did. A reader who follows the signpost arrives at an empty room. This is the clearest connectivity fault in the memo, because the text itself creates the expectation.

Support that no conclusion uses. The two field lessons in Section 2, that farmers plant for household security rather than profit alone and that half of canal supply returns to drains and groundwater, discipline the tone of the whole memo and are honestly carried. But no result is ever read through them. No conclusion in Section 4 changes because farmers plant thirsty crops at canal tail ends, and the memo never says what the lesson does to the price list, for example whether a guarantee delivered through planting guidance would bind less than the model assumes. The lessons function as a warning label. That is worth something, but the memo presents them as part of the argument, and as argument they are idle.

A conclusion that arrives before its support, in a mild form. Section 1 calls the capped list the list a decision reader can act on, four sections before Section 4 argues why caps restore price credibility. In a memo whose first section is labelled as the result, front loading of that kind is normal practice and I do not count it as a fault. The one sentence in Section 1 that does outrun its support is different in kind: "The hard floor, ceiling and feasibility results do not depend on the layer weights." That is true, and important, and the reason, that the floor binds scenario by scenario and never in expectation, is only assembled across Sections 3 and 4. A reader who doubts it on page one has to wait for the proof. One clause on page one would close the gap.

## 3. Logic of the framework

Verdict: the chain from question to method to result is sound, and two of its four main design choices are genuinely well judged. One link is asserted rather than argued, and one known bias is admitted without its direction, which is the one place the memo honesty falls short of its own standard.

The two planting decision points. This is the right skeleton for the question. A guarantee about a season must be evaluated under the information the season actually provides, and the split, winter planting fixed before the inflow is known, summer and Nili planting after it is observed, matches the calendar a Delta planner lives in. The design also produces the memo most underused insight: because planting cannot condition on the price and yield layer, the floor has to hold in the worst layer as well, and that is what makes the promise expensive. The logic is sound and the memo explains it in one clean paragraph.

The hard floor in every scenario. This is the correct formalisation of the word promise, and the memo argues it explicitly: a floor held only in expectation is a floor that fails in the years it was bought for. The cost of the choice is also visible, because the robustness passage then reports that the fixed 80 percent plan holds its floor in less than half of sampled single years. Read together, the two passages say something true and uncomfortable: even a guarantee engineered to hold in every modelled scenario is a promise about a small set of constructed years, not about the run of real ones. The framework survives its own stress test because the memo prints both halves.

The capped ladder against the relaxed benchmark. As a bounding exercise this is the best structural idea in the memo. Pricing one promise in two worlds, planting free and planting bounded, shows the reader that the price of a guarantee is mostly a property of the world it is priced against, and that lesson is worth more than any single figure. But the memo claims more for the caps than bounding. It calls the capped list implementable and the list to hand a decision reader. The caps are shares of district land carried from an earlier version, wheat 0.50, rice 0.35, maize 0.40, beet 0.30, potatoes 0.15, and no evidence in the memo supports those levels as the boundary of what markets or rotations allow. Section 5 concedes the point in its own words: market evidence "would turn the caps from a modelling device into an estimate." Until then the capped list is a second model result, better behaved than the first, and the word implementable is a claim the memo has not earned. The honest label is the one the memo uses for the relaxed ladder in reverse: a price under stated planting bounds, credible exactly as far as the bounds are.

The scaled anchor sensitivity. Sound, and the single most professorial move in the document. An input that no source publishes is not hidden or defended; its error is priced, rung by rung, and the finding that a tenth less water deletes the top rung is the kind of result that changes how a reader holds every other number. Two limits on the link. The sensitivity moves the total only: the split across governorates stays by land share at every anchor, so the test covers the level of the anchor and never its distribution. And the sensitivity is run on the relaxed ladder only. The capped ladder, the headline, is never repriced at 9.90 or at 8.80. The number the memo tells the reader to act on is therefore the one number in the document with no test against the model weakest input. That asymmetry is the largest logical gap in the memo.

The bias whose direction is not stated. The memo names the information advantage inside the model fairly: "once a scenario is realised, water allocation and abandonment respond with the full scenario in view, including its yield layer. No real planting season sees that much, and the size of that advantage is unmeasured." What it does not say is which way the advantage pushes the headline. Recourse that sees the yield layer before it allocates water can only make the floor easier to hold and the guarantee cheaper than a blind recourse would. The printed prices are therefore, on this one count, lower bounds on the cost of the promise. Every other weakness in the memo is admitted with its direction attached. This one deserves the same treatment, in one sentence.

## 4. Assumptions

Verdict: most of the load bearing assumptions were the best choice the available data allowed, and the memo admits nearly all of them at the right strength. Two were conveniences that the memo discloses but does not repair, and one assumption is not admitted at all because the memo never explains it.

1. The calibrated water anchor of 11.00 billion cubic metres. Necessary, not convenient. No published release series for the Delta exists in the sources the project holds, so some calibration was unavoidable, and using the WaPOR consumption total as a scale check rather than as the calibration itself was the disciplined choice. The split across governorates by physical land share is the convenient part: it assumes water follows land, which no canal system does. The better choice costs a dataset the project does not have, district level delivery or release records. Admission strength: full, and strengthened by the priced sensitivity. This is the model for how the other assumptions should be handled.

2. Forecast 2025 prices against official 2022 costs. The best available pairing, and the most consequential one. No official cost table after 2022 exists in the project sources, so the pairing was forced. But in an economy that lived through sharp inflation across exactly those years, the mixed basis moves every margin, and the memo knows it: "no margin in this memo is the figure for a single real year." The repair offered is a sensitivity at cost multipliers of 1.50 and 2.00. Two round multipliers are a bracket, not a bridge. The better choice, restating one side of the basis to the other year with a published price index, costs little work and would let the primary run stand on one year instead of two. The memo chose disclosure over restatement and says so. Admission strength: full. The choice itself is defensible for a study at this stage, and it caps what any margin figure can mean.

3. Nominal layer probabilities. The convenience the memo is most honest about and least able to defend. The quartile construction makes the weights and the bands agree in form, and then the measured coverage, 0.0 percent for wheat to 22.2 percent for potatoes against a construction value of 50 percent, shows the agreement is formal only. Every expected margin in the memo is a weighted mean over layers whose weights the author himself reports as wrong in a measured way. The better choice is named in the memo: rebuild the layers on a recent window and print the new coverage, or recalibrate. It costs one build cycle, not new data. The memo chose to print the shortfall as a standing limitation and to leave the decision open, which is honest and, for the document central product, expensive: feasibility and ceiling results are untouched by the weights, but the price list is made of expected values. Admission strength: full. Judgement: disclosure was the right call for this version, and the memo says plainly that the empirical label stays closed until the decision is taken. A reader should believe the rungs and the ceilings at full strength and the pound figures at reduced strength, and the memo, to its credit, tells him exactly that.

4. Fixed prices inside the caps. A partial repair presented as a fuller one. The caps bound how much land a crop may take, and inside those bounds the price still does not move, so a capped plan that doubles potato area still sells every tonne at the forecast price. The better choice, a price that falls as area grows, costs market absorption evidence that does not exist in the project sources. Admission strength: right in Section 5, weaker in Section 4, where the capped list is introduced with the confidence of a settled instrument. The gap between the two sections is the issue, not the assumption itself.

5. Published district yields as the yield basis. The one assumption the memo under explains rather than mis admits. Section 3 gives two yield stories side by side: published district yields of 2020 to 2021, which build the reference tonnage, and a forecasting module that produces national level yield forecasts per crop. The memo never states in one sentence how the two meet inside a scenario: whether the district yields stand and the layers scale them, or the forecast levels replace them. A reader cannot reconstruct the yield a single governorate plants with in the expected layer. This is a clarity fault with an assumption inside it, because whichever meeting rule was used is a modelling choice with consequences for the ceiling. The better choice costs one paragraph. Admission strength: absent, because the choice is invisible.

6. The reserve construction that writes off 30,136 hectares. A defensible construction with a measured alternative, and the memo says the alternative was run. The fault is not the assumption but the missing report, covered under Connectivity: the reader is told the price of the choice exists and is never shown it. Admission strength: the write off itself is admitted at full strength, in the open, with its size. Judgement: the best available construction given that unmodelled crops must live somewhere, and the winter first alternative split is the right sensitivity to have run.

7. Recourse that sees the full scenario. The convenient choice, made for tractability, and the only assumption whose admission understates its effect. Real allocation decisions are taken with partial knowledge; the model recourse takes them with complete knowledge of the realised layer. The better choice, recourse rules that respond to the inflow state only, costs a harder build and would raise the price of every rung by an amount nobody currently knows. Admission strength: the fact is admitted in full and in the right place, in the timing passage. The direction is not admitted anywhere. Judgement: acceptable for an exploratory study, provided the lower bound sentence is added.

8. Smaller carried assumptions, judged briefly. The minimum water share of 0.30 for a kept crop is stated in Section 1 as an assumption and is never given a sensitivity anywhere in the memo, although it shapes what abandonment means; one line reporting whether the ceiling moves across a tested range would close it. The sugar beet water duty, arbitrated to a midpoint of 6,100 cubic metres per hectare from field trial evidence, is stated with its range and is the right way to carry a contested figure. The rice yield response factor of 1.20 is carried as unverified with a tested range of 1.00 to 1.40 across which the ceiling does not move; that is admission at exactly the right strength.

## 5. Real value

Verdict: strip the polish and three findings remain that a reader did not have before, one idea that is worth the whole project, and one admission about the author that an admissions reader will weigh as heavily as any number.

What a decision maker learns. First, that the price of a staple guarantee is not a property of the floor. It is a property of the plan the promise is priced against. The same 75 percent promise costs 32,666,817,585 pounds when planting stays near the observed pattern and 242,759,240,394 pounds when it is priced against a free optimum, and no honest quotation of the price exists without its world attached. Second, that the current pattern is a stronger base than the public argument assumes: it already holds 65 percent in every modelled scenario, so the live policy question is the price of the step from 65 to 75, not the rescue of a failing pattern. Third, that water scarcity does not raise the price of the ladder smoothly; it deletes rungs. A tenth less water removes the top of the ladder whole, which means a guarantee policy written at the full anchor is a policy that silently expires in a dry decade.

The single strongest idea is the two world price list itself. Anyone can solve for the cost of a constraint. Pricing the same promise against the world as it is planted and against the world as an optimiser would plant it, and refusing to quote either figure without the other, turns a model output into an instrument a budget holder can use and a critic cannot easily misuse. If one sentence survives from this work, it should be that a guarantee has no price, only prices, each with its conditions printed beside it.

What an admissions reader learns is about the author. This memo withdraws two of its own earlier headlines in print, prices the error in its own weakest input, reports the forecast contest its preferred methods lost, and ends by naming the two events that must happen before its label may improve. A reader deciding whether this applicant can be trusted with harder problems learns more from the corrected baseline passage than from the ceiling figure. The strongest evidence in the document for the author readiness is that the document keeps trying to talk the reader out of overbelieving it, and keeps failing to find a reason to stop.

The weakest part that still carries weight. It is the headline itself. The capped ladder is the result the memo tells the reader to act on, and it rests at once on caps that are a device rather than an estimate, on expected values whose layer weights are admitted to be miscalibrated, and on a water anchor whose sensitivity was run only for the other ladder. Each weakness is disclosed somewhere in the memo. They are never disclosed together, at the headline, where their combined weight belongs. Beside that, a presentational fault compounds the problem: figures are quoted to the exact pound throughout, 32,666,817,585 pounds, a precision the inputs cannot support by orders of magnitude. A professor reading the memo will trust its logic more if its numbers stop pretending to a certainty its own Section 5 refutes. Report the run record to the pound, by all means. Report the memo figures in billions, with one decimal, and let the rounding itself teach the reader how much weight a figure can bear.

## Closing: the three changes that would most improve the memo

1. Run the capped ladder at the two lower water anchors, or state at the headline why the implementable list carries no anchor sensitivity. The memo most actionable numbers should be tested against its weakest input, as the relaxed numbers already are.

2. Add the one sentence on recourse, that with the full scenario in view the printed guarantee costs are lower bounds, and round every money figure in the memo to billions with one decimal, keeping exact pounds in the results note only. Both changes align the presentation with the honesty the text already practises.

3. Repair the two unkept promises and the missing definitions in one pass: print the rung to rung costs the tables omit, print the reserve split results Section 3 points to, and define the load bearing terms at first use, starting with Nili, recourse, layer, dual and relaxation. None of this needs a new run of substance. It needs the memo to finish the arguments it starts.
