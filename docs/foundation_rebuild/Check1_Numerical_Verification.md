# Independent Check 1: Numerical Verification

Prior audit files were opened only after these findings were complete; the comparison with them is the last section. Every number below is either recomputed by my own scripts or read from the run logs and named as such. My scripts sit in docs/foundation_rebuild/check1_work. No existing file was changed.

The two failures are not arithmetic slips. In both cases every printed component reproduces exactly, and the failure sits in what the printed sentence claims those components mean.

## Check 1. The baseline contradiction. Verdict: fail

The claim under test, from the paper: the historical pattern delivers 7,367,358 tonnes in its worst scenario, 60.12 percent of the reference tonnage of 12,254,407 tonnes, and inside this model it holds no guarantee at any level on the ladder. The ladder runs at 50, 60, 70, 75 and 80 percent.

The numbers reproduce. My own evaluation of the fixed historical pattern, using the build's own evaluate function, returns the printed figures to the pound and to the tonne: expected margin 86,006,146,997 pounds, expected staple tonnage 8,908,274 tonnes at 72.69 percent, minimum scenario staple tonnage 7,367,358 tonnes at 60.12 percent, in the dry inflow state at the low price and yield layer. That minimum is what a profit seeking recourse delivers, because the evaluation lets each scenario allocate water and abandonment for margin.

The claim does not survive the model's own floor test. I imposed the floor on the fixed pattern exactly as the model imposes it on its own plans, scenario by scenario. The pattern holds the 50 percent floor in all twelve scenarios. It also holds the 60 percent floor in all twelve scenarios. It fails at 65 percent and at 70 percent. Under the capacity reading the build itself uses for its floored plans, staple maximising recourse with the planting fixed, the pattern's worst scenario is again the dry state at the low layer, at 7,851,567 tonnes, 64.07 percent. Under the profit delivery reading, its worst scenario delivery of 7,367,358 tonnes clears the 60 percent floor tonnage of 7,352,644 tonnes by 14,714 tonnes. On every reading the pattern clears the two lowest rungs of the ladder.

Cause: the sentence treats a pattern that was never built under a guarantee as a pattern that can meet no guarantee level. Those are different statements, and the model's own arithmetic supports only the first. The correct sentence, on my runs, is that the historical pattern holds the 50 and 60 percent levels in every scenario and fails from 65 percent upward. As printed, the claim is false, and it is the paper's most quotable sentence about the present day pattern.

## Check 2. Frontier arithmetic. Verdict: pass

Scenario probabilities sum to one exactly at both levels. The inflow probabilities recomputed from the record are dry 0.227473, normal 0.445055, high 0.316484 and drought 0.010989, the last being one of 91 ten year windows. Weighted by the layer probabilities of 0.25, 0.50 and 0.25, the twelve combined scenario probabilities also sum to exactly one. The true mean budget recomputes at 10.9844 billion cubic metres.

Every frontier difference recomputes exactly from the margins printed in the results note. Against the unconstrained margin of 403,078,762,407 pounds: the cost at 50 percent is 105,339,563,475; at 60 percent, 140,558,355,676 against the unconstrained optimum and 35,218,792,201 against the next lower level; at 70 percent, 179,856,521,953 and 39,298,166,277; at 75 percent, 200,095,377,581 and 20,238,855,628; at 80 percent, 227,935,234,023 and 27,839,856,442. The wait and see value recomputes to the pound, 198,927,633,193, from the twelve scenario margins in the run log weighted by the exact probabilities. The baseline margin and the robustness floor reference of 9,803,526 tonnes also recompute exactly.

Two presentation notes. First, the raw run log prints two next lower level costs one pound away from the note, 35,218,792,200 where the note prints 35,218,792,201, and 27,839,856,443 where the note prints 27,839,856,442, because the log differences unrounded margins. The note's values are the ones consistent with the printed margins. Second, the printed reference components, wheat 4,617,795, rice 4,086,080 and summer maize 3,550,533 tonnes, sum to 12,254,408, one tonne above the printed total of 12,254,407; the unrounded components are 4,617,795.0, 4,086,079.6 and 3,550,532.6, which sum to 12,254,407.16, so the total is right and the components are rounded.

## Check 3. Monotonicity. Verdict: pass

The printed frontier is strictly decreasing in the floor: 403,078,762,407 at no floor, then 297,739,198,932, 262,520,406,731, 223,222,240,454, 202,983,384,826 and 175,143,528,384 pounds at 50, 60, 70, 75 and 80 percent. A higher hard floor never raises optimal profit in any printed or probed case.

My own probes land where monotonicity requires. The margin at a 65 percent floor, a level the build never printed, solves at 243,077,554,776 pounds, strictly between the 60 and 70 percent values. Maximum staple capacity falls as water falls: 9,833,867 tonnes at full budgets, my own solve of 9,441,472 tonnes at a 0.95 budget scale, then the printed 9,049,077 at 0.90 and 8,264,287 at 0.80. The unconstrained margin at a 0.95 budget scale solves at 389,350,014,945 pounds, strictly between the printed values at full budgets and at 0.90. More water never lowers the objective or the capacity in any test.

## Check 4. VSS and EVPI. Verdict: fail

The EVPI half passes in full. The true means are genuine probability weighted means: the budget mean of 10.9844 billion cubic metres recomputes from the four state budgets and their probabilities, and the mean prices and yields in the code are the layer values weighted 0.25, 0.50 and 0.25. The recourse value and the wait and see value stand on the same feasible set terms, the same 80 percent floor in every scenario, with the wait and see side free to choose planting per scenario. Their difference, 198,927,633,193 minus 175,143,528,384, is the printed 23,784,104,809 pounds, and I recomputed the wait and see side independently to the pound. The standard value of the stochastic solution is correctly withheld: the true mean plan cannot hold the floor in ten of the twelve scenarios, as the run log finding lists and the code mechanism confirms, so no standard VSS exists for this benchmark.

The penalised figure fails. The printed penalised value of the stochastic solution, 7,607,863,594 pounds, is the hard floor recourse value of 175,143,528,384 minus the penalised expected value of the true mean plan, 167,535,664,790. Both components are real and the subtraction is exact. But the stochastic side of a penalised comparison must be the optimum of the penalised problem, and that problem was never solved in the artifacts. I solved it with the model's own build function, floor 80 percent, penalty 25,000 pounds per tonne of shortfall. Its optimum is 216,434,391,884 pounds, which is 41,290,863,500 above the hard floor value, and it accepts shortfalls in all twelve scenarios, from 5,265,397 tonnes in the dry state at the low layer down to 867,259 tonnes in the high inflow state at the high layer. At this penalty the penalised criterion simply buys its way out of the guarantee. On a consistent definition the penalised value of the stochastic solution is 216,434,391,884 minus 167,535,664,790, which is 48,898,727,094 pounds, not the printed 7,607,863,594. The printed figure pairs two different criteria, the hard floor optimum on one side and a penalised evaluation on the other, and it travels with the word penalised attached. The number should either be recomputed on the consistent definition or renamed to say exactly what it is: the gap between the hard floor optimum and the penalised evaluation of the true mean plan.

## Check 5. Water values. Verdict: pass, with a material qualification

Provenance is confirmed in the code and in my own solves. The dual run builds the model with relax set to true, which makes every keep variable continuous; the printed values are the duals of the water constraints in that linear relaxation, divided by the combined scenario probability and by 1000 to undo the thousand cubic metre scaling inside the solver. At a floor of 0.8015 the relaxation objective solves at 174,061,181,459 pounds against the mixed integer objective of 173,434,491,032 at the same floor, so the relaxation sits above the true model by 626,690,426 pounds, 0.36 percent, as a relaxation must. These are relaxation duals. They may be called the marginal duals of the linear relaxation, conditional on the scenario, in pounds per cubic metre. They may not be called the marginal value of water in the mixed integer model, and they may not be called prices. No finite increment test on the mixed integer model itself exists anywhere in the artifacts, so no operational water value for the discrete model has been computed.

The degeneracy warning in the note is confirmed, and my experiment shows it is, if anything, understated. The build computes its dual floor as the ceiling share minus a tenth of a point, which is 0.8014759, printed as 80.15 percent. At that exact level my solve reproduces the run log: objective 174,088,727,469 against the logged 174,088,745,148, a difference of 17,679 pounds explained by the ceiling tonnage being printed rounded to the tonne, and Damietta in the normal inflow state at expected prices at exactly 19.17. At the rounded level of exactly 0.8015, the same solver returns Damietta at 16.68, the alternative value the note itself reports from a second optimal dual solution, and the quoted range minimum of 19.17 moves with it. So the printed ranges are exact only at the seventh decimal of a floor that is printed to two decimals. The note discloses the non uniqueness honestly; a reader who rebuilds at the printed floor will still land on different governorate values. A second solver, HiGHS, confirms the relaxation objective at the rounded level to within 605 pounds, but its interface returned no dual values, so the dual comparison rests on the CBC experiment.

## Check 6. Robustness. Verdict: pass

Every printed statistic recomputes from data/processed/robustness_samples_v4_1.csv. Minimum share 55.28 percent, tenth percentile 75.08, median 82.03, mean 80.15, maximum 82.67, maximum tonnage 10,130,719 tonnes. The fixed plan holds its 80 percent floor in 65.0 percent of the 100 samples. The worst decile mean shortfall recomputes at 1,103,685.6 tonnes against the printed 1,103,686, and the mean shortfall across all samples at 156,385 tonnes. The file is internally consistent: shortfalls equal the floor reference minus the sample tonnage, floored at zero, to within 0.13 tonnes of file rounding, and the shares equal the tonnages over the reference to within one millionth.

The reading caution is already printed in the note and my code reading confirms it: the sample mixes fifty single years with fifty ten year window means, and every sample is evaluated with late season planting frozen at the normal inflow variant of the fixed plan. The 65.0 percent is a share of that mixed sample under that frozen convention. It is not an annual reliability, and the note does not call it one.

## Check 7. Abandonment. Verdict: pass

I inspected the keep, water and production variables of the saved 80 percent floor plan, fixed and solved scenario by scenario. In the dry state at the low layer, of 64 governorate and crop cells, 45 are unplanted, 18 are kept and one planted cell is abandoned. In the drought state at the low layer, 44 are unplanted, 19 kept and one abandoned. In both scenarios the abandoned cell takes exactly zero water and produces exactly zero output. Every kept cell sits inside its water band, at least the minimum viable share of 0.30 of its full duty and at most the full duty, at solver tolerance. The production formula in the code, expected yield times one minus the response factor times kept area, plus the response term over the water applied, reproduces the solved production value of every cell exactly. No violation was found at tolerances scaled to the solver.

The cost side is charged once and weighted correctly. In the joint objective the planting cost is charged on planted area, not on kept area, so an abandoned crop keeps its sunk cost; winter planting is charged once at full weight because it is a single decision, and late season planting is charged weighted by the inflow probability of the state it is planted in. The evaluation function charges the full plan cost inside each scenario and weights by scenario probability, which sums to the same expectation. The identity is confirmed numerically: my evaluation of the historical pattern returned its printed expected margin of 86,006,146,997 pounds to the pound, which cannot happen under double charging or wrong weighting.

## Check 8. Solver integrity. Verdict: pass, with material qualifications

The original runs are clean. The solve function calls CBC with the relative gap set to zero and the primal and dual feasibility tolerance at 0.0000001. The frontier log shows Optimal at every level from 0 to 80 percent and Infeasible at 85 to 100 percent, and the infeasibility does not rest on status alone: the ceiling maximisation puts the maximum tonnage held in every scenario at 9,833,867 tonnes, 80.25 percent, so no plan can hold 85 percent. The saved plans respect every land pool limit at full precision; the largest excess in any saved plan is in the 75 percent plan and is below one billionth of a hectare.

The qualifications concern the saved plans at their floors. Replaying each saved plan by staple maximising recourse, the 50 percent plan sits 0.020 tonnes above its floor in each of the four low layer scenarios, and the 80 percent plan sits 0.028 tonnes below its floor in each of the four low layer scenarios. All four tie, because at the low layer the staple maximum of a fixed plan is set by its planting and its low yields, which confirms the corrected wording in the note's Section 3. Yet a hard floor re evaluation with CBC returns Infeasible in those four scenarios for both plans, including the 50 percent plan that is above its floor: at margins of hundredths of a tonne on floors of 6.1 and 9.8 million tonnes, the status is a tolerance artefact, the same phenomenon the build recorded in its own tolerance testing. Two consequences follow. The plans hold their floors to within three hundredths of a tonne, which is inside the tenth of a tonne band the note itself uses, so the substance stands. But the note's Section 14 sentence, that the saved 80 percent plan passes its own hard floor recourse in all twelve scenarios with its smallest headroom at plus 0.007 tonnes in the drought state at the low layer, does not reproduce: in my replay the size, the sign and the scenario attribution all differ, and any reader who re evaluates the saved files with the floor imposed will see Infeasible in four scenarios. The related claim that each saved plan's floored margin reproduces its printed margin to within 2,701 pounds is a property of the recovery script's own comparison, the CBC objective against the fixed binary linear program objective from HiGHS, not of the saved files evaluated directly; evaluated with no floor held, the saved 80 percent plan earns 178,163,274,014 pounds because its profit recourse delivers only 76.68 percent in its worst scenario. Finally, the pre recovery plan files described in Section 14, with six cells above their pool limits, were overwritten by the recovery and no longer exist, so that historical claim is unverifiable from the current artifacts.

## Comparison with the prior audits

I agree with the reaudit of version 4.1 on almost everything it tested: the frontier arithmetic, the wait and see value and the EVPI to the pound, the dual degeneracy including its Damietta value of 16.68, the clearance tie across the low layer, the robustness recompute and the baseline evaluation numbers. It found the two one pound next lower level costs in the version 4.1 note; version 4.2 corrected both, and my recompute confirms the corrected values are the right ones. Where the reaudit and I differ is in what we tested, not in any number. It graded the penalised figures as surviving because the penalised evaluation of the true mean plan and the subtraction both reproduce. It never solved the penalised stochastic problem, so it did not see that the printed difference pairs the hard floor optimum with a penalised evaluation. On the baseline, the reaudit and the paper audit both verified the pattern's numbers and neither tested the pattern against the ladder's low rungs, so the no guarantee sentence passed both of them untested. The paper audit describes the penalised figure as the exact difference of the recourse value and the penalised expected value, which is precisely the construction my Check 4 finds wanting.

The GPT audit of version 3 charged that the reported VSS used the wrong benchmark, a middle scenario budget of 11.0011 billion cubic metres instead of the true probability weighted mean. That charge was correct for version 3 and is repaired in this build: the true mean is 10.9844, my recompute confirms it, and the standard VSS is now correctly withheld rather than misreported. Its remaining findings concern the version 3 formulation and do not transfer to this build. On the two failures in this check, all three prior audits are silent, and on the penalised construction the two later audits repeat the build's own framing. That is the gap this independent check exists to close.
