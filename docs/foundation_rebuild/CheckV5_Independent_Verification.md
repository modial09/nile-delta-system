# Check V5: independent verification of the version 5 Nile Delta build

Written 15 November 2026 by the independent verifier for version 5. I did not build version 5 and I owe it nothing. My verdicts below were formed from the primary artifacts only: src/forecasting_v5.py, src/allocation_model_v5.py, data/external/faostat_producer_prices_egypt_dec2025.csv, the run logs run_v5 and run_forecasting_v5, the unrounded results_v5 JSON files, the saved plan files and robustness_samples_v5.csv. The build side documents (the S3 repair log, the S3 revised claim register, the S3 Gate A self assessment and paper/Results_Note_v5.md) were opened only after my verdicts were fixed; the comparison sits in its own section near the end.

Method, in brief. I reran the whole build in a scratch copy of the project: the forecasting module regenerated its forecast file, its scores file and its run log byte for byte identical to the saved ones, and the allocation main, robust and beet modes regenerated results JSON files exactly equal to the saved ones, with identical plan files and an identical robustness sample file. I recomputed every headline figure from the saved unrounded outputs with my own arithmetic. I replayed the floor 80 problem and the ceiling problem under HiGHS, a different solver from the CBC the build used. I checked the saved plans at full precision with my own code. My scripts sit in docs/foundation_rebuild/check_v5_work/ and no existing file was changed.

## Item 1. The frontier and its probabilities

Verdict: pass.

The unrounded frontier in results_v5_main.json reads: unconstrained 559,713,476,348.06; floor 50 percent 430,297,336,634.45; floor 60 percent 386,417,040,545.07; floor 70 percent 340,320,741,667.24; floor 75 percent 316,954,235,953.87; floor 80 percent 291,102,590,295.67; floors 85 to 100 percent null, that is infeasible. The margins fall strictly as the floor rises. The cost of the 80 percent guarantee against the unconstrained optimum of the same build is 559,713,476,348.06 minus 291,102,590,295.67, which is 268,610,886,052.39, matching the printed 268,610,886,052. The step costs also match the log: 129,416,139,714 from the unconstrained level to 50 percent, then 43,880,296,089, 46,096,298,878, 23,366,505,713 and 25,851,645,658. My full rerun regenerated every one of these values exactly, and HiGHS independently returned 291,102,587,089.51 for the floor 80 problem, a difference of 3,206 pounds on 291.1 billion. Probabilities: the inflow states carry 0.23, 0.45 and 0.32 from record counts of 23, 45 and 32 years in 100, summing to exactly 1. The price and yield layers carry 0.25, 0.50 and 0.25, summing to exactly 1. The nine combined scenario probabilities sum to exactly 1. One design fact a reader should know: price and yield share a single layer index, so the low price state always coincides with the low yield state inside a scenario. Independence is claimed only between the inflow layer and the combined price and yield layer, and the code matches that claim.

## Item 2. The ceiling and the drought object

Verdict: pass.

The ceiling share in the JSON is 0.8241405523868819. Multiplied by the reference tonnage of 12,254,401.23 tonnes, which I recomputed from the loader, it gives 10,099,349.00 tonnes, the printed ceiling, which is 82.41 percent. My own ceiling maximisation under HiGHS returned 10,099,349.32 tonnes, a third of a tonne from the saved value. The infeasibility of floors at 85 percent and above follows from this ceiling, not from solver status alone. The drought object enters no expected value: the scenario set built in the code has exactly nine members and none of them is the drought state; the drought budget is consumed only inside the stress section of the code, and in the results JSON it appears only under its own drought stress key, holding a profit margin, a staple tonnage of zero and a capacity, none of them probability weighted.

## Item 3. The historical pattern against the ladder

Verdict: pass.

From the unrounded pattern capacities in the JSON, the worst scenario is the dry state at the low layer, 8,056,850.5 tonnes, 65.75 percent of the reference tonnage. The floor tonnages are 6,127,200.6 at 50 percent, 7,352,640.7 at 60 percent, 7,965,360.8 at 65 percent and 8,578,080.9 at 70 percent. The worst capacity clears the first three and fails the fourth, so the highest ladder level the fixed pattern holds in every scenario is 65 percent, as printed, and its worst scenario tonnage is 8,056,850 tonnes. My rerun regenerated the pattern block exactly.

## Item 4. The penalised VSS, the withheld standard VSS and EVPI

Verdict: pass.

The penalised stochastic optimum is 346,643,462,500.25 and the penalised expected value of the true mean plan is 270,712,423,013.91, both under the same penalty of 25,000 pounds per tonne of shortfall and the same 80 percent floor in the code paths I read and reran. Their difference is 75,931,039,486.34, exactly the penalised VSS in the JSON. The standard VSS is genuinely withheld: the results JSON carries no standard VSS entry, and the log prints the reason, that the true mean plan cannot hold the floor, in 7 of the 9 scenarios by the register count. EVPI is computed on comparable sets: the wait and see value and the recourse value share the same floor, the same nine scenarios and the same probabilities. I recomputed the wait and see value from the nine printed scenario margins and probabilities and obtained 304,561,130.30 against the saved 304,561,130.37, a difference of seven pence caused by the printed margins being rounded to whole pounds. EVPI equals wait and see minus recourse exactly in the unrounded file: 13,458,470,834.70.

## Item 5. Band construction, weights and coverage

Verdict: pass on construction and on reporting, with a calibration caveat that binds at Gate A.

For all ten forecast series I reran the backtest and computed the residual quartiles with my own arithmetic. In every case the saved band equals the point forecast plus the 25th and 75th percentiles of the selected model backtest residuals at the used horizon, to the fourth decimal. The layer weights of 0.25, 0.50 and 0.25 therefore describe the same object the bands are built from. The coverage figures printed in the run log and the forecast file equal my sequential recomputation for all ten series: prices 0.0 percent for wheat, 11.1 for maize, 22.2 for potatoes, 20.0 for paddy rice and 20.0 for sugar beet; yields 21.1 for wheat, 15.8 for paddy rice, 57.9 for maize, 26.3 for sugar beet and 26.3 for potatoes. The caveat: a quartile band should cover about half of later outcomes in sequence, and the measured coverage for prices is 0.0 to 22.2 percent. The bands are built exactly as stated and reported honestly, but they are much thinner than the recent price behaviour they are meant to span, so the layer probabilities are nominal and every expected money figure inherits that thinness. A second property of the same construction: both residual quartiles for potatoes prices are positive (123.56 and 727.88), so the low price layer for all three potato crops, 10,876.83, sits above the expected layer, 10,753.27. The low layer is not low for potatoes. The arithmetic is right; the label misleads for that one series.

## Item 6. Robustness, split by sample kind

Single years: minimum share 53.75, 10th percentile 69.12, median 79.24, mean 76.52, maximum 80.59; the plan holds its floor in 46.0 percent of samples; worst decile mean shortfall 1,911,850 tonnes; mean shortfall 459,096 tonnes. Every figure matches the run log line for line. No mixed figure survives in the version 5 reporting: the log prints the two kinds as two distributions and no combined number. My rerun of the robust mode regenerated the sample file identically, so the sampling itself is reproducible under its stated seed.

## Item 7. Prices, target years and the rice failure

Verdict: pass.

The corrected extract holds the 2024 observed values exactly as required: wheat 17,380, maize 19,942, potatoes 10,446 pounds per tonne. Paddy rice and sugar beet end at 2023 in that file, with 2019 to 2021 missing, as the documents state. The forecasting module reads the corrected extract, and the allocation model consumes the version 5 forecast file built from it. Because drift was selected for every price series, I could recompute all five price forecasts from the raw series with my own drift arithmetic; all five match the saved values exactly, at horizon one from 2024 for wheat, maize and potatoes and horizon two from 2023 for paddy rice and sugar beet. The forecast target year is 2025 in every row of the forecast file, for prices and for yields alike. The rice failure is handled by a stated rule and not silently: the ridge model won the paddy rice price backtest, its full data refit at horizon two returned an infinite value, the module records that event as a failure note in the run log and passes selection to the next model in score order, which is drift. The rule is implemented in the code as a finite and positive forecast condition, and the event is on the printed record. Two presentation remarks: the failure note is printed in the log between the wheat block and the rice header, where a quick reader can attach it to the wrong crop, and the scores file still marks the rice ridge row as a normal scored row, which is true of its backtest and silent about its refit failure. Neither remark touches a number.

## Item 8. Duals at the exact floor

Verdict: pass.

The exact floor printed for the dual run is 0.8231405524 as a share of the reference tonnage. At full precision in the JSON it is 0.8231405523868819, which equals the ceiling share of 0.8241405523868819 minus 0.001 exactly, so the printed floor is the true floor of the run and not a rounded neighbour. My own solves at that floor reproduce the relaxation objective of 273,356,788,635.82 and the mixed integer objective of 272,969,293,876.63 to the pound, and sampled dual cells match the printed table (Beheira in the dry state at the low layer: raw 51.3494, conditional 893.03; Damietta in the normal state at the expected layer: raw 5.5007, conditional 24.45). The gap is 387,494,759.19 pounds, 0.142 percent of the integer objective, as reported. The labelling is correct in the log and in the note: the values are presented as one optimal dual solution of the LP relaxation at that floor, with the standing warnings that dual solutions are not unique, that they are not marginal values of the mixed integer model and that they are not prices.

## Item 9. Monotonicity and plan feasibility at full precision

Verdict: pass, with one caveat that must travel with the guarantee claim.

Monotonicity holds everywhere I tested it. The frontier falls strictly at every rung. A fresh solve at floor 65 percent, a level the printed ladder skips, returned 363,687,244,832.66, which sits between the floor 60 and floor 70 values as it must. The ceiling rises to 10,501,232 tonnes when budgets scale by 1.05 and falls to 9,697,467 tonnes at 0.95. The variant ladders behave the same way: capped margins sit below primary margins at every shared level, the capped ceiling of 79.50 percent sits below the primary ceiling, costlier cost brackets lower every margin, and the winter first reserve split lowers both the ceiling, to 80.38 percent, and the floor 80 margin, to 278,159,876,800. Plan feasibility at full precision: for each saved plan, the largest excess of a pool sum over its land limit is at most 0.0032 hectares, on pools of tens of thousands of hectares, and no area is negative. That is solver tolerance, honestly small. The caveat concerns the floor itself. When I imposed the floor as a hard constraint on the saved floor 80 plan, scenario by scenario, the solver returned Infeasible in the three low layer scenarios, the scenarios where the plan is tight. The clearance maximisation, which asks how much staple the fixed plan can hold in each scenario, returns 9,803,521.000 tonnes against a floor of 9,803,520.985 in those scenarios: the plan can hold its floor, with 0.015 tonnes to spare. Easing the floor by a single tonne makes all nine scenario solves return Optimal. My clearance values reproduce the build printed clearance table scenario for scenario. So the guarantee of the saved plans is real and is established by maximisation, exactly as the build states, and it is also exactly as thin as the build standing sentence admits: tight within solver tolerance, not exact. One related property the note does not state: the floor lives in the plan together with its recourse. Replaying the saved floor 80 planting with margin seeking recourse and no floor imposed delivers a minimum scenario tonnage of 8,926,542 tonnes, below the floor, at a higher expected margin. Anyone handed only the plan files must also impose the floor holding recourse to reproduce the guarantee.

## Further observations

Four small documentation slips, none of which moves a number. The robustness log says the fixed plan carries four late season planting variants; version 5 has three inflow states, so there are three. The forecasting log header describes selection as lowest one step backtest RMSE, while paddy rice and sugar beet prices are selected at horizon two. One code docstring still speaks of twelve scenarios where the build has nine. The sugar beet factory price variant is arithmetically correct and empty as a sensitivity: my own solves show beet planted at exactly zero hectares at both the farm gate forecast and at 2,400 pounds per tonne, at floors 0 and 80 percent, which is why its frontier equals the primary frontier to the pound.

## Gate A opinion

Version 5 may not yet be called validated empirical results. It may now be called something narrower and stronger than the build self assessment allowed: a numerically verified exploratory model study on a stated basis. Every headline number I was asked to test reproduces from the saved code and inputs, most of them exactly, the rest within solver tolerance, and the key values survive a change of solver. The numerical half of Gate A, as the handoff defines it, is met by this pass.

What still blocks the empirical label is not arithmetic. First, the scenario layers are miscalibrated against their own backtest: sequential coverage of the price bands runs from 0.0 to 22.2 percent against a construction value of 50 percent, so the probabilities on the layers are nominal and every expected money figure is thinner in its uncertainty than its weights claim. The hard floor, ceiling and feasibility results do not depend on those weights and stand. Second, every water budget scales from a calibration of 11.00 billion cubic metres that no source publishes, and the ladder has never been repriced at scaled anchors; my spot checks priced only the ceiling at 0.95 and 1.05. Third, the money basis mixes 2025 forecast prices with official 2022 costs, bridged only by assumed escalation factors of 1.50 and 2.00. Fourth, the optima plant potatoes at up to several hundred times their observed areas at fixed prices, so the frontier prices the guarantee inside a relaxed world, as the results note itself says; the capped ladder is the companion a decision reader needs beside it. Fifth, recourse decisions in the model see the full scenario, including the yield layer, an information advantage over any real planting season whose size is unmeasured. Sixth, no Egyptian irrigation or agricultural specialist has reviewed any version of this work, and no computation of mine can substitute for that review.

To close the gate, in order: take the band calibration decision in the open, either rebuilding the layers on a stated recent window with its coverage printed, or printing the coverage shortfall as a standing limitation beside every expected figure; reprice the ladder at scaled water anchors; and obtain a human domain review. Until then, every version 5 figure should travel with the label the results note already gives it: a model result on a stated basis, with its conditions printed beside it.
