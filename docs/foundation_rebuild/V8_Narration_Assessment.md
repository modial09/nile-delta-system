# Version 8 Narration Assessment

Written 18 November 2026. The Anusha academic paper was read cold, as a reader who knows nothing about Egypt. The other three documents were read with the same lens, faster. Findings first, then the changes made, then the font record.

## The academic paper, read cold

**What works.** The problem lands inside the first two pages. The introduction opens on a real event, the October announcement, and within four paragraphs a reader who has never heard of an ardeb holds the whole stake in two numbers: growing one feddan costs about 50,000 pounds, and delivering its crop in full at the announced price returns about 47,700. Section 2 then teaches the three facts the paper needs, announcement, farmer choice, gap at the port, each in its own bold lead paragraph, each with dated evidence. The August 2024 tender that sought 3.8 million tonnes and bought 280,000 does more work than any general sentence about import risk could. Every method arrives with its why attached to a section heading or a bold lead: why a stochastic programme, why shadow prices, why the record and why a ratio, why a tonne is not a cost. The reasoning chain holds paragraph to paragraph; nothing in Sections 4 to 7 could be lifted into a paper about another country, because every paragraph carries an Egypt specific number, decree, or season. The honesty passages are placed where a skeptic would look for them: the validation failure is reported before the grid it conditions, and the sensitivity ranking is printed in full even though it puts an unsourced assumption first.

**Weak passage 1, executive summary.** The summary says the delivery response was fitted to eight seasons of public record, and two sentences later gives the fit quality on six fit seasons. A cold reader trips on the mismatch and has to stop and reconcile it. The record holds eight seasons; the fit uses six. The sentence was rewritten to say exactly that.

**Weak passage 2, introduction.** The sentence that names the product calls it the priced table and the honesty apparatus around it. Apparatus is abstract and self referential; it sounds like a paper admiring its own machinery, and it is the one phrase in the introduction that could sit in any paper about any model. It was rewritten in plain words: the priced table, and the record of checks that lets a reader trust it.

**Weak passage 3, Section 4, why shadow prices.** The paragraph promises that in Section 7 the dual values price the rice feddan and the beet tonne in the governorates where those crops compete with wheat. Section 7 does no such thing; it runs the Monte Carlo, the berseem break even on margins, the sensitivity ranking and the Pakistan mirror. A promise made in Section 4 and not kept in Section 7 is the closest thing in the paper to an overclaim, and a careful reviewer would find it. The sentence was rewritten to claim only what the paper delivers: the ladder is read off the shadow cost of the floor, rung by rung.

**Repetition, judged acceptable with one note.** The step cost arithmetic, the raise is paid on every tonne, appears in the executive summary, in Section 6, in the appendix identity and in the conclusion. Three of the four earn their place: the summary needs the headline, Section 6 is the canonical working, and the appendix is a formulation record. The conclusion restates it in one clause as part of the headline finding, which is normal conclusion practice. It was left as written, and it is recorded here so the choice is visible rather than accidental.

**Citations.** In text citations are present throughout and consistent in form, author and year, with the source named at first use. The reference list carries several news items under bracketed descriptive titles because the originals carry no formal title; that is honest and stays. One inherited gap: the El Agha, Molden and Ghanem entry names the journal and volume but no article title, because the project source record never held one. It was not invented here. The build now sets the reference list with hanging indents, which the earlier build did not.

## The walkthrough

The story is the strongest of the four: one number each autumn, a man with a harvest, two buyers, a ship later. One passage was compressed past clarity. The sentence that says the other road ends in a wall, and the berseem test below shows the wall, asks a plain English reader to decode a metaphor before he knows what the other road is. It was rewritten to name the road: working the answer out from crop margins, and to say plainly that it fails. Nothing else in the walkthrough needed the knife; the weak rice pass and the holding period warning sit in the open, and the close is honest about what the work does not prove.

## The ministry brief

One cryptic cell. The basis block said financing at 0.25, on treasury bill yields, a number with no unit that a busy reader could read three ways. It now reads financing at 25 percent a year, on treasury bill yields. The finding paragraph, the readings and the assumptions block otherwise do their job at brief length, and the curve framing is stated the way the record supports, as a question about which curve, not as a flat failure.

## The Larson paper

Read fastest and changed least. The formulation leads, the estimation section reports its own out of sample failure before the results, and the verification section hands the reader a record with a failure in it rather than a curated count. The step cost identity appears in Section 2 and again in the results; for this reader that recurrence is a feature, because the identity is the result he will test first. No prose changes were made. Its only change is typographic.

## Font record

Times New Roman is not installed on this machine and no package candidate exists for the Microsoft core fonts installer, so it cannot be installed here. The font stack in the build now declares Times New Roman first, then Liberation Serif, then serif. Liberation Serif is installed, and the system font matcher resolves the name Times New Roman to it. Liberation Serif is the metric clone of Times New Roman: same advance widths, same line breaks, same pagination. On this machine the PDFs render in Liberation Serif; on any machine that holds real Times New Roman, including the reader machines these papers are sent to, the same files render in Times New Roman with identical layout. Body sizes after this pass: academic paper and Larson paper at 12 point, walkthrough at 11 point to hold its three pages, ministry brief at 11 point inside its two pages.

## Build outcome, recorded after the rebuild

All four PDFs were rebuilt with the updated build script, rendered to page images, and read page by page. Final page counts: walkthrough exactly 3 pages; academic paper 20 pages, with page 1 still the standalone executive summary and nothing else on it; ministry brief 2 pages; Larson paper 7 pages. The Anusha paper carries the APA 7 dress in full: page number top right on every page, one inch margins, 12 point body, hanging indents in the reference list, and its three fixes visible in place. The brief shows the financing line in plain words. The appendix of the academic paper keeps its plain text formula notation, with every symbol also written out in words in the symbol table beside it; the Larson paper uses the same plain notation for its formulas, so the style is consistent across the set, and it was left as found rather than rebuilt at risk to the claim discipline around it.
